The **Sturdy Industry** program was an industrial policy created by the government of [[Boris Serec]] and continued by his successor [[Japlen Razol]] aimed at supporting specific "national champion" companies in each sector.  The bulk of the program was designed by [[Laus Karnej]], but it was heavily influenced by organized interests, such as those in the [[Confederation of the Industries of the Confian Nation]]. Karnej later called it "an abomination that was the crown jewel of Boris [Serec]'s 'deformed developmentalism'"
It involved the heavy and direct subsidization of [[Ragged-sleeve|ragged-sleeve]] conglomerates, reaching a peak of Н¢147 billion, or 0.73% of GDP by 1990, as well as high industrial tariffs of up to 30% on foreign competitors. The program aided in providing funds for the reconstruction of Confia post-[[Continental War]], but was heavily criticized for constraining the Confian budget, tightened by the [[Senjap Kunsekowić|Kunsekowić]] fiscal rules, leading to cuts to social programs under Razol and for further entrenching the ragged-sleeves as a powerful economic force.
During the second government of [[Katerina Armoljubca Nožeslawna|KAN]], the program underwent large reforms, having its additional tariffs capped at 10% and maximum yearly grant for a given company at Н¢2 billion. The companies receiving it were also changed, namely [[PIKW]] to [[Făzmelj]] for banking and [[Žošewoš Machinery]] to [[Ljetron]] in automobile production, breaking the cycle of ragged-sleeve capture. The [[Noljež Teššan|Teššan]] government continued this policy, eliminating the additional tariff and setting the grant limit at H¢ 500 million. The funds cut from the program were directed into WESPROWIK and into retraining programs, aiming to shuffle the workforce of dying industries into modernizing ones.
The program was terminated entirely on the first day of the [[Grawolja Lasmanna|Lasmanna]] government.
