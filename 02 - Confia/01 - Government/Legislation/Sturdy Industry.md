The **Sturdy Industry** program was an industrial policy created by the government of [[Boris Serec]] and continued by his successor [[Japlen Razol]] aimed at supporting specific "national champion" companies in each sector. It involved the heavy and direct subsidization of [[Ragged-sleeve|ragged-sleeve]] conglomerates, reaching a peak of Н¢147 billion by 1990, as well as high industrial tariffs of up to 30% on foreign competitors. The program aided in providing funds for the reconstruction of Confia post-[[Continental War]], but was heavily criticized for constraining the Confian budget, tightened by [[Senjap Kunsekowić|Kunsekowić]]'s spending caps, leading to austerity under Razol and for further entrenching the ragged-sleeves as a powerful economic force.
During the second government of [[Katerina Armoljubca Nožeslawna|KAN]], the program underwent large reforms, having its tariffs capped at 20% and maximum yearly grant for a given company at Н¢2 billion. The companies receiving it were also changed, namely [[PIKW]] to [[Făzmelj]] for banking and [[Žošewoš Machinery]] to [[Ljetron]] in automobile production.
