
The **Duolij lobster** (*Speriosves Najkroh*) is a species of slipper lobster native to the coastal shelves of the [[Sea of Galil]]. It's named after the city of [[Duolij]], [[Postia]], and has since the [[Dripstanian Empire]] been regarded as a status symbol of the elite.

An adult Duolij lobster typically reaches 18 to 25 cm in length and weighs 300 to 600g. Like other members of the genus, it lacks claws, with a broad, flattened carapace and paddle-like antennae. It is distinguished from related species by its steel-blue to cobalt-grey coloration, caused by crustacyanin proteins binding astaxanthin pigments in the shell. The flesh is white, fine-textured, and notably sweet with low brininess.

The *S. Najkroh* inhabits sandy and fine-sediment substrates at depths of 30 to 80m along the mid-offshore shelf of the Galil Sea, in the clear, warm oligotrophic waters beyond the coastal upwelling zone. It is nocturnal and solitary. Spends daylight hours buried shallowly in sediment with only the antennae exposed. Feeds on small bivalves, polychaete worms, and organic detritus. Slow-growing, reaching market size in approximately 5 years.

The Duolij lobster is considered a prestige seafood throughout [[Susia]]. It has a cultural significance as a status symbol and showcase of power, with references to it in sayings like "[[Lobster table]]". 
Its market price reflects the depth at which it must be harvested, its relatively low meat yield (approximately 25-30% of body weight), and the care required in handling to preserve the shell's color.
