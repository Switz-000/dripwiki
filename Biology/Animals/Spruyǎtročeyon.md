The **Spruyǎtročeyon** (*Bromačij Spruyǎtroch*) is the largest wild bovine on the [[Dripstanian Continent]] and the larger of the two bison species native to [[Susia]]. Its name in [[Susian language|Susian]] translates approximately as "absolute sovereign of the plains". Found along the margins of the [[Pounnun Desert]] and in the parkland of the eastern interior, it has been the prestige game of the [[Cericeiro|Cericeiros]] since the [[Dripstanian Empire|imperial period]].

Mature bulls weigh 1,600 to 2,000 kg and stand approximately 2.4 to 2.5 m at the shoulder, with horn spreads of 1.8 to 2.4 m. Cows weigh 900 to 1,150 kg. The species is distinguished from the [[Susian wild bison]] by its larger body, more pronounced shoulder hump, and significantly longer horns, which curve outward rather than upward. The coat is dark brown across the body and lighter on the forequarters, with longer guard hair than its smaller relative.

The *B. Spruyǎtroch* inhabits the transitional country between open plain and forest or scrub, preferring habitats with cover within reach. It lives in defensive groups of 5 to 15 individuals, typically a bull or two with cows and calves, which back into available cover and orient outward when threatened. The defensive structure of these groups is the principal source of the difficulty associated with hunting the animal. The species browses as well as grazes, feeding on tall grasses, broadleaf shrubs, and seasonal browse. Cows do not calve until age four or five, and herd growth is slow.

The species' current range includes the eastern margins of the [[Pounnun Desert]] in [[Troli]], the country of the [[Sužielaj]] and [[Misocévia]] border uplands, the northern forest-plains transition in upper Misocévia, and portions of [[Troli]] territory extending into [[Sutsa-Fuhu]]. The historical range was considerably wider, extending across northern [[Vitrika]], the southern [[Dripia]] plains margin, and the Misocévia and [[Confia]] forest belt, before agricultural clearance and frontier hunting reduced it to its current marginal-country distribution.

Hunting the Spruyǎtročeyon has carried significant cultural weight in the eastern states since the imperial period. In traditional usage, the title of [[Cericeiro]] in its full sense was reserved for hunters who had taken one, and contemporary working ranchers of the [[Susian wild bison]] are not generally considered Cericeiros in this older sense regardless of professional standing. In the contemporary era, hunts are conducted under federal permit and seasonal restriction, and the tension between traditional hunting communities and the modern permit system remains a live cultural issue in [[Troli]] and the eastern states. The species' standing in Susian cultural memory is reflected in the term "Behemoth Tamer," originally a Cericeiro hunting honorific in the eastern states and later borrowed by federal regulators following the [[AFCC]]'s prosecution of [[Troli Ustaras]] executive leadership.

The Spruyǎtročeyon has no commercial ranching presence. Its size, defensive behavior, slow reproduction, and habitat requirements have prevented adaptation to the infrastructure used for the [[Susian wild bison]] and the [[hybrid bison]]. Meat taken in licensed hunts is not generally commercialized, and is distributed within the hunter's community or served at communal feasts including [[Hăjaven]].
