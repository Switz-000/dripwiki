The **Buta genocide** was the ethnic cleansing of the inland [[Buta people|Buta]] population of [[Yar-Firol]] by the Yar-Firolian state. It began by 1963, under [[Bejňar Keke]], and peaked between around 1965 and 1975. Between tens of thousands and the low hundreds of thousands were killed, and over one million were displaced into [[Bershad]]. The state framed the campaign as security policy, population transfer, agricultural modernization and counterinsurgency.

## Background

Before the genocide the Buta made up about 48% of Yar-Firol's population and inhabited the interior. The anti-Buta nationalism of the regime drew on a crisis in the founding years of 1959 to 1961.

## Campaign

### State policy

The campaign was bureaucratically rationalized. It ran concurrently with the [[Nguan regime]] across the border and was presented officially as security policy, population transfer, agricultural modernization and counterinsurgency.

### Settler violence

Settlers armed themselves and put bounties on the Buta, adding private ethnic violence to state policy.

### Exemptions

Coastal Buta professionals who had assimilated were not targeted. They were retained in state service, and their presence was offered as evidence that the regime's policy was not ethnic in character.

## Displacement

Over one million Buta fled into Bershad. In the northwest of Bershad, refugees were subjected to predation by [[San'motko]] between around 1972 and 1976.

## Casualties

Between tens of thousands and the low hundreds of thousands of Buta were killed. The Buta share of Yar-Firol's population fell from about 48% to about 30%.

## Aftermath

### International response

International press coverage was muted. The Susian establishment did not embarrass its ally.

### Historiography

Historians of the second wave of the [[Bershadi diaspora]] opened the historiography of the genocide from the 2010s onward.

### Survivors

> *Main article: [[Yar-Firolian Buta survivor caucus]]*

The surviving coastal Buta professionals rose in the Yar-Firolian civil service, foreign ministry, intelligence services and academia. Buta on both sides of the border lost relatives through the 1970s.
