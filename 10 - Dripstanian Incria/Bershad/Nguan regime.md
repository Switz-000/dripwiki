The **Nguan regime** was the government of [[Boe Nguan]] in [[Bershad]] from 1962 until the collapse of the Bershadi state in 1973. It ruled on a native-nationalist program to "decolonize" Bershad from Dripstanian influence, under [[Parund people|Parund]] leadership and with [[Ashgerad|Ashgeradi]] sponsorship. The program ran from 1962 to 1967; the measures taken from 1968 onward were improvised responses to its failure. By 1973 the state had collapsed, [[San'motko]] had won the regime's internal ideological contest, and Nguan remained head of state as a figurehead.

## Background

Nguan came to power in 1962 on a native-nationalist platform. Ashgerad sponsored the regime from the start. Its material support was modest; its ideological framework was formative.

## Coalition and hierarchy

The regime was a coalition before it was an ideological project. The Parund made up 11% of the population in the 1950 census and could not govern alone. The program announced a Parund hierarchy over all other peoples while Nguan depended on most of them to govern. The compromises between the two defined the regime.

| Predator               | Parund (dominant), [[Mtole people\|Mtole]] (junior)                                                       |
| Tolerated-instrumental | [[Daiko people\|Daiko]], [[Xaya people\|Xaya]], [[Kadus people\|Kadus]]                                  |

Yokno who did not accept elevation were progressively treated as prey. The Parund and Mtole were rivals rather than partners within the predator tier.

## Program (1962 to 1967)

### Reform (1962 to 1965)

The regime redistributed land to the Parund, Mtole and Daiko, in declining order of priority, introduced commercial protectionism and nationalized foreign property. The reforms failed. The colonial economy was the only economy in the country, and redistribution followed Parund precedence rather than extending to indigenous peoples in general.

### Persecution (1966 to 1967)

In 1966 Nguan had his main political opponent and other Haiel arrested on charges of treason for cooperating with [[Susia]] and [[Yar-Firol]]. Persecution of Haiel, Dripstanians and Armotists began as administrative removals and expropriations. Military service was introduced for ages 18 to 35.

In 1967 the Minister of Economy and the Minister of Foreign Relations were arrested and dismembered, and their bodies were displayed in government buildings in the capital. A further 106 government agents were charged with treason. The use of the [[Susian language]] and of creole languages was banned, with prison sentences of 30 years. The import of televisions and magazines was banned.

### Absolute rule (1968 to 1972)

In 1968 a plebiscite revoked the 1962 constitution. It was replaced by a decree granting Nguan absolute power for life and banning every political organization except his party. The decree was framed as a cosmological consecration of the ruler rather than a self-coronation. In the same year Nguan killed the president of the central bank, and the regime purged the country's economists and accountants. No reliable economic or demographic data on Bershad exists after 1968.

From 1968 to 1972 temples were seized and converted into public consumption sites, and the Haiel and Bokta were subjected to mass victimization. In 1969 Nguan purged the entire cabinet and left the capital permanently for a presidential villa in the jungle. In 1971 the Parund language was sacralized as the language of the cosmos and of the sovereign.

## Crisis measures (1968 to 1973)

From 1968 the regime's measures were reactive improvisations to the failure of the program rather than policy.

- 1968: fishing and boats banned on the Haiel coast.
- 1968: borders closed; the military ordered to shoot anyone approaching them.
- 1968: some schools, hospitals and temples demolished. The rest remained standing without functioning. Traditional medicine was promoted in their place, while advisors and friends of the government travelled abroad for surgery.
- 1969: landmines laid on every road leading out of the country.
- 1969: military service extended to begin at age eight.
- 1969: the currency collapsed, and barter and subsistence farming followed.

A mass exodus followed. Tens of thousands died, shot by the military or while crossing the jungle.

### Cholera epidemic

A cholera epidemic broke out in 1970. Vaccines donated by international organizations were hoarded in Nguan's villa and distributed only to close supporters and his family. Hundreds of thousands died of preventable disease in the following years.

## Relationship with San'motko

> *Main article: [[San'motko]]*

The regime maintained a public fiction of separation from San'motko: "we do not direct this, this is what the population does when given freedom." San'motko served the regime as an instrument of terror and deniability. The relationship inverted over the regime's final years, and by 1973 San'motko held the regime's program.

## Collapse

The Bershadi state collapsed in 1973. San'motko ruled some areas and warlords divided the rest. Nguan remained head of state as a figurehead, held hostage in his villa, until his death in 1981. The regime left no successor and no plan for transition.

> *Main article: [[Bershadi Civil War]]*

## Deaths

Deaths under the regime from starvation, cholera, landmines, the exodus and executions are estimated at two to four million.
