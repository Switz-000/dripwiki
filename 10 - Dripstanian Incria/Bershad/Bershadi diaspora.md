The **Bershadi diaspora** comprises the communities of [[Bershad|Bershadi]] origin abroad. It formed in two waves: the first fled the [[Nguan regime]], the second fled the [[Bershadi Civil War]]. The waves differ in composition and in their reading of Bershadi history.

## Origins

### Wave 1

Wave 1 fled the Nguan regime. It comprised the coastal [[Haiel people|Haiel]] class, the [[Bokta people|Bokta]], Dripstanians, Armotist clergy, and an educated cohort of [[Yokno people|Yokno]] from the tier of partial collaborators, who left the country.

[[Yar-Firol]] absorbed the Haiel coastal class and the Armotist clergy. Wave 1 has been largely silent on Yar-Firolian responsibility for the [[Buta genocide]], out of gratitude for shelter from a worse regime.

### Wave 2

Wave 2 fled the civil war of the 1990s, fleeing the war itself rather than a particular regime. It includes people who had lived under [[San'motko]]'s state in the northwest. Wave 2 carried [[Buta people|Buta]] into [[Susia]].

## Historiography

The division between the waves is generational and ideological. Wave 1 historiography reads San'motko as an instrument of the Nguan regime. The reading holds for 1966; by the collapse of 1973 San'motko had captured the regime.

Wave 2 historians opened the historiography of the Buta genocide from the 2010s onward.

## Contemporary Relevance

> *Main article: [[Bershad–Susia relations]]*

A Wave 1 figure publishing in Susian turned Susian public opinion toward intervention in Bershad around 1980 to 1982.
