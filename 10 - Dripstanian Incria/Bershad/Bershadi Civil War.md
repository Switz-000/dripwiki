The **Bershadi Civil War** was fought between the consolidated zones of [[Bershad]] from around 1990 until the partition of the country froze. It was interrupted by the [[Bershadi Conference]] in the late 1990s and resumed around 2000, when the first national election was rejected by the losing coalitions. The war was funded by the sorophine economy. Deaths from the collapse of the state in 1973 to the partition are estimated at three to five million.

## Background

After the Bershadi state collapsed in 1973, [[San'motko]] ruled parts of the northwest and warlords divided the rest of the country. Until around 1990 there was no civil war: the zones held in equilibrium, with no national authority and no international actor proposing one.

## Course

### Conflict between zones (from around 1990)

Open conflict began around 1990 as the consolidated zones fought over their boundaries.

### Conference and election

> *Main article: [[Bershadi Conference]]*

[[Susia]] convened a peace conference between around 1995 and 1998. A transitional administration followed, and the first national election was held around 1999 to 2000. A coalition of the [[Haiel people|Haiel]] and the coast won narrowly. Within weeks the northern coalition of [[Yokno people|Yokno]] revanchists and the Western Coalition led by San'motko rejected the result, and [[Buta people|Buta]] militias mobilized defensively.

### Resumption (from around 2000)

Open war resumed around 2000, fought over a state that existed on paper.

### Susian intervention

> *Main article: [[Bershad–Susia relations]]*

From around 2000 Susia led a nation-building intervention in Bershad in collaboration with its [[ODDN]] allies in Incria: [[Veskera]], [[Yar-Firol]] and [[Sekyo]].

### Partition

The partition of Bershad froze. Since then the country has had two governments, neither of them sovereign.

## Belligerents

| Side                    | Composition                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Western Coalition       | San'motko-led: [[Parund people\|Parund]], [[Daiko people\|Daiko]], [[Mtole people\|Mtole]], [[Kadus people\|Kadus]] |
| Eastern factions        | Yokno revanchists, Buta loyalists, southern Haiel, [[Sanye people\|Sanye]] in the northeast           |

After the Conference the Yokno revanchists and the Western Coalition formed the northern government; the Haiel formed the southern government.

## Conduct

### Western Coalition

Western Coalition forces displayed skulls as trophies on their vehicles, mutilated captives, conducted terror operations against villages and left mass graves.

### Eastern factions

Eastern factions carried out ethnic cleansing and sexual violence on an industrial scale. Their mass graves were discovered after the fact.

### Child soldiers

Militias recruited child soldiers and dosed them with sorophine as an operational method, particularly in the Western Coalition.

### Press

Eastern factions issued communiqués, held press conferences and employed spokespeople. Western factions did not.

> *See also: [[Cannibalism in Bershad#Contemporary Relevance]]*

## Financing

> *Main article: [[Sorophine]]*

The war was funded by the sorophine economy, through Yar-Firolian commodity boards and Veskeran refining.

## Casualties

Deaths from the collapse of 1973 to the partition are estimated at three to five million.

## Aftermath

By the 2020s millions of internally displaced people lived in semi-permanent camps. The partition has remained frozen since.
