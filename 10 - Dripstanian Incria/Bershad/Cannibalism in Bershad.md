**Cannibalism in Bershad** refers to institutionalized cannibalism among five of the peoples of [[Bershad]]: the [[Parund people|Parund]], [[Mtole people|Mtole]], [[Daiko people|Daiko]], [[Xaya people|Xaya]] and [[Baya people|Baya]]. The five practices differ in who is eaten, for what purpose and in what form. The Baya practice ended with their conversion to [[Armotism]]. The Parund, Mtole and Daiko practices continue in the [[Western Coalition Zone]].

The term covers cannibalism accepted by the culture in which it occurs. It excludes acts committed under starvation for immediate survival and acts by isolated individuals regarded as criminal by their own society.

## Origins

The practices predate Dripstanian contact. Parund predation in particular is a pre-Dripstanian ideology of Parund supremacy.

According to a Dripstanian colonial account, the Parund, who live along the coast, and the Mtole, whose territory runs from a coastal section deep into the interior, were better fed than the peoples of the interior and often physically larger and stronger. The account states that they "raided inland 'bush' people with impunity and little care", primarily the [[Kadus people|Kadus]], Daiko and [[Yokno people|Yokno]], all of them inland peoples, and that they hunted people for food even when game was plentiful.

## Terminology

- **Endocannibalism**: consumption of a member of one's own community, often as part of a funerary ceremony.
- **Exocannibalism**: consumption of a person from outside the community, frequently as an act of aggression or in warfare.
- **Human predation**: hunting people from unrelated and possibly hostile groups in order to eat them.
- **Gastronomic cannibalism**: eating human flesh for its flavor, or in the same manner as the flesh of any other animal.
- **Ritualistic cannibalism**: consumption performed as part of a ceremony.

Cases of people eating human livers and hearts, especially those of enemies, have been reported across the region.

### Parund

The Parund practice ritualized exocannibalism with human predation. Only people from outside the community are eaten, and the act expresses Parund superiority over them.

### Mtole

The Mtole practice is gastronomic and includes both endocannibalism and exocannibalism. It draws no distinction between human and animal flesh.

### Daiko

Daiko cannibalism functions as medical care. Daiko have cut and boiled their own flesh to treat sick relatives. Children were sometimes killed, because eating their boiled hearts was considered a way to extend a person's life. The legend of [[Mt'yorsur]] concerns a Daiko chief said to have ordered officials to "harvest the hearts and livers of healthy children, younger than 13" to extend his life span. Accounts say he lived to the age of 170, bathing in the blood of the young and eating hearts every two weeks.

The Daiko also ate human flesh because animal meat was scarce. A former Daiko cannibal stated that it would have been wasteful to let the flesh of killed enemies spoil.

### Xaya

The Xaya practice funerary endocannibalism. It is ceremonial and rare. In Xaya practice, the dead remain part of the community through being eaten.

### Baya

The Baya practiced seasonal ceremonial endocannibalism. Armotist missions ended the practice by substituting an Armotist rite for it.

## Parund and Mtole rivalry

The Parund and Mtole both practice human predation but disagree on what it means. The Parund eat outsiders as a ritual of superiority; the Mtole eat people from within and outside the community as ordinary food. Each regards the other's practice as mistaken. Mtole resentment of Parund pretension has persisted through the [[Nguan regime]] and after it.

## Contemporary Relevance

Under the [[Nguan regime]], temples were seized and converted into public consumption sites between 1968 and 1972.

During the [[Bershadi Civil War]], after massacres of [[Buta people|Buta]] by Parund perpetrators, a [[Susia|Susian]] reporter interviewed a young participant in a "human cookout". He said that "it tastes just like chicken, specially the heart and liver", and that his favorite parts were women's breasts and the soles of the feet.

> *Main article: [[Western Coalition Zone]]*

In the [[Western Coalition Zone]], the Parund, Mtole and Daiko each continue their practice, and public ritual practice forms part of state celebration.
