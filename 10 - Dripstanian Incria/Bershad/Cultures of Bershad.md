## Canibalism in Bershad

This is a summarized list of cannibalistic practices in the cultures that compose [[Bershad]]. This list refers to *institutionalized cannibalism* that is, cannibal acts that are accepted by the culture in which they occur. These do not include acts committed under starvation to ensure one's immediate survival or those committed by isolated individuals considered criminal and often pathological by society at large.

### Terminology

Endocannibalism: Consumption of a person from the same community. Often as a part of a funerary ceremony.

Exocannibalism: Consumption of a person from outside the community. Frequently an act of agression, or in the context of warfare

Human Predation: Hunting of people from unrelated and possibly hostile groups in order to eat them

Gastronomic: Human flesh eaten for its flavor or in the same manner as the flesh or any other animal.

Ritualistic: Eaten with a cerimony or not

As the Parund and Mtole peoples were concentrated in the coast, they are historically better fed and hence often physically larger and stronger than tose living inland, they "raided inland 'bush' people with impunity and little care" as one dripstanian account goes. Primarly the Kadus, Daiko and Yokno peoples. The Parund and Mtole hunted humans for food even when game was plentiful.

The Daiko people practiced cannibalism primary due to lack of animal meat, an account of a former Daiko cannibal argues that it would have been wasteful to let the flesh of killed enemies spoil.

Cases of people eating human livers and hearts, specially of enemies have been reported across the region. The Daiko people have practiced medical cannibalism over centuries, with people cutting their own body parts and boiling them to cure ailing relatives. Children were sometimes killed because in Daiko culture, eating their boiled hearts was considered a good way of extending a persons' life. The legend of [[Mt'yorsur]], a Daiko chief that supposedly orded officials to "harvest the hearts and livers of healthy children, younger than 13" in order to extend his life span. Accounts say that he lived up to 170 years old, with the habit of bathing himself in young blood and eating hearts every two weeks.

After the massacres of the Buta minority people during the Bershadian civil war, by the Parund perpetrators. a [[Susia|susian]] reporter interviewed a young cannibal, who had just participated in a "human cookout". The young cannibal said, without any hesitation that "it tastes just like chicken, specially the heart and liver" he also claimed that his favorite part were women's breasts and the sole of their feet.
