BERSHAD

→ Boe Nguan (person article)
  - Section header date corrected: rule runs 1962 to ~1980, not 1962–1973.
  - 1962–65 is a reformer phase: native-nationalist platform, real attempts
    at decolonization (land redistribution to Parund-Mtole-Daiko in declining
    priority, commercial protectionism, foreign-property nationalization).
  - Reforms fail because the colonial economy is the only economy, AND because
    the redistribution was hierarchically Parund-first rather than universally
    indigenous.
  - 1965 (slightly later than current 1963): secret Neoveli trip, psychiatric
    care, sorophine dependency begins.
  - Late stage (~1971): central bank president personally dismembered by Nguan
    himself over inflation — not in 1968.
  - Dies ~1980 in the jungle villa, sorophine-related cardiac failure, no
    successor, no transition plan.
  - The villa retreat (~1972) holds 500 loyalists and all foreign currency
    reserves; he is the seed of the post-regime extraction economy.

→ Demographics of Bershad
  - Hierarchical tier structure under Nguan's program:
    Predator tier: Parund (11%, dominant), Mtole (8%, junior, rival).
    Tolerated-instrumental: Daiko (7%), Xaya (5%), Kadus (3%).
    Prey tier: Haiel (23%), Bokta, Dripstanians, Armotist clergy, later Buta.
    Yokno (25%) handled through selective elevation: pagan Yokno who renounce
    Armotism and accept Parund primacy receive a junior collaborator tier;
    rest are processed as prey progressively.
  - Parund-Mtole relationship is rivalry, not partnership. Both predator-tier
    but contest the meaning of the practice: Parund ritualistic-as-superiority
    (exo only), Mtole gastronomic (endo + exo). Each accuses the other of
    getting it wrong. Mtole resentment of Parund pretension is unbroken across
    regime and post-regime eras.

→ Politics of Bershad
  - The regime is mathematically a coalition project before an ideological
    one. Parund at 11% cannot govern alone; Nguan compromises while the
    program announces Parund hierarchy over all others. This compromise IS
    the regime's defining tension.
  - Persecution sequence: administrative removals and expropriations (1966),
    then displayed bodies in the capital (1967), then temple seizure and
    conversion to public consumption sites (1968–72), then mass victimization
    of Haiel and Bokta (1968–72).
  - Late-regime panic measures (NOT policy): currency collapse (~1973–74),
    border closure and shoot-on-sight (~1974–75), landmines on major routes
    to Sekyo, Yar-Firol, and the coast (~1974–75), fishing and boat bans on
    Haiel coast (~1974–75), conscription extended to children (~1975).
  - Institutional cascade (1972–79): schools defunded and abandoned, hospitals
    lose staff and supplies, Armotist clergy fled or dead. No demolition edicts;
    formal institutions intact, functionally void.
  - Cholera epidemic ~1978: international vaccine aid hoarded in the villa.
    Hundreds of thousands die of preventable disease.
  - Parund sacralized as the language of cosmos and sovereign (~1971). Speaking
    Susian marks prey, not a fixed sentence.
  - Plebiscite revokes 1962 constitution, single-party decree, absolute power
    for life (~1971). Cosmological consecration framing, not Mobutu-style
    self-coronation.

INTERPRETATION CHANGES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The mirror effect
Yar-Firol and Bershad each justify themselves by pointing at the other, and
each side's accurate description of the other is empirically correct. The
Buta genocide really is a successor-state operation in continuity with GGI
methods. The Bershadi predation really is pre-imperial Parund hierarchy scaled
to state power. Neither side is lying about the other. The lie is that either
side's accurate description of the other justifies its own program. I had
been reading this as parallel pathology; it is actually a single justification
engine with two terminals, structurally interdependent. Upstream: any
Yar-Firol writing must handle the mirror, not just the Buta operation; any
Bershad writing must handle the mirror, not just Nguan's internal program;
Susian Wave 2 critique's sharpest form runs through the symmetry.

The 11% problem and the inversion
Nguan announces Parund hierarchy over all others while needing most of those
others to govern at all. The regime spends eighteen years selling those
compromises to a base that didn't agree to them. San'motko is born from this
gap — the radicals who view every coalition compromise as betrayal — and the
regime captures them as instruments until they capture the regime back.
Nguan deteriorates not from personal pathology but because San'motko wins the
internal ideological argument; by ~1975 he is performing their program because
it is the only program left that legitimates him. This is bible non-negotiable
#4 (intellectual capture) in its purest form: every move Nguan makes to
escalate or restrain feeds San'motko's frame. Replaces the earlier reading
of the regime as a generic deteriorating dictatorship. Upstream: the Western
Coalition's behavior in 2026 is downstream of this dynamic, not a post-war
improvisation; Mtole resentment is unbroken across both eras; Wave 1
historiography's misidentification of San'motko-as-instrument is the
specific blind spot this reading exposes.

The right thing for the wrong reasons, sixty years late
Every neighbor of Bershad benefits from the catastrophe continuing. Yar-Firol
launders the sorophine economy through commodity boards; Sekyo runs its
democratic-transition narrative on the comparison; Veskera profits from
refining; Ashgerad denies Susia a sphere. Only Susia has no positive interest
in the current arrangement, and Susia's eventual intervention is driven by
the Susian-Ashgeradi cold war making Bershadi resolution geopolitically
useful — the humanitarian framing is the public face, the cold-war framing
is the operational logic. This replaces the prior implicit reading that
international neglect was passive. It is actively maintained complicity.
Upstream: any post-1980 Bershadi history runs through this; the failure of
Susia's intervention is over-determined by the fact that none of its regional
partners genuinely supported it; Wave 2 critique's deepest form is "the
intervention was designed to fail because the structure of regional incentives
required it to."

The empire's most faithful student
Susian establishment historiography reads Nguan as confirmation of imperial-
civilizing-necessity: look what they do when we leave. The actual mechanism
is the empire's own method scaled and pointed inward — one tribe using the
apparatus the empire built to suppress every other tribe. The empire picked
the Sanye, Haiel, and Bokta and integrated them; Nguan picked the Parund
and integrated them. The methodology is identical; only the favored tribe
changed. This is darker than the "internal pathology" reading and structurally
sharper. It is also the form of Wave 2 critique I expect to be ascendant in
the 2010s, which is what eventually cracks the empire-apologist consensus
that survived Arempur.

Native distrust of central authority is mechanically over-determined
The Yokno, Daiko, Buta, Kadus, Mtole, Xaya — every native group except the
Parund — share the same lesson from the Nguan period: the central state was
captured by one tribe, every other tribe was either prey or instrument, the
moment you trusted the apparatus you became one or the other. This is not
abstract anti-government sentiment but lived institutional memory. Any
successor central authority is read as the next vehicle of the next tribe's
capture, and there is no governance reform that overcomes this because the
lesson is correct. This is the structural explanation for why Bershad cannot
be rebuilt that the cluster has been missing. Replaces softer "ethnic
diversity is hard" and "post-colonial legacy" framings. Upstream: the 2026
frozen partition is rational equilibrium, not failure of state-building;
the international constitutional process's collapse in ~2000 is over-
determined by this lesson; any future writing on Bershadi politics has to
hold this as the default reading.
