REPORT (2026-08-24) — Yatovar dynasty, person schema, and the imperial succession

Topic: An editor session that began as a frontmatter audit of the Susian people
folder and became the construction of the Yatovar dynasty from an author-supplied
family chart and three tranches of Portuguese reign material. Twenty-one person
articles were repaired or created, the person template was changed, and the
imperial dating system was resolved against three independent sources.

Note on format: this was an editing session, so the material below is not a
proposal queue in the usual sense. PROPOSED CHANGES records what remains
unwritten. The rulings that govern how the vault is now read are in
INTERPRETATION CHANGES; they are already applied in the articles and are
recorded here so a later session does not re-litigate them from the flags
blocks alone.

INTERPRETATION CHANGES

The AS epoch, and the chart's error class
Gregorian equals 1950 minus AS. The epoch is confirmed exactly against the List
of Dripstanian emperors at four independent points: Mantichev 1674 to 1686,
Agamilos 1686 to 1738, Veronique 1740 to 1787, and Yaneoli's accession in 1787.
The apparent ten-year drift in the later generations of the family chart is not
an epoch shift. It is a class of digit errors in the chart itself: 145 for 135,
137 for 127, 170 for 150, and 143 for 123. Every one resolves to a value the
emperors list or the YALM era vocabulary already held. Where the chart and a
prose source disagree on a date, the prose source has won every time; the chart
has been reliable on relationships and unreliable on numbers in exactly one
generation.

Konph and Tekur are two peoples, two territories, two deportations
The vault previously linked [[Tekur|Konph]] in Veronique's article, which made
one population of two. They are separate: Konph is filed under Countries and has
no surviving members; Tekur is filed under Biology and remains populous in
Drippalia, Kapiogg and Celiolaj. Veronique deported the Konph to the Konph
Territory, which became Confia. Jartes I deported the Tekur to the Tekur
Territory, which became New Celiolaj. Two rulers, two peoples, two destinations,
two generations apart. The Tekurubićni Patrol already named both in a single
sentence and was the evidence that settled it.

Relations replaces spouse and children_count
The person template now carries a single relations field of typed kinship edges
and no separate spouse or children count. The rules that fell out of building it:
record the closest degree that holds, so Osvien carries Ligória as Sister with
the union in notes and Babal II carries one Grandfather entry with the doubling
noted; put legitimacy and stillbirth in notes rather than inventing relation
values; and record an unnamed group as one entry with person blank and the count
in notes. Each article holds only its own edges and the graph assembles itself.
The field exists because the chart could not otherwise be represented, and it
also gave the Soites adoption its first home in data rather than in one sentence
of prose.

Superseded place names are a recurring pattern, not isolated errors
Three confirmed this session: Poulônia for Zaphonia, Sussulândia for Sužielaj,
Nuva Celzielande for New Celiolaj. Each appeared in older author material and
each resolved a conflict rather than creating one; the Poulônia rename in
particular turned an apparently irreconcilable clash between the chart and the
Zaphonia article into an exact corroboration. Older material should be checked
for superseded naming before its content is treated as contradictory.

The chart is canonical on family, prose sources on institutions
The Capraže ruling and the Lekhertos reversal point in opposite directions and
together define the boundary. The chart was ruled canonical against two prose
sources on whether Capraže was Veronique's son or her son-in-law, because family
structure is what the chart is for. The chart was then vindicated against the
Zaphonia article on Lekhertos being Prince Consort rather than Emperor, but only
once a prose source established that his father Mawesi held the throne. The
reasoning that had favoured the article, that a consort has no throne to leave a
widow, was sound on a false premise: Prazde inherited through Mawesi's suicide,
not through her husband's death.

Slavery as the regional exception
Slavery in the empire was small and confined to Troli, and the reason is
agricultural rather than legal. Troli's plantation scale supported bonded labour;
Vitrika's homesteads did not generate the demand. The enslaved were Zaphonians
taken in Cericeiro raids across a border Troli shares with Zaphonia, which the
1790 vassalage then closed. Abolition came from Prazde mid-civil-war, and the
Trolian landowners revolted in the same period. The integration that followed
runs from Jartes I to Soliman and ends with the descendants as the state's
largest minority, observing traditions associated with slavery without friction,
in a state that also keeps Cericeiro Heritage Week.

COLLABORATION NOTES

- Do not report a red link as a created article. "Six new red links" was read as
  six new people and was not; only one file had been created that turn.
- Check for superseded place names before declaring a conflict. Poulônia,
  Sussulândia and Nuva Celzielande each looked like a contradiction and each was
  a rename.
- When a shared date looks like a placeholder, say so as a question rather than
  a finding. The three Khoseri deaths in 157 AS were flagged as probable chart
  artefacts and were the Tragedy of Takhesire.
- Leave a field blank rather than write a value already shown to be wrong.
  Prazde II carried no birth year for two turns and it was the right state.
- Outline fragments go into a stub with the outline preserved verbatim in flags,
  not into finished prose. The second half of the civil war is four bullets and
  is recorded as four bullets.
