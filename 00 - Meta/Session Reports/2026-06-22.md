Topic: Replaced the long Bershadi dictator (Ceyarp Igmud → Boe Nguan, Parund), removed the Bogtu lieutenant era, and redesigned the post-Nguan transition as an internationally-brokered constitution producing an immediately-exploded multiparty election. - Ashgeradi sponsorship from the start: material support modest, ideological framework formative.  
- ~1980: villa stormed by Bershadi opposition. Nguan dragged out and executed on live television. Footage became the cluster's iconic visual moment.

Cluster  
→ Modern degredo (practice article)  
- Successor-state continuation of imperial-era degredo (the abandonment of convicts on distant shores).  
- Yar-Firol operates the largest program. Procedures locally administered through provincial judiciary and border officials; no national policy on paper. Convicts driven to the Bershadi border in vans and told to walk.  
- Sekyo operates a smaller version, primarily Lawhá Xaya defendants returned to Bershadi Xaya regions as "ethnic repatriation."  
- Veskera does not degredo directly; pays Yar-Firol a fee to handle Veskeran convicts processed under deportation orders to Yar-Firol.  
- Susia does not participate. The Susian prison system absorbs domestic carceral cost.  
- Diaspora and Modernist critics use the archaic term _degredo_ deliberately; receiving states use "voluntary return," "third-country resettlement," and "repatriation processing."  
- Categories: stateless persons, Bershadi-descended residents convicted of any offense, mentally ill persons the host carceral system cannot or will not house, political undesirables, foreigners whose home countries decline repatriation.  
- Some degredados with useful skills (accountants, chemists, ex-soldiers with logistics training) rise in Bershadi warlord factions.

INTERPRETATION CHANGES

Cannibalism as five non-equivalent mechanisms  
The Bershadi cannibalism axis is not a single moral variable. Five groups practiced five different things, each with its own conversion susceptibility under Armotist missionary pressure. Baya converted because seasonal ceremonial endocannibalism had a structural slot Eucharistic ritual could fill. The other four did not, because their mechanisms had no equivalent substitution. The empire generalized from the Baya case and claimed the others would respond to the same pressure. They didn't, because the mechanism wasn't the same thing. Imperial apologetics about civilizing legacy break against this distinction. Affects Demographics of Bershad and any future article on Armotist missionary history.

Nguan as structurally over-determined, not a maniac  
Nguan's atrocities follow from a coherent combination: Parund supremacy ideology operating as state, Ashgeradi anti-colonial framework adopted regardless of fit, iatrogenic sorophine dependency from Susian psychiatric pharma, the impossible governing task of the most-extracted Bershadi territory, and the Buta refugee crisis from Yar-Firol. The man is over-determined by position. The "fucking maniac" framing should be retired from the article when carved. The decolonial nationalist rhetoric and the Parund-language imposition reveal "decolonize" as Parund supremacy with anti-colonial gloss, not indigenous pluralism. The dismemberments are Parund cosmology operating at state scale, one step shy of consumption. The sorophine-from-Neoveli-psychiatry mechanism is the cluster's cleanest single illustration of the empire-returns-through-pharmacology pattern. Affects the eventual Nguan person article and the Bershadi history article.

East-west visibility, not east-west moral temperature  
The asymmetry between eastern and western Bershadi civil-war factions is audience, not virtue. Eastern factions operate near ports, foreign press, NGOs, diplomatic contacts, and cross-border kin in Yar-Firol and Sekyo; they perform respectability for cameras. Western factions operate after Nguan's purge of telecoms and roads, with no audience; they kill less by volume but more visibly, in a register no eyes are pointed at. The cannibal frame focuses international attention on the part of the country international attention barely reaches and lets the eastern factions running larger-volume ethnic cleansing buy themselves a cleaner image. The western experience is also the cluster's least-narrated because survivors who could escape were selection-biased toward east-of-origin, leaving the deepest western suffering without diaspora voices. Affects the Bershadi Civil War article and any future article on Bershadi diaspora politics.

Frozen partition as active extraction architecture  
The 2026 frozen Bershadi partition is not residue of the failed 1990 election but the active product of neighboring states' continuous interest in Bershadi non-sovereignty. A unified internationally recognized Bershadi state could tax sorophine production, regulate cross-border trade, prosecute foreign intelligence operations, and refuse degredo. None of that is in Yar-Firol's or Sekyo's interest. The 1990 recognition split was less a divergence between Yar-Firol and Sekyo than a zoning agreement: each state recognized the Bershadi faction controlling its preferred extraction zone. Affects the Bershadi Civil War article, the Yar-Firol article, the Sekyo article, and Susian foreign-policy treatments of Incria.

Cluster parasitism: Rwanda-Congo applied through ethnic cross-border channels  
Yar-Firol and Sekyo are the regional Rwandas to Bershad's regional DRC. Their official prosperity is real and substantially funded by illicit cross-border extraction laundered through state-owned agricultural and chemical boards. Yar-Firol runs the operation through Buta cross-border kinship — the same population it genocided two decades earlier, now operationally indispensable via the surviving coastal Buta caucus. Sekyo runs the parallel operation through Haiel and Xaya cross-border kinship with Susian basing as operational depth. Veskera is one step downstream, refining and shipping what the two extractors deliver. The cluster's wealth gap is not the inherited compound interest on the 1960 secession alone; it is continuously produced. Affects the entire Incria-cluster treatment, the Yar-Firol article, the Sekyo article (significantly), Veskera's economic profile, and the Bershadi Civil War article.

The Yar-Firolian proxy realignment  
Yar-Firol genocided the Buta interior in the 1960s-70s and now arms, funds, and trains Buta-led Bershadi militia successors to Bogtu's apparatus. The 1990 recognition of the northern coalition is the diplomatic capstone. The same regime that organized one of the cluster's worst ethnic killings has become the international patron of an ethnically-Buta political formation, twenty years later, because the demographic identity it tried to eliminate at home is now operationally useful abroad. The Buta survivor caucus brokers the relationship. Institutional logic operating without moral memory in its clearest cluster instance. Affects the Yar-Firol article, the Buta Genocide article, the Buta survivor caucus article, and the Bershadi Civil War article.

Modern degredo as state-capacity practice  
Degredo is not state cynicism or offshored superpower complicity. It tracks state capacity: states that can't afford to house convicts for decades and can't politically afford to release them dump across the Bershadi border because the geography and the incentive align. Yar-Firol's industrial-scale operation, Sekyo's small version, Veskera's fee-based outsourcing, and Susia's procedural non-participation form a graduated moral cartography. Susia's procedural cleanliness is real and depends on the regional architecture in which its neighbors do what Susia does not. Modernist critique stops short of advocating Susian absorption of regional resettlement because such advocacy is politically impossible in Susia. Affects the Modern degredo article, the Yar-Firol article, the Veskera article, and Susian foreign-policy treatments.

Bogtu removed  
The lieutenant era between Nguan and the civil war is eliminated. The long dictator is killed directly by the civil war's opening operation. The televised execution transfers from Bogtu's killing of Ceyarp to Nguan's own killing by Bershadi opposition forces storming the villa. The Buta-loyalist faction in the post-Nguan war still exists but is led by genocide-refugee militia commanders, not by an ex-president. Affects the Bershadi Civil War article and any article touching the 1980–1989 period.
