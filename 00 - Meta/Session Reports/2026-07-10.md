INTERPRETATION CHANGES

The nation/idea chiasmus
TU and Van Ritter are the ideological poles of the Yarnojte set because they are the only two that take a position on Susia-as-concept; the other four take positions on domains (choice, biology, labor, invisibility). The two split the founding into its idea and its form and hold each in opposite valence. Van Ritter keeps Susia's body and voids its content; TU keeps Susia's content and voids its country. Susia as Versij imagined it, liberty enacted through a republic, lives in neither of its two most powerful institutions. This replaces the earlier reading of Van Ritter as the weakest and most genre-conventional of the six; that read graded the FEZ container rather than the object. Affects Van Ritter, Troli Ustaras, and the poles-versus-interior structure of the Yarnojte master article.

Sincere denationalization as the capture mechanism
TU's erasure of Susian origin is sincere, not cynical. Apaj grounded his cosmopolitan ethics on the Versijian Self-Axiom while defining the framework as derivable from consciousness and reason alone and from no specific civilization, so TU's founding text is structurally obligated to deny its Susian source. The sincerity is the capture: the most Susian thing in existence, the axiom the 1954 order rests on, was floated free of the nation and believed to belong to no one. This sharpens the existing Apaj/Kolkov capture material and should govern any writing on Apajian Universalism. Affects Troli Ustaras, Ňotrič Apaj, Apajian Universalism.

Method-agnostic Susification and the rebellion-as-instrument inversion
The federal government does not see TU as an ideological rival. It sits above both poles and treats them as two delivery mechanisms for one product, the Susia-friendly client, buying the outcome and remaining indifferent to the road. This makes TU's sincere denial of origin its value to the state rather than a limit on it: a foreign convert to Apajian universalism experiences self-generated conviction, never feels colonized, and never registers as Susian influence because the doctrine forbids the Susian label, which makes the conversion permanent and invisible. TU's attempt to transcend Susia is Susia's most effective instrument of reach. Each pole's real relation to the nation inverts its self-image: Van Ritter knows it is a state tool and is proud of it; TU believes it is free and is the most used of the six. Retires the "rival evangelisms / active state-TU tension" framing raised earlier this session. Affects Cericeiro Doctrine, Troli Ustaras, Van Ritter, FVS foreign policy.

The latent fracture (future-century, not active)
Post-national eventually means post-Susian-friendly. TU's loyalty is grounded in universal reason rather than in Susia, and the doctrine that dissolved a convert's old nation contains no clause exempting Susia's grip from the same dissolution. Given enough time and converts, TU's universalism can reason its way out of alignment with Susian interests; Van Ritter's object-loyalty to Susia cannot. The state is harvesting an edge it is not watching. Hold this as a fracture for a later century, latent, not to be surfaced as present tension. Affects future TU foreign-policy writing and the Cericeiro Doctrine.
