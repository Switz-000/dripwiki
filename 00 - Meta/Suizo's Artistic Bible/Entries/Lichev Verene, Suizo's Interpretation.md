This is my interpretation and the narrative logic I use to make [[Lischev-Verene]]

LV is a benevolent actor whose benevolence is the problem. There is no mask to peel back (the cures are real, the care is sincere) and nothing to vindicate (the care costs you the burden of being a person, and you hand it over gladly). You cannot hate it and cannot trust it.

## Traits:

**Not idealistic, not insidious. Unillusioned.** TU lies to itself; LV lies to itself about nothing. It knows exactly what it is, the data grab is a data grab, the Garden is a golden hospital, and does it anyway, clear-eyed.

**The noble lie.** A guardian curates truth in your interest, because it has decided you are better **served rested than informed**. Not deception to exploit, management for your good.

**The two halves** "It is what it is" (tired pragmatists, the Lischev/cynical side) vs. "better us than them" (the Verene/idealist side, justifying the paternalism by pointing at TU's worse one). Neither half is zealous; both share the premise that LV's authority over the patient is real and good. (WIP)
## Main inspirations

**Circe.** LV offers relief from **the burden of being human**. Like Circe's men in the sty, people accept gratefully, well-fed, tended, unburdened, and _do not want to change back_. The cage is mercy. The loss is invisible to the one who suffers it, and they are not wrong to choose it.

**[[Verene]]**. The goddess of rest who _guides_ the soul back to life, **she does not offer a menu**, she takes your hand because she knows the way and you do not. LV's self-image and theology. The withholding is priestly, not corporate.

**Leviathan (just a light touch).** The unmanaged world outside: The [[Ňotrídulij]] rat race, [[Cognitive enhancement]], existential dread, materialism, the decaying body, is the state of nature. You surrender sovereignty to LV not because it is virtuous but because the alternative is worse, and you know it.
