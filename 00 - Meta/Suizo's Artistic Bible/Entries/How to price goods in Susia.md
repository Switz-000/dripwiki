Method: every price is built from one baseline anchor, then each lever applied in order. A good's final shelf price is a path through the levers. To price a new good, walk the levers and ask which apply.
Step 0 — Anchor by ratio, never by IRL conversion.
Price against an established good, not a real-world price. The commodity anchors are: bread D$3.80 / 500g, milk D$3.20 / 1L, commodity beef D$11 / 500g. A new good's pre-tax baseline is set by its ratio to the nearest anchor, justified by mechanism. (This is the discipline that kept correcting beef downward.)
Lever 1 — Commodity input floor (sets the ratio).
What are the raw inputs, and are they Susia-cheap? Grain, sugar, dairy, bison-meat, and animal coproducts (tallow, butterfat, hide) are all cheap — LV yield-compression on crops, the coproduct web off the meat/dairy systems. A good made mostly of these has a low floor. Establishes where in the ratio-space the good starts.
Lever 2 — Processing / overhead share (the master lever).
What fraction of the price is irreducible input+labor versus strippable overhead (brand, marketing, packaging, margin)? This single ratio governs Levers 4, 5, and 6 — it determines how big every subsequent discount is. Commodity staple = low overhead share (near floor already). Processed/branded good = high overhead share. This is the most important lever; determine it before pricing.
Lever 3 — LV / genetics compression (input-cost modifier).
Does LV genetics reach this good's inputs? Crops: yes, taxed (seed moat, litigation-enforced — partial claw-back keeps the discount modest). Livestock: yes, untaxed (competitive animal-genetics market, retained breeding stock available — full pass-through). Applies to the input floor, not the shelf price. Crop goods carry a small LV tax; meat goods don't.
Lever 4 — Sisra (private label discount).
Only if a branded competitor exists. Discount scales inversely with commodity content (i.e. with Lever 2): ~15-20% off branded on commodity staples, ~30-46% on processed/branded-heavy goods. Sisra is near-useless on raw staples, devastating on processed. Not applicable to goods with no meaningful brand layer (fresh, unbranded).
Lever 5 — Point-of-sale geography (two axes, both on the store not the good).
5a Logistics axis: distance from the Nayotai rail spine. FFTZ/core-corridor cheapest; national baseline is the default quotable price because dense rail collapses regional spread; rail-periphery (Nessel, deep interior) carries a small premium.
5b Cost-of-living axis: urban land/wage overhead at the point of sale. Neoveli/Duolij cores dear. Size scales with Lever 2 — modest on commodity staples (good is mostly good), steep on prepared/fresh (price is mostly the store).
The two axes can hit the same number for opposite reasons (far-thin-market vs near-thick-market).
Lever 6 — FFTZ discount (only inside the Soites FFTZ).
Two independent components:
6a Retail discount: ~25% on margin-heavy goods, but compresses on labor-heavy goods to ~15%, because wages are a floor the discount can't reach.
6b VAT rebate: Soites absorbs Postia's state VAT, delivered as 8% at register. (Postia-specific; the FFTZ is in Postia.)
Lever 7 — Tax (always last, applies to post-everything pre-tax price).
Federal VAT 5% nationwide always. State VAT on top, variable: Dripia 0% (SIC-financed abolition), Postia [rate open] but rebated inside FFTZ, others variable.
The two hard rules the levers encode:
Overhead share (Lever 2) is the master variable. It sets the magnitude of Sisra, urban premium, and FFTZ retail discount simultaneously. Determine it first. It's why the same three goods (bread/milk/beef) are the ones least affected by Sisra, least affected by urban premium, and least affected by FFTZ discount — one property explains all three.
Discounts made of margin propagate; discounts made of labor resist. Any lever that strips overhead (Sisra, FFTZ retail) works on margin-heavy goods and fails on labor/wage-heavy goods. The more a price is wages and equipment, the flatter it is across the whole system — which is exactly why restaurants sit outside the entire lever structure.
Worked example — the beaver tail, showing why it's the edge case:
Anchor: fresh good, no clean commodity anchor → priced on labor, not ratio. L1 inputs trivial (cheap flour/sugar/jelly/oil). L2 overhead share: inverted — almost no brand overhead, but high labor share. L3 crop inputs, minor LV tax, negligible. L4 Sisra: N/A (fresh, unbranded). L5b urban axis: steep (labor-dominated). L6a FFTZ retail: compressed to ~15% (labor resists). Result: the good where the margin-levers all go quiet and the wage-levers all dominate — the bridge between the grocery lever-system and the restaurant sector that sits entirely outside it. The beaver tail is where you can see the lever-system hand off to the wage economy.
