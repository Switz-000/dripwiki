BERSHAD

→ Boe Nguan (person article; replaces the Nguan section of [[Politics]])
  - Parund, Pagan. Born in a small village in inland Parund territory. Served as a public order officer in the General Government.
  - Rules de jure 1962–1981. Rules de facto 1962–1973, after which the country has collapsed and he is a figurehead.
  - Native-nationalist platform; campaign to "decolonize" Bershad from Dripstanian influence.
  - Ashgeradi sponsorship from the start: material support modest, ideological framework formative.
  - 1962–1965, reformer phase: land redistribution to Parund, Mtole and Daiko in declining priority, commercial protectionism, nationalization of foreign property. The reforms fail because the colonial economy is the only economy, and because the redistribution was hierarchically Parund-first rather than universally indigenous.
  - 1965: secret trip to [[Neoveli]] with an aide for psychiatric treatment. Sorophine dependency begins, possibly from the prescribed medication.
  - 1966: arrests his main political opponent and other Haiel on treason charges of cooperating with [[Susia]] and [[Yar-Firol]]. Persecution of Haiel, Dripstanians and Armotists begins as administrative removals and expropriations. Military service introduced for ages 18–35.
  - 1967: orders the arrest of the Minister of Economy and the Minister of Foreign Relations; both dismembered, bodies displayed in government buildings in the capital. 106 further government agents charged with treason. Susian and creole languages banned, 30-year prison sentences for use. Import of televisions and magazines banned.
  - 1968: plebiscite revokes the 1962 constitution, replaced by a decree granting Nguan absolute power for life and forbidding all political organizations besides his party. Cosmological consecration, not self-coronation.
  - 1968: personally dismembers the president of the central bank after an argument over debt and inflation, so the man could "learn how to count." Purges the country's economists and accountants. No reliable economic or demographic data exists after this point.
  - 1968–1972: temples seized and converted into public consumption sites. Mass victimization of Haiel and Bokta.
  - 1969: purges the entire cabinet. Leaves the capital permanently for a presidential villa in the jungle, out of paranoia, with 500 loyal men and all of the state's foreign currency reserves. The reserves in the villa are the seed of the later extraction economy.
  - 1970: cholera epidemic. Internationally donated vaccines hoarded in the villa, distributed only to close supporters and family. Hundreds of thousands die of preventable disease in the following years.
  - 1971: Parund sacralized as the language of cosmos and sovereign.
  - 1973: de facto collapse of the Bershadi state. San'motko rules some areas; warlords divide the rest. Nguan remains head of state as a figurehead, held hostage in his villa.
  - 1981: dies in the villa of sorophine-related cardiac failure. No successor, no transition plan.
  - Panic measures, from 1968 (reactive improvisations to the program's failure, not policy):
    - 1968: fishing and boat ban on the Haiel coast.
    - 1968: borders closed, military ordered to shoot anyone approaching them.
    - 1968: some schools, hospitals and temples demolished. The rest remain standing and functionally void. Traditional medicine promoted in their place, while advisors and government friends travel abroad for surgery.
    - 1969: landmines on every road leading out of the country.
    - 1969: military service extended to start at age 8.
    - 1969: currency collapses; barter and subsistence farming follow.
    - Mass exodus: tens of thousands die, shot by the military or crossing the jungle.

→ Politics of Bershad (the regime's structure)
  - The regime is a coalition project before an ideological one. The Parund, at 11%, cannot govern alone. Nguan compromises while the program announces Parund hierarchy over all others; that compromise is the regime's defining tension.
  - Tier structure under the program:
    - Predator tier: Parund (11%, dominant), Mtole (8%, junior, rival).
    - Tolerated-instrumental: Daiko (7%), Xaya (5%), Kadus (3%).
    - Prey tier: Haiel (23%), Bokta, Dripstanians, Armotist clergy, later Buta.
    - Yokno (25%) handled through selective elevation: pagan Yokno who renounce Armotism and accept Parund primacy receive a junior collaborator tier; the rest are processed as prey progressively.
  - Parund-Mtole relationship is rivalry, not partnership. Both are predator-tier but contest the meaning of the practice: Parund ritualistic-as-superiority (exo only), Mtole gastronomic (endo and exo). Each accuses the other of getting it wrong. Mtole resentment of Parund pretension is unbroken across regime and post-regime eras.
  - The regime maintains a public fiction of separation from San'motko: "we do not direct this, this is what the population does when given freedom."

→ San'motko
  - Founded 1966 (per frontmatter). Origins in the reformer phase (~1962–1965) as the radical fringe of Parund supremacism. Public partnership with Nguan formalized ~1965–1966, concurrent with the Neoveli trip and sorophine onset.
  - Nguan's strategic use of it: terror instrument against insufficiently aligned coalition partners; plausible-deniability operator for the regime's signature horrors; ritual-purity custodian that legitimates Nguan's coalition compromises elsewhere.
  - Processes Buta refugees fleeing the Yar-Firolian genocide at scale in the northwest, ~1972–1976. Directed, not grassroots.
  - The leash inverts: San'motko wins the regime's internal ideological argument. By the 1973 collapse, Nguan is a figurehead.
  - Consolidates northwestern Bershad as a parallel state by ~1975, held continuously through the partition to the present.
  - 1981: public refoundation after Nguan's death.
  - Public posture during the vacuum (1973 to ~1995): restoration of Parund traditional authority in the absence of central government. Internal posture: political-revolutionary organization. Both held simultaneously.
  - By the 2080s: functions as the state party of the Western Coalition Zone.

→ Cannibalism in Bershad (own article, split from Cultures of Bershad)
  - Parund: ritualized exocannibalism with predation, as superiority. A pre-Dripstanian supremacy ideology that scales to a state.
  - Mtole: gastronomic, endo and exo. Flat ontology between human and animal.
  - Daiko: medical care-cannibalism. Self-flesh boiled for sick kin; children killed for boiled-heart life extension; legend of [[Mt'yorsur]]. Also eaten out of scarcity. Functions as healing technology, not aggression.
  - Xaya: funerary endocannibalism, ceremonial, rare. Kin remain in the community through consumption.
  - Baya: seasonal ceremonial endocannibalism. Converted out under Armotist mission through ritual substitution into an Armotist rite.

→ Bershadi Civil War (eventual article)

  - Nguan-era deaths from starvation, cholera, landmines, exodus and executions: 2–4 million (working figure).
  - Collapse and civil-war deaths up to the partition: 3–5 million (working figure).
  - Cumulative Bershadi deaths from 1962 to the 2020s: 6–10 million (working range).
  - By the 2020s, millions of internally displaced people in semi-permanent camps.
  - 1973–~1990: no civil war yet. Warlord-zone equilibrium, no national authority, no international actor proposing one.
  - ~1990: open inter-zone conflict begins as consolidated zones fight over their boundaries.
  - Funded by the sorophine economy through Yar-Firolian commodity boards and Veskeran refining.
  - Sorophine dosing of militia child soldiers as an operational mechanism, particularly in the Western Coalition.
  - Western Coalition practice: skull trophies on vehicles, mutilation of captives, terror operations against villages, mass graves.
  - Eastern faction practice (Yokno revanchist, Buta loyalist, Haiel southern, Sanye northeast): ethnic cleansing, industrial-scale sexual violence, child soldier recruitment, mass graves discovered after the fact.
  - Eastern factions issue communiqués, hold press conferences and employ spokespeople; western factions do not.
  - The Conference (see below) and its election; open war resumes ~2000; Susian intervention; partition freezes.

→ The Bershadi Conference (placeholder name)
  - Convened by Susia ~1995–1998. Internationally observed, neutral venue, brokered constitution.
  - Yar-Firol, Sekyo and Veskera participate publicly while protecting their extraction interests. Ashgerad absent.
  - Produces a document everyone signs and no one accepts.

→ Western Coalition Zone (eventual article, working title; 2080s)
  - International pariah. Sanctioned by Susia, Yar-Firol, Sekyo, Veskera and most of the world. Ashgerad declines formal recognition. Possible unrecognized missions in Ashgerad-aligned peripheral states.
  - San'motko is the state party.
  - Coalition: Parund, Daiko, Mtole, Kadus, Parund dominant. Three practice in different modes (Daiko medical and scarcity, Mtole gastronomic, Parund predation); the Kadus do not practice but live among those who do.
  - The capital contains a walled foreign-protection district where the state suspends predation as policy. It houses embassies, unrecognized missions, foreign journalists on tours, NGO compounds and aid distribution. An elite unit enforces non-predation inside the wall.
  - Boundary-test incidents (foreign citizens or aid workers harmed) produce televised internal punishments that restore the foreign zone.
  - Internal life is structured around predation status as political hierarchy. Public ritual practice as state celebration; broadcast festivals show the leadership participating.
  - "Enemies of the people" added to the predation pool by state decree.
  - Economy outside sorophine is subsistence. Infrastructure declines.

YAR-FIROL

→ Buta Genocide (event article)
  - Bureaucratically rationalized ethnic cleansing of the inland Buta population. Begins by 1963; peak years ~1965–~1975.

  - Settlers armed themselves and put bounties on the Buta: private-enterprise ethnic violence layered on state policy.
  - Coastal assimilated Buta professionals not targeted; retained as institutional evidence of the regime's non-ethnic character.
  - Concurrent with Nguan's regime.
  - International press coverage muted. The Susian establishment did not embarrass the ally.

BUTA

CLUSTER

→ Modern degredo (practice article)
  - Successor-state continuation of imperial-era degredo (abandonment of convicts on distant shores).
  - Yar-Firol runs the largest program. Locally administered through provincial judiciary and border officials; no national policy on paper. Convicts driven to the Bershadi border in vans and told to walk.
  - Sekyo runs a smaller version, primarily Lawhá Xaya defendants returned to Bershadi Xaya regions as "ethnic repatriation."
  - Veskera does not degredo directly; it pays Yar-Firol a fee to handle Veskeran convicts processed under deportation orders to Yar-Firol.
  - Susia does not participate. The Susian prison system absorbs domestic carceral cost.
  - Diaspora and Modernist critics use the archaic term _degredo_ deliberately; receiving states use "voluntary return," "third-country resettlement" and "repatriation processing."
  - Categories: stateless persons, Bershadi-descended residents convicted of any offense, mentally ill persons the host system cannot or will not house, political undesirables, foreigners whose home countries decline repatriation.
  - Some degredados with useful skills (accountants, chemists, ex-soldiers with logistics training) rise in Bershadi warlord factions.

→ [[Sorophine]] (trade)
  - Legitimate Susia-Veskera-Yar-Firol pharmaceutical pipeline routes around the Western Coalition Zone. Sources: Yar-Firolian interior, Tehtíe, [[Ditania]].

→ Diaspora waves (eventual article)
  - Wave 1: fled Nguan's regime. Haiel coastal class, Bokta, Dripstanians, Armotist clergy, and a Yokno educated cohort (the partial-collaborator tier who read the hierarchy correctly and got out).
  - Wave 2: fled the 1990s civil war, for the war's sake rather than a specific regime. Includes people who lived under San'motko's northwestern state.
  - The schism is generational and ideological. Wave 1 fled the regime apparatus and reads San'motko as its tool: correct in 1966, false by the collapse.

INTERPRETATION CHANGES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The mirror effect
Already carried in the bible entry "Geopolitics of dripstanian Incria." Consequence for writing: any Yar-Firol article must handle the mirror, not just the Buta operation; any Bershad article must handle it, not just Nguan's program. The sharpest Wave 2 critique runs through the symmetry.

The 11% problem and the inversion
Nguan announces Parund hierarchy over all others while needing most of those others to govern at all. San'motko is born in that gap, from the radicals who read every coalition compromise as betrayal; the regime captures them as instruments until they capture the regime back. The regime's program runs 1962–1967. From 1968 its measures are panic, and by 1973 the state has collapsed and Nguan survives as a figurehead of the program San'motko now owns, held in his own villa. Every move he made to escalate or restrain fed San'motko's frame. Replaces the reading of the regime as a generic deteriorating dictatorship. Upstream: the Western Coalition's behavior is downstream of this dynamic, not a post-war improvisation; Wave 1 historiography's reading of San'motko as instrument is the blind spot this exposes.

Nguan: over-determined, and still the man
Nguan's atrocities follow from a coherent combination: Parund supremacy operating as state, an Ashgeradi anti-colonial framework adopted regardless of fit, iatrogenic sorophine dependency from Susian psychiatric pharmacology, the governing task of the most-extracted Bershadi territory, and the Buta refugee flow from Yar-Firol. "Decolonize" is Parund supremacy with anti-colonial gloss, not indigenous pluralism; the dismemberments are Parund cosmology at state scale, one step short of consumption. The Neoveli sorophine is the cluster's cleanest instance of the empire returning through pharmacology. The pathology framing is retired. But the structural thesis strains on him: the personal acts before 1973 are his, and reading him purely as institutions understates the man. [[Ergagério Sienes]] is the case the thesis explains cleanly; Nguan is the case it works around. Both belong in canon.

The empire's most faithful student
Susian establishment historiography reads Nguan as confirmation of imperial civilizing necessity. The actual mechanism is the empire's own method scaled and pointed inward: the empire picked the Sanye, Haiel and Bokta and integrated them; Nguan picked the Parund. The methodology is identical; only the favored tribe changed. This is the form Wave 2 critique takes, and it is what eventually cracks the empire-apologist consensus.

Native distrust of central authority is over-determined
Every native group except the Parund learned the same lesson from the Nguan period: the central state was captured by one tribe, and the moment you trusted the apparatus you became prey or instrument. That is lived institutional memory, not abstract anti-government sentiment, and no governance reform overcomes it because the lesson is correct. This is the structural explanation for why Bershad cannot be rebuilt. The frozen partition is a rational equilibrium, and the Conference's collapse around 2000 is over-determined by it. Replaces "ethnic diversity is hard" and "post-colonial legacy" framings.

Cannibalism as five non-equivalent mechanisms
Five groups practiced five different things, each with its own susceptibility to Armotist missionary pressure. The Baya converted because seasonal ceremonial endocannibalism had a slot Armotist ritual could fill. The other four had no equivalent substitution. The empire generalized from the Baya and claimed the rest would respond to the same pressure; they did not, because the mechanism was not the same thing. Imperial apologetics about civilizing legacy break on this distinction.

Cluster parasitism through cross-border kinship
Yar-Firol's and Sekyo's official prosperity is real and substantially funded by illicit Bershadi extraction laundered through state boards. Yar-Firol runs it through Buta kinship, the same population it tried to eliminate at home, now indispensable through the survivor caucus; Sekyo runs it through Haiel and Xaya kinship with Susian basing as depth; Veskera refines downstream. The cluster's wealth gap is not only inherited from the secession; it is continuously produced. The Conference's recognition split was less a divergence than a zoning agreement: each state recognized the faction controlling its preferred extraction zone. A unified sovereign Bershad could tax sorophine, regulate trade, prosecute foreign intelligence operations and refuse degredo; that is in no neighbor's interest.

The Yar-Firolian proxy realignment
The regime that organized one of the cluster's worst ethnic killings becomes, within two decades, a patron of Buta-led Bershadi militias and recognizes the coalition they belong to, because the identity it tried to eliminate at home is useful abroad. The survivor caucus brokers it. Institutional logic operating without moral memory.

The right thing for the wrong reasons
Every neighbor benefits from the catastrophe continuing: Yar-Firol launders, Sekyo narrates, Veskera refines, Ashgerad denies Susia a sphere. Only Susia has no positive interest in the arrangement, and its intervention is driven by the cold war, with humanitarian framing as the public face. The intervention fails in part because none of its regional partners, allies in name, wanted it to succeed. The deepest Wave 2 critique: it was designed to fail because the regional incentive structure required it to.

The complicity model toward the Western Coalition Zone
Toward the eastern and southern zones, the cluster runs an extraction architecture. Toward the Western Coalition Zone it runs something else: open sanction, tolerated smuggling, non-intervention. The regime is straightforwardly evil; the cluster's stance is structural cowardice. They condemn it and will not pay what removal would cost. The partition is stable in the sense that nothing changes, not in any moral sense.

The foreign district as self-indictment
The regime can suspend the practice inside a walled district when it chooses. That proves predation is policy, not cultural compulsion: they eat the people they choose to eat. This is the detail that makes the polity legible without making it sympathetic.

Visibility, not moral temperature
The asymmetry between eastern and western factions in the war years is audience, not virtue. Eastern factions operate near ports, press, NGOs and cross-border kin, and perform respectability for cameras. Western factions, after the collapse took telecoms and roads, had no audience; they killed less by volume but more visibly, in a register no one was watching. The cannibal frame focuses attention on the part of the country attention barely reaches, and lets the eastern factions running larger-volume ethnic cleansing buy a cleaner image. The western experience is also the least narrated: those who escaped were selected toward eastern origins. By the 2080s the Western Coalition Zone manufactures its own controlled audience inside the foreign district.

Modern degredo tracks state capacity
Degredo is not cynicism or offshored complicity. States that cannot afford to house convicts for decades and cannot politically release them dump across the Bershadi border because geography and incentive align. Yar-Firol's industrial program, Sekyo's small one, Veskera's fee and Susia's non-participation form a graduated moral cartography. Susia's procedural cleanliness is real and depends on its neighbors doing what it does not. Modernist critique stops short of advocating Susian absorption of regional resettlement, because that is politically impossible in Susia.

Four models of coexistence under hatred
One group wins so hard the others stop being political subjects (Yar-Firol and the Buta). Exclusion formalized below public discourse (Sekyo and the Lawhá). Demographic absorption before politicization (Veskera). Shared procedural commitment with the unsolved pieces postponed (Susia). Bershad got none. Replaces flat "diverse society" framings.

Susia is not Bershad with better luck
Susia's coexistence works because the deferred questions were small enough to slow-resolve through institutional persistence; the republic's handling of the Tekur is the cleanest example. Susia is in a different problem space from Bershad, not the same one with better outcomes. Replaces the framing of Susia as a latent catastrophe waiting for a trigger.
