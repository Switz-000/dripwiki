**Eplevakt** was a vesicant chemical weapon fill produced by [[Kapiogg]] and employed against [[Confia|Confian]] positions on the northern front of the [[Continental War]] between 1976 and 1977. It consisted of a sulfur mustard agent held in chlorobenzene solution and was issued in two seasonal formulations, Eplevakt 9 and Eplevakt 11. Its employment is recorded as the [[Sweet Apple Incidents]].

## Development

Eplevakt originated as a commercial orchard pesticide line. It was produced at a chemical works in the western provinces of Kapiogg, built during the [[Continental Divide]] period under Susian agricultural modernization aid and part of the wider program of industrial assistance [[Susia]] extended to Kapiogg following Kapioggean entry into the [[ODDN]]. The western provinces are fruit-growing country, and the works was sited to serve that agriculture.

Kapioggean work on vesicant fills began after the northern front settled into static positions. Production of weaponized fills started in 1976. No program designation was created and no new product file was opened at the works. The fills were manufactured, accounted for and shipped under the existing Eplevakt product designations.

## Composition

The active agent was a sulfur mustard, recorded systematically as 1-chloro-2-(2-chloroethylsulfanyl)ethane and also as bis(2-chloroethyl) sulfide. It is a vesicant. It damages skin, eyes and the respiratory tract on contact, penetrates clothing and rubber, and produces no immediate pain at the point of exposure. Injury appears between roughly 12 and 36 hours afterward.

The agent was held in solution in chlorobenzene, an industrial solvent used in dye and pesticide manufacture. Chlorobenzene lowers the freezing point of the fill, which in undiluted form solidifies at temperatures reached routinely on the northern front in winter, and thins it for shell filling and dispersal. Chlorobenzene carries a sweet, almond-like odor. The sulfur mustard component is the toxic one. The solvent is not a vesicant.

### Eplevakt 9

The warm-weather fill, weighted toward the agent. It was the less volatile of the two, persisted on ground, clothing and equipment for extended periods, and produced predominantly skin injury and denial of terrain. Secondary casualties among medical staff and among families handling returned equipment are attributed principally to this fill.

### Eplevakt 11

The cold-weather fill, weighted toward the solvent to keep it liquid in low temperatures. It volatilized more rapidly, dispersed more widely on delivery, and accumulated as vapor in trenches, dugouts and cellars. Casualties from Eplevakt 11 were predominantly respiratory and ocular.

Neither designation corresponded to a fixed formulation. The works held no specification for either fill, and the proportion of solvent to agent varied between batches according to feedstock supply.

## Production

Production ran at the same works throughout, using the plant, staff and accounting of the pesticide line. The product sheet was never amended. Shell manifests issued through 1976 and 1977 describe the contents of consignments by the chemical name of the fungicide the Eplevakt line had carried before 1976.

## Employment

> *Main article: [[Sweet Apple Incidents]]*

Eplevakt was employed against Confian positions and occupied settlements in the western provinces between 1976 and 1977. Casualties included Confian soldiers, [[Drippalia|Drippalian]] and Kapioggean [[Tekur]] civilians, Kapioggean soldiers advancing across ground contaminated by their own artillery, and allied aircrew. Susia issued a formal condemnation under President [[Čalsor Boňár]] and took no further measures against Kapiogg for the duration of the war.

Because the solvent is more volatile than the agent, ground contaminated by Eplevakt ceased to carry the odor before it ceased to be hazardous. Troops on both sides came to treat the absence of the odor as an indication that ground was clear.

## Designation and naming

The fill was recorded under five designations.

At the works and in Kapioggean military accounting it was Eplevakt, with the fill number appended. kapioggean artillery units shortened this to VAKT 9 and VAKT 11 in written orders, and referred to the fills in speech as the nines and the elevens.

Confian and Drippalian troops named it sweet apples, after the odor. The name was in general use on the northern front before the end of 1976 and is the name under which the campaign is recorded.

Susia and the ODDN assigned no designation during the war. Collection on the works was held in economic and trade files under an agricultural classification. The designation Vranik was assigned in 1978, after the war had ended, when the file was reclassified.

The kapioggean product name and the Confian field name were coined independently of one another. Eplevakt was the trade name of an apple orchard spray. The field name described the odor of a solvent. Neither refers to the sulfur mustard. The correspondence between the two was identified in 1978, in the course of the examination of the works records.
