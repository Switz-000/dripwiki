(stub)

**Apaj's perscription to society:**
- The nation-state is a scarcity artifact. It was built to manage competition over limited resources and provide identity in a world of impotence. Its necessity diminishes as scarcity diminishes.
- Cultural governance is illegitimate at the universal level. Laws built on particular traditions cannot claim universal authority.
- A post-national order must be grounded in [[Apajian Universalism|universal principles only]] those derivable from [[Versijian Self-Axiom|consciousness and reason]], not from any specific civilization.
- Technology is the enabler, not the solution. It defeats material scarcity and creates the conditions for political evolution. It cannot replace the political work itself.
- The transition is the hardest part. He acknowledges this without solving it.


**Unresolved questions:**
- Existential scarcity has no technological solution yet. Meaning, belonging, identity, these cannot be engineered.
- Universal principles need a universal community to give them weight. But community requires particularity, shared history, language, narrative.
- The transition to post-national order passes through maximum danger. The moment when old structures dissolve but new ones aren't built yet.
