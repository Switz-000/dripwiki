The **right to rebel** is the right of [[Susia|Susian]] citizens to take up arms against a government that systematically violates the natural rights recognized by the [[Susian Constitution]]. It derives from the philosophy of [[Armadesh Versij]] and was written into the constitution in 1954. In law it is a right; in Susian civic culture it is also a duty.

## Origins

Versij held that citizens retain the right to overthrow a government that systematically violates natural rights, and that the right is credible only where they hold the organized means to exercise it. In [[Architecture of Freedom]] he tied it to the concept of the "citizen guards of the republic": rights not backed by the capacity to defend them are privileges contingent on the goodwill of whoever holds power.

### The Liberal Revolts

> *Main article: [[Liberal Revolts]]*

[[Jartes II]] banned the [[Sorzenko|Sorzenkos]] and restricted civilian firearms ownership before the Liberal Revolts of 1840 to 1844. The reading that became dominant in Susian political memory holds that the disarmament was a precondition of the revolt's defeat. The constitution of 1954 enshrined the right to rebel partly in direct response.

## Legal form

The constitution permits citizens to take up arms to resist a government that systematically violates the natural rights it recognizes, including freedom of speech, the right to self-defense, the right to own and use property, and the right to life. It confers a right and imposes no obligation.

The provision sits alongside the constitutional protection of open and concealed carry. Susian constitutional scholars regard the combination of these protections, [[Military Service in Susia|mandatory military service]] and the Sorzenko system as making the right practically credible rather than symbolic, since organized militia capacity exists at the level of the civilian population.

## Civic reading

Susian civic culture treats the right as a duty. The reading rests on a distinction general in Susian political thought between the nation and the state. The nation is the compact among citizens and the constitutional order, known as *[[List of political positions#Susian Nationalism|Hǎčech]]*; the state is the government that administers it and is capable of violating it. The patriot's loyalty runs to the nation, and includes the duty to defend the nation against the state when the state breaks the compact.

The duty is carried by institutions and custom rather than by law. Sorzenko membership, private firearms ownership and service are understood as the means by which citizens remain able to meet it, and Versij's formulation that a citizen owes competence to their family, their neighbors and the republic is its common expression. Firearms safety is taught in the civics curriculum of every Susian middle and high school on the same reasoning.

## Contemporary Relevance

During the [[Ant wars]], Versij's argument that undefended rights are privileges supplied ideological cover for armed political organization across the political spectrum. The [[Knights of the Republic]] fought syndicalist street organizations and, as [[Filevir Matri|Matri's]] crackdown widened beyond syndicalist targets, the state itself, which they read as misusing its power against the constitutional order they existed to defend. The [[Supreme Court of Susia|Supreme Court]] ruling that dissolved the [[Eossaj|eossajs]] was contested by the Knights and the [[Pačese ro Virspruje]] as a misreading of Versij's citizen-defender framework.

Among [[Tekur Susians]], the right is read through the [[March Against the Sun]], whose victims had been left without an armed force of their own before the deportation.
