**Sacoitism** is a religion originating on the continent of [[Gaiyan]], centered on the worship of [[Sacoites]], whom its adherents regard as the supreme being and creator of the universe. Its core doctrine holds that consciousness is neither created nor destroyed but transformed continuously through the [[Cycle of Souls]], a process overseen by Sacoites and administered by four subordinate divinities. Sacoitism served as the state religion of the [[Kingdom of Duloc]] and provided the theological foundation for its theocratic monarchy. It is the parent tradition from which [[Armotism]] emerged following the [[Armotist Revolution]].

### Origins

The origins of Sacoitism are traced to the prophet [[Olod]], a commoner from the [[Kingdom of Duloc]] on the continent of [[Gaiyan]]. According to the [[Ladan]], Sacoitism's primary scripture, Olod received a vision in which [[Sacoites]] transported him to [[Zion]], the divine realm, and charged him with spreading Sacoites' word throughout Duloc and restoring the kingdom to righteousness. Olod descended from Zion and transcribed the laws and teachings he had received, establishing the doctrinal foundation of the religion.

The Ladan does not record a precise date for Olod's vision. Sacoitism subsequently became the dominant religion of Duloc and the theological basis for its system of government, in which the monarch ruled as Sacoites' representative on the world. 
### Scripture

> _Main article: [[Ladan]]_

The primary scripture of Sacoitism is the [[Ladan]], a text divided into four books: the [[Caput ro Sacoites]], the [[Caput ro Olod]], the [[Caput ro Armotos]], and the [[Caput ro Zion]].

The Caput ro Sacoites contains the foundational theological claims of the religion, describing Sacoites as the creator and sovereign of the universe and establishing the terms of the covenant between Sacoites and his worshippers. The Caput ro Olod records Olod's vision, his charge from Sacoites, and the laws and teachings he received in Zion. Both books are treated as canonical and literally true across all Sacoitist traditions.

The Caput ro Armotos, which records the life and teachings of [[Armotos]] and culminates in his sacrifice and confrontation with Sacoites in Zion, is not recognized as canonical within Sacoitism. The Caput ro Zion, which describes the end of the world, is accepted as canonical in its Sacoitist recension, in which Sacoites governs the final judgment. Sacoitism does not recognize the Armotist recension of the Caput ro Zion, in which that role is attributed to Armotos.

The relationship between the Sacoitist and Armotist versions of the Ladan is a point of ongoing doctrinal dispute between the two traditions. Sacoitism holds that the Armotist books represent a corruption of the original scripture rather than a legitimate continuation of it.
### Theology

#### Sacoites

Sacoitism holds that [[Sacoites]] is the supreme being and creator of the universe. All life originates either directly from Sacoites or from beings created by him. Sacoites is described in the [[Ladan]] as the sovereign of both the material world and [[Zion]], the divine realm, and as the ultimate authority over the [[Cycle of Souls]].

Sacoitist theology does not treat Sacoites as distant or uninvolved. The Caput ro Olod records direct communication between Sacoites and a mortal, and the tradition holds that Sacoites maintains active governance over the cycle and over the four divinities who administer it.

#### The Cycle of Souls

> _Main article: [[Cycle of Souls]]_

The central doctrinal claim of Sacoitism is that consciousness is neither created nor destroyed but transformed continuously. At death, every soul enters a process administered by four divinities and overseen by Sacoites, passing through judgment and either returning to the material world or remaining in Zion in states of service or rest. The cycle has no end point for individual souls except through the final judgment described in the [[Caput ro Zion]].

#### The four divinities

Sacoitism recognizes four divinities subordinate to Sacoites, each responsible for a distinct function within the Cycle of Souls.

**[[Teósio]]** receives the soul at death. He explains the structure of the cycle, integrates the soul's memories across all its previous lives, and guides it to the [[Realm of Mirrors]].

**[[Vartelos]]** presides over the Realm of Mirrors and conducts the judgment of each soul. Vartelos is held to be omniscient, having witnessed every life the soul has lived. Each soul must account for its actions before Vartelos, who then assigns one of three paths.

**[[Verene]]** guides souls who have completed their time in Zion back to the material world for rebirth.

**[[Kalti]]** administers purification for souls judged to require it. The process is proportional to the soul's transgressions and may extend across years, centuries, or longer. Purification under Kalti is finite: all souls who enter it eventually complete it and become eligible for return to the material world.

#### The three paths

Following judgment by Vartelos, each soul is assigned one of three destinations.

Souls of sufficient purity are offered the path of **Service**, assisting in the administration of the [[Garden of Latice]], the verdant resting place in Zion, as guides, guardians, and teachers to souls at rest. The most senior souls in Service may be admitted to the [[Celestial Council]]. The offer of Service is extended only to those souls Vartelos determines will accept it.

Souls whose lives were incomplete or whose accounts are settled may pass to **Rest** in the Latice, remaining there for as long as they require before choosing to return to the material world with Verene's guidance.

Souls judged to have acted against Sacoites and his laws are assigned to **Purification** under Kalti before they become eligible for return.

### History

#### The Kingdom of Duloc

Sacoitism served as the state religion of the [[Kingdom of Duloc]] on the continent of [[Gaiyan]]. The Duloquese monarchy was organized as a theocracy in which the king governed as the earthly representative of [[Sacoites]], deriving political legitimacy directly from that claim. The [[Knights of Olod]], a military order named after the founding prophet, served as the kingdom's primary armed force and as enforcers of religious orthodoxy.

The institutional structure of Duloc treated religious and political authority as inseparable. #### The Armotist Revolution

> _Main article: [[Armotist Revolution]]_

The [[Armotist Revolution]] began in the village of [[Nizeli]] in Duloc, when a philosopher named [[Armotos]] publicly challenged the existence of Sacoites and was expelled from the village church. Armotos subsequently claimed to have received a vision in which Sacoites transported him to Zion, an experience that led him not to belief in Sacoites' legitimacy but to the conclusion that Sacoites, while real, had no rightful claim to worship or political authority.

Armotos fled Duloc following the announcement of a new king whose selection he opposed, taking refuge in [[Racpalia]], where he used the recently invented printing press to disseminate his critiques of Sacoitism and the Duloquese monarchy. His writings spread through the eastern kingdoms of Gaiyan before reaching Duloc, where they were banned and possession was punishable by death.

With the support of Racpalia's government, Armotos led a armed revolt against Duloc. The revolution was suppressed by the Knights of Olod. Armotos performed a sacrificial ritual and is held by Armotist tradition to have fought Sacoites in Zion, winning safe passage for his surviving followers to the continent of [[Dripstan]]. Sacoitism does not recognize this account.
