Military service in [[Susia]] takes two forms: mandatory national service, which applies to all citizens, and voluntary career service within the professional armed forces and the corporate defense sector. The mandatory service requirement has roots in the early [[Dripstanian Empire|imperial]] era and was formally reinstated after the founding of the republic in 1959. It is understood in Susian civic culture as both a practical security measure and an expression of the Versijian principle that rights must be backed by the capacity to defend them.

## History
### Imperial Origins

The institutionalization of military service in Susia traces to [[Empress Veronique]]'s [[Army Structuring Act]], enacted in the eighteenth century following the [[Babalist Revolt]]. Prior to the act, the empire raised troops through the captaincy system, mobilizing forces through feudal obligation to local lords. The eight months required to assemble an army of 50,000 men against the Babalists exposed the inadequacy of this arrangement. Veronique abolished it, centralizing the [[Sorzenko|Sorzenkos]] under imperial authority and creating a permanent professional army. The act also introduced civil examinations for officer promotion, establishing a meritocratic element within the military for the first time.

Throughout the imperial era, military service remained a feature of Susian life, particularly in the eastern frontier states where the [[Cericeiro]] culture treated martial competence as a basic expectation of adulthood. The Sorzenko system and associated local militias provided a civilian parallel to the professional army, ensuring that armed capacity was distributed across the population rather than concentrated solely in imperial institutions.

### The Liberal Revolts and Republican Memory

> _Main article: [[Liberal Revolts]]_

The political meaning of civilian military capacity became explicit during [[Jartes II]]'s reign. His suppression of Sorzenkos and restriction of civilian firearms ownership preceded his broader crackdown on liberal movements, and the failure of the [[Federated Provinces of Galil]] in 1844 was widely attributed in subsequent Susian memory to the effectiveness of that disarmament. The [[Susian Constitution]] enshrined the [[Right to Rebel|right to rebel]] in direct response, treating organized civilian military capacity as a structural safeguard against future tyranny. [[Armadesh Versij|Versij's]] argument that rights which cannot be defended are privileges waiting to be revoked became the philosophical foundation for this position.

### Reinstatement Under the Republic

[[Sergio Fimoises]] reinstated mandatory military service as one of the founding institutions of the [[Susia|Federative Republic of Susia]] in 1959, alongside the elected [[Susian Senate|Senate]] and the constitutional protection of natural rights. The requirement was understood as both a practical response to the [[Continental Divide]] and a civic statement: service was a duty owed to the republic, not merely a contribution extracted from its citizens.

## Mandatory National Service

All Susian citizens are required to complete mandatory national service upon reaching adulthood. The structure offers two tracks.

### Combat Track

The combat track requires one year of service in a military role. Service members receive training in firearms competence, physical fitness, tactics, discipline, and disaster response. Conscripts are assigned to units drawn from across Susia, placing people of different regional backgrounds, economic classes, and [[Free Economic Zone|FEZ]] or non-FEZ origins in shared conditions. This deliberate mixing is considered one of the service requirement's more significant civic functions: it produces bonds and mutual obligations across social divisions that few other institutions create.

### Non-Combat Track

Citizens may alternatively choose to serve two years in a non-combat role. Non-combat service encompasses logistics, medical support, infrastructure, communications, civil defense, and disaster response operations. The longer duration compensates for the reduced direct military contribution.

### Skills and Civic Function

Regardless of track, service produces a population with baseline competence in firearms handling, physical fitness, organizational discipline, and emergency response. This distributed competence is what Susian constitutional scholars describe as making the [[Right to Rebel|right of rebellion]] practically credible: organized civilian militia capacity exists at the level of the general population, not merely as a constitutional provision.

The cross-class character of service is noted consistently in political commentary. A factory worker from [[Sužielaj]] and a [[Troli Ustaras|TU]] FEZ resident from [[Dripia]] are assigned to the same unit, eat the same food, and depend on each other in the same conditions. The [[Sorzenko]] system and the [[Hăjaven]] festival produce similar bonds at the local level; mandatory service extends them nationally.

[[Tekur]] Susians enlist at rates above the national average, a pattern attributed to both strong civic engagement traditions within the community and the particular resonance of armed civic participation for a population that experienced formal statelessness until the [[Reconciliation Act]] of 1997.

## The National Guard

After completing mandatory service, citizens may join the [[National Guard]] of their state. The National Guard functions as a reserve force with dual activation authority: state governors may call it up for natural disasters and public disorder, and the federal government may activate it for foreign missions. Guards members maintain their civilian occupations in peacetime and receive periodic training to sustain readiness.

The overlap between National Guard membership and [[Sorzenko]] membership is common, particularly in rural areas and the eastern states. The two institutions reinforce each other: Sorzenko culture sustains the firearms competence and civic mindset that Guard membership requires, and Guard service deepens the mutual obligations that Sorzenkos formalize.

## Voluntary Professional Service

Beyond mandatory service, Susia maintains a professional volunteer military for full career service. The professional military operates under the [[Secretary of Defense]] as part of the federal executive structure.

### Frumentarze

> _Main article: [[Frumentarze]]_

The [[Frumentarze]], Susia's federal intelligence agency, recruits primarily from the military, law enforcement, and corporate security sectors. It is not a military body but draws heavily on veteran personnel, particularly for its Counter-Intelligence and Foreign Operations divisions. The Counter-Intelligence division is the largest, focused on Ashgeradn spy networks in Susia, FEZ corporate espionage, protecting technology from defection, and coordinating with [[Van Ritter]] on military secrets.

## Cognitive Enhancement and Military Service

Enhancement in the context of mandatory service is more limited. Conscripts serve for one to two years, too short a period to make enhancement economically rational for most, and the 25-year minimum age requirement under the Cognitive Rights Act means that most conscripts complete their service before they are eligible for procedures in any case. The issue is more acute for career service members and [[Van Ritter]] employees, where enhanced colleagues and performance metrics create structural pressure on unenhanced individuals.
