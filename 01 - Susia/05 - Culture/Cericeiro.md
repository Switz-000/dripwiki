**Cericeiros** were the frontier horsemen who led and populated the eastward expansion of the [[Dripstanian Empire]] from the established western coastal settlements into the interior territories that now comprise the states of [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]]. Operating beyond the boundaries of settled imperial administration, Cericeiros explored, mapped, and established the first permanent communities across the eastern plains during the imperial era. The figure of the Cericeiro, skilled, independent, and operating at the edge of civilization, became one of the most persistent archetypes in Susian popular culture and remains a significant marker of regional identity in the eastern states.

## Historical role

### Eastward expansion

The western coastal settlements of the early empire were established in the decades following the first Armotist landing at [[Forchev]] in 1654. Expansion into the interior was driven primarily by individuals and small groups operating ahead of formal imperial administration rather than by organized state campaigns. Cericeiros were the primary agents of this expansion: skilled horsemen who traveled beyond the frontier to explore, hunt, trade, and eventually settle new territory.

The eastern plains presented both opportunity and sustained danger. [[Armotism|Armotist]] settlers faced persistent conflict with indigenous populations throughout the settlement and early imperial periods, and Cericeiros operated in conditions of constant exposure to that conflict. The expansion they carried out was understood within the [[Fortress Complex]] logic of the era as preemptive defense: pushing the frontier eastward was framed as securing the settlements rather than threatening indigenous peoples. The actual process involved prolonged violence and ultimately the displacement and destruction of indigenous communities across the eastern territories.

The Tekur people were among those most directly affected. [[Emperor Jartes I]] ordered the forced deportation of approximately 350,000 Tekurs to the designated "Tekur Territory" in what is now [[New Celiolaj]], a march during which historical records indicate roughly 60% of the deportees died.

### Spread of civic institutions

Cericeiros carried [[Sorzenko]] culture with them as they expanded eastward. The shooting clubs that had developed among the coastal settlements as communal defense institutions took root across the eastern states through Cericeiro networks, embedding the Sorzenko tradition in regions where it remains strongest today. The [[Hăjaven]] festival, organized through Sorzenkos, spread eastward through the same process.

## Character and reputation

The Cericeiro figure in Susian cultural memory is defined by a specific cluster of traits: physical skill on horseback, willingness to operate in isolation and danger, practical self-sufficiency, and a degree of independence from institutional authority that was frequently described in contemporary sources as unruliness. Cericeiros were regarded by settled imperial society with a mixture of respect and wariness. Their role was indispensable; their disregard for formal structures was a recurring source of administrative friction.

This ambivalence persists in how the figure is understood today. In the eastern states, Cericeiro identity is a point of pride associated with toughness, self-reliance, and a certain suspicion of centralized authority, values that map onto those states' tendency toward the [[Coalition of State's Rights]] in contemporary politics. Outside those states, the Cericeiro archetype is recognized but carries less personal weight, appreciated as history rather than lived identity.

## Legacy and contemporary presence

### Eastern state identity

The states of [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]] all identify Cericeiro heritage as a defining element of their regional character. Cericeiro monuments are prominent in [[Yatovarlaj]], the city named after the nickname of a famous Cericeiro known as "[[Yatovar (Cericeiro)|Yatovar]]" meaning "Wanderer of the Plains." The city's patriotic character is closely tied to this heritage.

[[Cericeiro Heritage Week]] is a state holiday observed in [[Troli]], [[Misocévia]], and [[Sužielaj]], honoring the frontier settlers who explored and established eastern Susia.

### In literature and popular culture

Cericeiros are constantly referenced in Susian literature. They appear across popular culture as figures of adventure, independence, and a specifically eastern Susian masculine identity.

The [[Donetruže Bisons]] crolball team in [[Troli]] are explicitly associated with Cericeiro heritage in the naming and identity of the club.

Traditional Cericeiro foods survive in the regional cuisine of [[Misocévia]]: badger stew, described as a traditional preparation that is rarely eaten today due to its difficult cooking requirements and challenging flavor profile, and Yatovar's trail mix, a preserved food of nuts, dried goods, and jerky attributed to the legendary Cericeiro Yatovar and still sold and consumed in the region.

### Relationship to gun culture

The Cericeiro tradition is one of the primary channels through which [[Gun Culture in Susia|Susian gun culture]] reached the eastern states. Cericeiros carried firearms as a practical necessity, organized [[Sorzenko|Sorzenkos]] as they settled new territory, and established the armed frontier culture that persists in the eastern states' higher rates of open carry, Sorzenko membership, and military enlistment relative to the national average.
