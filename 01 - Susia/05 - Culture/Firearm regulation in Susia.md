In [[Susia]], ownership and both open and concealed carry are protected by the [[Susian Constitution]], and the governing constitutional doctrine treats regulation as permissible only where it preserves meaningful exercise of the right rather than amounting to prohibition. Within that constraint, Susian law distinguishes between firearms an individual may own directly and heavier weapons held under additional licensing or through a [[Sorzenko]], producing a tiered system that is the most permissive on the continent.

## Constitutional basis

> _Main article: [[Gun Culture in Susia]]_

The constitutional protection of firearms ownership originates in the [[Liberal Revolts]] of 1840 to 1844 and [[Jartes II]]'s disarmament of the population, which Susian political memory treats as the precondition of his tyranny. The constitution recognizes ownership and carry as protected and pairs them with an explicit [[Right to rebel|right of rebellion]]. The dominant doctrine distinguishes regulation that conditions the exercise of the right, which is permitted, from regulation that nullifies it in practice, which is not. Registration of individual owners and retention of purchase records are generally regarded as falling on the prohibited side of that line, on the reasoning that a register of owners is a precondition of disarmament.

Federal law sets a floor that states may add to but not fall below. States and [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]] may designate specific buildings as gun-free zones, such as schools and government facilities, but may not prohibit ownership or carry across a whole territory.

## Classification

Susian firearms law distinguishes three categories, defined by who may hold the weapon and under what conditions rather than by a single technical standard.

### Personal firearms

Personal firearms are those an individual may acquire and own directly. They may be purchased at licensed retailers subject to a background check, and a citizen who has not completed [[Military Service in Susia|mandatory military service]] must complete a basic training and safety requirement before purchase; the requirement is waived for those who have served, as service includes firearms competence training. Personal firearms may be kept at home and carried openly or concealed in any location not specifically designated a gun-free zone. This category covers the majority of firearms in civilian hands.

### Licensed automatic and heavy firearms

Automatic weapons and other firearms above the personal threshold are lawful to own but require either an individual license or membership in a licensed Sorzenko. The individual license route applies heightened scrutiny, including a more rigorous background check and periodic renewal, and is the more demanding of the two paths because it lacks the collective accountability that membership in a Sorzenko provides. The asymmetry is deliberate: the route through a civic institution is less onerous than the route available to an individual acting alone.

### Sorzenko firearms

> _Main article: [[Sorzenko]]_

A [[Sorzenko]] may hold firearms as the property of the institution and assign them to vetted members. Lighter weapons in this category may be assigned to members to keep at home. Heavier and fully automatic weapons are held in a Sorzenko armory rather than in private homes, and are checked out for drills, training, [[Hăjaven]], or activation. Weapons in this category are never individually owned; the Sorzenko bears civil and potentially criminal liability for their misuse, which functions as the system's principal accountability mechanism in place of individual registration.

## Sorzenko armories

Any group may form a Sorzenko, but holding an armory requires a separate license issued under state law and granted only after the Sorzenko meets defined prerequisites. These include a minimum operating history as an active club, a designated and licensed custodian who bears personal responsibility for the armory in addition to the Sorzenko's collective liability, physical security standards for the storage of weapons, and in most states a minimum membership threshold. The custodian requirement is central to the system, converting the Sorzenko's diffuse institutional liability into the responsibility of a named individual.

Armory licensing is the principal point at which the otherwise self-governing Sorzenko comes into contact with the state, and the rigor of that contact varies considerably by region. The eastern states, where Sorzenko culture is strongest, tend to treat the license as a formality with minimal ongoing oversight. The western states, particularly [[Dripia]] and [[Postia]], more commonly conduct annual inspections and treat the license as a revocable privilege. The same legal category therefore describes substantially different institutions depending on the state in which the armory operates.

## Smart firearms

> _Main article: [[Firearm regulation in Susia]]_

The introduction of networked and biometrically locked firearms following the [[The Great Transition|Great Transition]] of 2038 produced a continuing political dispute. Proponents present the technology as a safety measure, since a weapon locked to its owner is inert if stolen. Opponents, drawing on the constitutional logic of the right of rebellion, argue that a weapon which can be disabled by an external party is not fully owned and that its mandatory adoption would constitute a new form of disarmament. The resulting settlement permits the technology but does not mandate it: states may not require it, and some [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]] require it within their territory as a condition of the workplace or property rather than as a firearms law. Whether a FEZ mandate amounts to an effective prohibition for residents of that zone is an unresolved constitutional question.

## Scale

Susia is the most heavily armed society in the [[World]], a condition that follows directly from the combination of constitutional protection, near-universal completion of mandatory service, and the provision allowing citizens to retain their service weapons. Civilian firearms ownership is estimated at roughly 1.8 firearms per capita, well above the level recorded in any comparable society, with additional military-pattern materiel held in Sorzenko armories that is not reflected in per-capita figures because it is collectively rather than individually owned. The distribution is uneven: ownership rates are highest in the eastern and rural states and in communities such as the [[Tekur]], and lowest in the urban west and within [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]].

The scale is understood in Susian strategic doctrine as a defensive asset rather than only a civil liberty. An armed and trained population organized through Sorzenkos constitutes a distributed reserve that, in the framing of the [[Fortress complex]], makes occupation of Susian territory impractical. The same distributed capacity underwrites the right of rebellion against the Susian state itself.

## Data collection

> _Main article: [[Susian census]]_

The [[Susian census]] devotes a substantial section to firearms, recording the number and type of weapons owned, carry practices, Sorzenko affiliation, and the respondent's service record. The data is collected in aggregate and protected by a statutory firewall that prohibits linking individual responses to identity or sharing them with law enforcement, which is what reconciles the collection with the constitutional bar on a register of owners.
