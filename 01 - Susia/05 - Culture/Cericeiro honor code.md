The **Cericeiro honor code** is an informal ethos of personal conduct originating among the [[Cericeiro|Cericeiros]] of the eastern Susian frontier, organized around the principle that respect is earned through demonstrated restraint rather than demonstrated force. It developed as a frontier code of bearing for armed people and persists as the cultural substrate of gun culture in the eastern states. It is distinct from, though compatible with, the civic-armament philosophy of [[Armadesh Versij]]: Versijian thought supplied the political argument for an armed citizenry, while the Cericeiro code governed how an armed individual was expected to carry themselves.

## Origins

The code developed during the settlement and imperial periods among the Cericeiros who pushed the frontier eastward through what are now [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]]. On the frontier, competence with firearms was treated as a basic expectation of adulthood, and the conventions governing how that competence was displayed and restrained passed down through generations as custom rather than written law.

The code's obligations operated within a defined community. Its courtesies extended to other Cericeiros and to those under their protection. Indigenous populations, with whom the settlers were in sustained armed conflict during the frontier expansion, fell outside it. The broader history and outcome of that expansion are treated in [[Fortress complex]] and [[Cericeiro]].

## Tenets

The central principle of the code is that respect is established by restraint, summarized in the traditional saying, "Carry a revolver, be polite, and you will go far." Capacity for force is expected to be visible so that the choice not to use it is legible to others. An individual who is openly capable of violence and nonetheless conducts themselves with courtesy demonstrates mastery; courtesy without visible capacity carries no comparable weight.

From this principle follow several conventions. Discourtesy while armed is treated as the central failing, both a breach of the code and a practical danger in a society where most adults are equally equipped. The code attaches a protective obligation to capacity: an armed individual who fails to intervene on behalf of someone weaker is understood to have failed the code rather than merely declined to act. Bearing is valued, specifically a calm and unhurried manner understood to reflect a person with nothing left to prove.

The code is enforced through two opposing social pressures. An individual who does not carry stands outside the system of mutual respect and cannot fulfill the obligation to intervene. An individual who carries and behaves aggressively faces direct and potentially lethal consequence from an equally armed community. The equilibrium produced by these pressures is a population that is both heavily armed and consistently courteous.

## Weapons and carry

The revolver is the customary sidearm of the code and functions as a visible credential of capacity. In the eastern states it is worn openly, and its open display is read as a statement of capability paired with restraint rather than as a threat.

In the contemporary period the revolver has acquired a secondary meaning. As networked and biometrically locked firearms entered the Susian market after the [[The Great Transition|Great Transition]], the mechanically simple revolver, which cannot be disabled remotely, became associated with the position that a weapon subject to external control is not fully owned. The distinction between the revolver as inherited custom and the revolver as deliberate statement is read by Susians from how it is worn and what it is paired with.

## Dueling and status

> _Main article: [[Dueling in Susia]]_

Formal dueling in Susia was primarily a western institution of the settled gentry and officer class during the [[high-imperial|high-imperial period]], conducted through fixed procedure between social equals. It was distinct from the Cericeiro code, which favored direct and proportionate resolution over ritual and regarded the formal duel as an affectation. The professionalized imperial officer corps, drawing recruits from across the regions, was the principal setting in which the two traditions met.

The career of [[Kaichet Satratonie]] illustrates the distinction. Born in [[Lore-Kurji]] in [[Misocévia]], Satratonie rose through the ranks by merit rather than birth and sought standing within a western aristocratic order that did not regard him as an equal. During the [[Liberal Revolts]] he repeatedly sought to duel the admiral [[Zagoste Paroska]], who came from a prestigious family and who had conducted an affair with the [[Countess of Jafnidia]], a woman Satratonie wished to marry. Satratonie believed that wealth, status, and the death of his rival would secure the marriage, and on that expectation he accepted [[Jartes II]]'s offer of land, money, and a pardon in exchange for changing sides.

The western refusal to accept Satratonie as an equal rested on his perceived absence of honor rather than on his origin. By the standards of the Cericeiro code he had committed its central breach, using capacity for personal gain and abandoning those alongside whom he was obligated to stand. By the standards of the western tradition he lacked the breeding the duel presupposed. Having left one code and failed to enter the other, he is recalled as a figure who satisfied neither.

## Criticism

Like other honor cultures, the code generates costs alongside its stabilizing effects. A system in which standing depends on public respect produces feuds and situations in which yielding becomes impossible without loss of face, so that disputes that the code's preference for restraint might otherwise contain can instead escalate. The same social pressure that produces courtesy also makes a reputation for coldness or evasiveness costly, with the result that the warmth characteristic of the eastern states carries an element of obligation.

## Contemporary relevance

The code persists most visibly in the [[Gun Culture in Susia|gun culture]] of the eastern states, where open carry, [[Sorzenko]] membership, and military enlistment exceed the national average. Its influence is also read in regional differences in social manner. In the east, where capacity is displayed openly, public conduct tends toward an open and secure friendliness. In the western states, where firearms are typically carried concealed and a given individual's capacity is unknown, public conduct tends toward greater reserve and caution. Both patterns coexist with comparably low levels of actual violence.

The figure of Satratonie survives in the idiom "pulling a Satratonie," used to describe the betrayal of one's principles for money or status. The idiom captures the public account of his defection while omitting the personal motive recorded in the historical material, an instance of the way national memory reduces a complex figure to a single usable meaning.
