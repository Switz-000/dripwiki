**Tekur Susians** are citizens of [[Susia]] of [[Tekur]] descent. They are concentrated in [[New Celiolaj]], the former Tekur Territory to which the population of the [[Kingdom of Tekuria]] was deported in the [[March Against the Sun]], and were excluded from Susian citizenship from the republic's founding in 1954 until the [[Reconciliation Act]] of 1997. Tekur Susians have higher rates of [[Sorzenko]] membership, firearms ownership and military enlistment than the national average.

## History

### Imperial era

> *Main article: [[March Against the Sun]]*

Tekur presence in the territory of modern Susia predates the [[Armotism|Armotist]] settlement of 1651. The eastward expansion of the [[Dripstanian Empire]], led by the [[Cericeiro|Cericeiros]], displaced and in large part destroyed the indigenous populations of the interior, the Tekurs among them.

In 1794 [[Empress Yaneoli]] united the western Tekur tribes as the vassal Kingdom of Tekuria, whose armed forces were an imperial army. The kingdom declared for [[Jartes I]] in the [[Dripstanian Civil War]]. After his victory Jartes absorbed its forces into the imperial army and deported its Tekur population to the Tekur Territory. The majority of the deportees died on the march.

### Exclusion

At the founding of the republic in 1954, [[Suizo Soites]] excluded Tekurs from Susian citizenship because the northern states would not otherwise have joined, a decision he privately acknowledged as moral cowardice. Tekurs remained formally stateless for the following 43 years. Discrimination against them in the republic was legal and economic: they held no citizenship, and the Tekur population was economically marginalized.

### Citizenship

The Reconciliation Act granted Tekurs full citizenship in 1997. [[Reconciliation Day]], a federal public holiday, marks the grant and acknowledges historical injustice. Since 1997 the political question has shifted from legal status to the pace and depth of economic integration.

## Demographics

Tekurs make up approximately 40% of the population of New Celiolaj.

Most Tekur Susians are Armotists of various denominations. Around 16% practice a form of Armotism that incorporates Tekur practices, spiritual concepts and ceremonies, and around 2% practice traditional Tekur polytheism.

## Civic participation

Tekur Susians have higher rates of Sorzenko membership, firearms ownership and enlistment in [[Military Service in Susia|military service]] than the national average. [[Firearm regulation in Susia|Firearms ownership]] is highest in the eastern and rural states and in Tekur communities, and lowest in the urban west and in [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]].

### Explanations

Historians debate two explanations for the pattern. Most accounts hold that both apply, and the dispute concerns their relative weight.

The first treats Tekur participation as an expression of Susian identity. One version reads it as demonstration: a population admitted to citizenship late, showing its membership through the civic practices by which Susians recognize one another. The other version reads it as belonging rather than display. On this account Tekur communities are more attached to Susian history and its civic institutions than urban western and FEZ populations, and participate as a matter of course rather than as a statement.

The second traces the pattern to the March Against the Sun. The population of the Kingdom of Tekuria was deported after its armed forces had been absorbed into the imperial army, and Tekur political culture draws from this the conclusion [[Armadesh Versij]] set out in [[Architecture of Freedom]]: rights not backed by the capacity to defend them are privileges contingent on the goodwill of whoever holds power. On this account Sorzenko membership, firearms ownership and service are the practical form of that conclusion.

## Political culture

Tekur political culture is more Versijian than that of Susia as a whole. Versij is held in high regard among Tekur Susians, and his argument is treated as correct in general and as demonstrated by the Tekur case in particular.

The Tekur reading of the deportation and the exclusion follows Versij's position that rights belong to individuals rather than to groups. Both are treated as violations of individual rights by a state, the empire in one case and the 1954 settlement in the other, and not as crimes of the Susian nation against the Tekur people. The distinction between nation and state on which this reading rests is general in Susian political thought and underlies the civic reading of the [[Right to rebel|right to rebel]] as a duty. Tekur political discourse does not generally describe Susia as a colonial power, and [[List of political positions#Tekur Separatism|Tekur separatism]] remains a minority position without significant national representation.

Tekur Susians do not regard their history as in tension with their patriotism.

### Electoral politics

In the 2080s Tekur voters support the [[Coalition of State's Rights]] at higher rates than the national electorate. The lean is a tendency rather than a bloc: Tekur voters are distributed across all three federal groupings, and Tekur identity is not a primary axis of Susian electoral politics, which is organized around ideology and state interest rather than ethnicity.

## Culture

[[Tekur Heritage Month]] is observed in New Celiolaj. Frozen pine tea, a traditional Tekur drink brewed from pine needles, is listed among [[List of susian foods and drinks|Susian foods and drinks]].
