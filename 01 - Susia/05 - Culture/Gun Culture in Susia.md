Firearms ownership and competence are deeply embedded in Susian civic identity. The tradition traces its roots to the practical demands of early colonial settlement, was codified as political philosophy by [[Armadesh Versij]], and was cemented as national doctrine by [[Jartes II]]'s disarmament of the population before the [[Liberal Revolts]]. Both open carry and concealed carry are constitutionally protected at the federal level. Gun safety is a required component of the civics curriculum in all Susian middle and high schools.

## Origins

The earliest Armotist settlers arrived on a hostile continent and faced persistent conflict with indigenous peoples throughout the settlement and imperial eras. Firearms were a practical necessity, and communities organized around [[Sorzenko|Sorzenkos]], shooting clubs that provided firearms training and formed the backbone of local defense militias. The Sorzenko system spread eastward alongside the [[Cericeiro]] frontier settlers who pushed into what are now the states of [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]]. Cericeiros, skilled horsemen known for operating far beyond established settlements, carried the gun culture into the interior and embedded it in the folklore and literature of the eastern states.

The [[Hăjaven]] festival, celebrated at the start of winter, originated within this Sorzenko militia tradition. In its traditional form, it involves a communal hunt followed by a shared meal with neighbors and townspeople. It remains a federal public holiday observed nationally, though its character varies considerably between rural and urban areas.

## The Liberal Revolts and Constitutional Foundation

> _Main article: [[Liberal Revolts]]_

The defining political moment for Susian gun culture came during [[Jartes II]]'s reign. In an attempt to suppress growing liberal opposition, Jartes II banned Sorzenkos and imposed restrictions on civilian firearms ownership before implementing a broader crackdown on liberal writers and political movements. [[Armadesh Versij]] was among those forced into exile. The armed resistance that followed, organized around surviving Sorzenkos and underground militias, ultimately failed when [[Kaichet Satratonie]] defected to the imperial side and the [[Federated Provinces of Galil]] collapsed in 1844. Versij and [[Zagoste Paroska]] were publicly executed.

The interpretation that became dominant in Susian political memory holds that the disarmament was a precondition of the tyranny, and that the revolt's defeat was made possible by it. This reading is disputed by some historians, who argue the republic faced structural military disadvantages that firearms alone would not have overcome. The popular position is captured in a quote attributed to Versij at his execution: "You will have me die for treason. So be it. Yet know this: had my fellows not been disarmed you would not now stand to pronounce it." Its authenticity is contested, but its cultural currency is not.

The [[Susian Constitution]] guarantees the right to bear arms and includes an explicit [[Right to rebel|right of rebellion]]: citizens may take up arms to resist a government that systematically violates the natural rights recognized by the constitution, including freedom of speech, the right to self-defense, the right to own and use property, and the right to life. This provision is understood as a direct response to the events of 1840 to 1844.

## Philosophical Roots

[[Armadesh Versij]]'s political philosophy provided the theoretical framework that elevated firearms ownership from a practical habit to a civic obligation. In his [[Architecture of Freedom]], Versij introduced the concept of the "citizen guards of the republic": the argument that rights not backed by the capacity to defend them are privileges contingent on the goodwill of whoever holds power. Ownership of a firearm is therefore not merely personal protection but an expression of civic standing and mutual obligation. A citizen owes competence to their family, their neighbors, and the republic.

A second quote in wide circulation, often attributed to Versij, reads: "A nation of citizen soldiers is mightier than any standing army." The attribution is incorrect. The line originates with [[Kaichet Satratonie|Satratonie]], a fact that Susians tend to find either ironic or embarrassing, given Satratonie's place in national memory as the archetype of betrayal.

## Legal Framework

Firearms ownership is constitutionally protected at the federal level for both open and concealed carry. States and [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]] may designate gun-free zones within specific buildings such as schools or government facilities, but they cannot prohibit ownership or carry entirely.

Susia maintains [[Military Service in Susia|mandatory military service]], reinstated at the start of the [[Continental Divide]]. All citizens are required to serve one year in combat roles or two years in non-combat roles. Service includes firearms competence training alongside instruction in discipline, physical fitness, and disaster response. After service, citizens may join their state's [[National Guard]], which can be activated for natural disasters, public disorder, or federal foreign missions. The combination of constitutional protections, mandatory service, and the Sorzenko system means that organized militia capacity exists at the civilian level, which Susian constitutional scholars regard as making the right of rebellion practically credible rather than merely symbolic.

## Regional Variation

Gun culture is not uniform across Susia. Open carry is more common in the eastern states, which retain stronger Cericeiro and Sorzenko traditions. Western states tend toward concealed carry and treat ownership as a more private matter. Urban residents across the country generally keep firearms at home rather than carrying them publicly, and treat the topic as personal rather than political.

[[Tekur]] Susians show notably higher rates of firearms ownership, Sorzenko membership, and military enlistment than the national average.

Within [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]], ownership is more socially discouraged. Corporate governance cultures, particularly at [[Troli Ustaras]] and [[Atompron]], treat civilian firearms as anachronistic, and some FEZs have promoted virtual shooting as an alternative. The attitude is generally regarded with skepticism outside FEZ populations.

## Civic Institutions

The [[Sorzenko]] system remains active, particularly in smaller towns, rural areas, and the eastern states. Sorzenkos function as civic institutions in a broader sense than firearms training: membership creates cross-class social bonds, and members are understood to bear mutual obligations of defense toward one another.

Gun safety is a mandatory component of the civics curriculum in all Susian middle and high schools. The civics course covers the structure and rationale of the Susian political system, the philosophy of Versij and [[Yário Kolkov]], and practical civic competencies including firearms safety. The inclusion of gun safety in the same curriculum as constitutional philosophy is deliberate and reflects the Versijian argument that civic knowledge and the capacity for self-defense are not separate subjects.
