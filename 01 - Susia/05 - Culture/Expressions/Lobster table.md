**Lobster table** is a Susian political expression referring to a meeting, setting, or pattern of decision-making in which consequential political or economic outcomes are determined outside legitimate democratic channels, typically through private meals or informal arrangements among executives and officials. The expression derives from the [[Duolij Lobster]], a prestige seafood dish native to [[Postia]] that functions as the standard centerpiece of corporate power lunches nationwide.

The phrase is used both descriptively and critically. Descriptively, it refers to any instance of significant political or economic business conducted through private social arrangements rather than formal institutional process. Critically, it implies that outcomes reached at the lobster table displace or preempt outcomes that should properly be reached through legislative or democratic means.

## Usage

The expression is one of several established Susian political sayings in common circulation. It appears in political commentary, journalism, and ordinary speech to characterize politicians, executives, or corporate-government relationships perceived as governing through personal network rather than transparent process.

[[Filevir Matri]] is frequently cited as the defining exemplar of the lobster table style in Susian political history. His preference for building coalitions through private meals and informal afternoon conversations, rather than through floor advocacy or public argument, was noted by contemporaries and became central to how his political career has been assessed.

The phrase acquires additional resonance in the context of [[Yarnojte]] corporate power, where the proximity of [[Free Economic Zone|FEZ]] executives to federal legislators, and the concentration of upscale restaurants within FEZ-adjacent areas, produces situations that critics describe as lobster-table governance.
