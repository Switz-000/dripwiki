**Hăjaven** is a Susian federal public holiday celebrated at the start of winter. In its traditional form it involves a communal hunt organized through the local [[Sorzenko]], followed by a shared meal at which the game is distributed to the wider community. In urban areas and households without Sorzenko affiliation, the observance takes the form of a special dinner shared with neighbors or family. It is among the oldest civic traditions in Susia, predating the [[Dripstanian Empire]], and remains one of the few federal holidays with unbroken roots in the settlement era.

## Origins

%% TODO: expand on the religious significance of Hăjaven within Armotism %%

Hăjaven originated among the earliest [[Armotism|Armotist]] settlers on the Dripstan continent. Sorzenko militias, formed to provide communal defense against conflict with indigenous peoples, organized seasonal hunts as a practical measure for supplementing food supplies before winter. The game taken during these hunts was shared communally with the broader town rather than kept by the hunters, establishing the redistributive character that defines the holiday. The shared meal at the end of the hunt was both a practical provision and a public expression of mutual obligation between the militia and the civilians it defended.

The festival spread eastward alongside the [[Cericeiro]] frontier settlers who pushed into the territories now comprising [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]], becoming embedded in the frontier culture of those regions. In the eastern states, where Sorzenko culture has always been strongest, Hăjaven retained its traditional form more completely than in the urbanizing west.

## Observance

### Rural and traditional

In rural communities and smaller towns, particularly in the eastern states, Hăjaven is organized through the local Sorzenko. Members conduct a communal hunt, and the game is prepared and served at a town-wide shared meal. The event functions as both a practical celebration and a renewal of the mutual obligations that Sorzenko membership entails. It remains the defining annual event of Sorzenko life in these communities.

### Urban and contemporary

In cities and among households without active Sorzenko membership, the hunt element is absent. Observance centers on a special dinner, typically shared with neighbors, extended family, or both. The communal character of the original tradition is retained in this form, though it is organized at the household or neighborhood level rather than through a civic institution. The specific foods served vary by region and household.

## Status as a federal holiday

Hăjaven is one of Susia's federal public holidays, a status that gives the tradition national recognition without incorporating it into any state structure. The Sorzenko system that organizes its traditional form receives no federal funding and operates as a network of private civic associations. The holiday's federalization is understood in Susian civic culture as an acknowledgment of the tradition's role in founding-era survival rather than as a state endorsement of the Sorzenko institution specifically.

## Contemporary relevance

Hăjaven is observed nationally across all states and [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]]. Within FEZ populations, where [[Sorzenko]] membership is uncommon and gun culture is informally discouraged, the holiday is typically observed through the household dinner form. Some FEZ corporate cultures have incorporated team meals or catered events into their observance, adapting the communal meal element to an institutional context.

The holiday's cross-class character, particularly in its traditional rural form, is noted in political commentary alongside the [[Sorzenko]] system and [[Military Service in Susia|mandatory military service]] as one of the mechanisms through which Susian civic life produces shared obligations across otherwise stratified social groups.
