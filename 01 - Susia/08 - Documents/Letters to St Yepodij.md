Dear Mom and Dad,

I am well and so is Titič, we are now in [[Hertpatt]], which means we made it to [[Bershad]]. We crossed from [[Yar-Firol]] yesterday morning. The border is one big electric fence, birds don't even dare sit on it. It's easy to tell which side you're on because only [[Bershad]] looks like a thousand men started desperately digging for diamonds. Hopefully Uncle Ňotrič will stop complaining about our roads now.

The border guard was nice, he spoke a little [[Susian language|susian]]. He told me to be careful, and said something about the [[Buta people|Buta]] that I couldn't make out, his accent was thick. I remember reading [[Ti-ka!]] back home reporting on how brutal the patrol can be. Yet, there weren't many people guarding the border and those who were seemed awfully bored.

I asked him about it, he said that the Bershadians don't try to cross the border as much, and when they do it's mostly at night. His friend was redeployed to the interior. Their president, [[Bejňar Keke]], is focused on reducing violence there. He then went on and on about how the [[Susia|susian]] media portray [[Yar-Firol]] unfairly and how we need to get rid of the [[Susian Liberal Party|PNS]], I didn't know if he meant metaphorically or literally, with these people, you can't be sure. 

When I told him that I wanted to cross into Bershad he was surpised, I remember him asking, a little nervously, if I was a tourist. I replied yes. I have no idea what happened, but something clicked in him? Maybe he thought I was working for the embassy? Some higher-up? He then went on about how "the shipments" haven't arrived yet, and how "they" wouldn't be able to support me.

After crossing into Bershad, I followed my friend Mtela's advice: walk alongside the road, never on it, never outside it. She told me that during Nguan's regime they scattered landmines and barbed wire all over the north to keep the people from escaping. I don't know why but I remembered Dad forbidding me from participating in my school's [[Battle of Neoveli]] reenactment in 8th grade, something about guns being dangerous and how children shouldn't be playing with them. But I can kind of understand why we do them, I might be misremembering it, Dad correct me if i'm getting it wrong.

I walked for about two hours southwest, the roads got much better and I was surprised by how well-preserved they were, from what I heard, the [[General Government of Incria|GGI]] used a technique where they mixed rubber with butane or something like that, making the roads last a lot longer. I made camp near a creek.

Uncle Ňotrič's advice to always beat the boots against the ground before wearing them saved me from a nasty bite yesterday, the little visitor tried running but Titič took care of him. I didn't have to deal with a swollen foot in the middle of [[Zaphonia]], in the middle of a war, unlike a certain someone. Or do you think he did it on purpose? To get discharged and head home sooner? Imagine choosing to almost lose your foot over staying a few more months guarding some villages. Please confirm it for me Mom, I can't stop laughing.

Now, i'm going to meet Mtela soon. If you are reading this, thank her, she has her way with things. Before you ask, no Mom, she's just a friend. If you want grandchildren so bad, start nagging Chedji instead, he's the older one, I still don't understand why do you think he wouldn't have any.

Is Dad's back doing better? If it was up to me I'd bring some [[Sorophine]] to him, it's cheaper than water around here, but I don't want to get killed buying it nor do I want to get jailed for bringing it, so paracetamol will have to do. Don't forget to send the reply to Mtela's office, not mine, she'll know where I am. I'll tell you about Bershadi rice tastes like when I'm home. Kiss Dad for me.

Your son, Matev.
