# Article I – Fundamental Rights and Freedoms

---

Section 1 – Inherent Rights

1. All persons within the Techno-Federative Republic of Susia possess, and this Constitution recognizes, the following natural and inalienable rights:
    

2. Life;
    
3. The right to self-defense;
    
4. The right to bear arms;
    
5. Freedom of speech and expression;
    
6. Freedom of association and assembly;
    
7. Freedom of movement;
    
8. Freedom of religion and conscience;
    
9. The right to privacy;
    
10. The right to acquire, own, use, and dispose of property freely.  
      
    

11. These rights shall not be infringed upon except by law or decree enacted in accordance with this Constitution and only in extraordinary circumstances clearly justified for the preservation of public order or national security. Any such decree must be presented to the Congress for approval within seven (7) days, and may be overruled by a majority vote of either chamber.
    

Section 2 – Equality Before the Law

1. All persons are equal before the law and are entitled to equal protection and benefit of the laws without discrimination.  
      
    
2. No individual or group shall receive special privileges nor be subject to any discrimination.
    

Section 3 – Limits on Government Power

1. The government of Susia shall not interfere arbitrarily in the private lives of all persons.  
      
    
2. The rights and freedoms recognized herein may only be restricted when necessary to protect the rights of others or the collective security of the nation, and such restrictions shall be proportional, lawful, and subject to judicial review.
    

Section 4 – Due Process and Privacy Protections

1. No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law, including the right to a fair and public trial before an impartial tribunal.  
      
    
2. The privacy of persons, their homes, property, papers and effects shall not be violated without a lawful warrant based on probable cause.  
      
    

Section 5 – Rights to Petition and Participation  
Every citizen shall have the right to petition the government for redress of grievances and to participate in public affairs consistent with the law.

Section 6 –  Right to Resist Tyranny  
The people retain the right to resist or oppose any government or official that acts contrary to the fundamental rights guaranteed by this Constitution.

Section 7 – Obligations of States

1. States are constitutionally obligated to provide their residents with access to:  
          a. Quality education;  
          b. Effective policing and public safety;  
          c. Sanitation and environmental health;  
          d. Healthcare services.  
      
    
2. Such services must be provided in a manner consistent with the protection of the fundamental rights guaranteed by this Constitution.

## Article II – Federal Structure

## 

---

Section 1 – Composition and Republican Form of States

1. The Federative Republic of Susia shall consist of States and a Federal District, each governed by a republican form of government.  
      
    
2. Each State shall have an elected legislature with at least one chamber directly representing its citizens.  
      
    
3. Citizens of each State shall be entitled to all privileges and immunities of citizens in every other State.  
      
    
4. States shall not have the right to secede from the Techno-Federative Republic of Susia.
    

Section 2 – Creation and Admission of States

1. New States may be admitted to the Republic with the approval of the President of Susia and a two-thirds (2/3) majority vote in both chambers of the Federal Congress.  
      
    
2. Existing States may create new States or alter their boundaries only with the consent of the Governor of the State, the State’s legislative chambers, and the approval of the President and Federal Congress.  
      
    
3. Territories may be transformed into States upon meeting the requirements established by Federal law and by the process defined in this Section.
    

Section 3 – Distribution of Powers  
The powers of government are divided into three categories: those reserved exclusively to the Federal Government, those reserved exclusively to the States, and powers shared concurrently by both.

Section 4 – Powers Exclusive to the Federal Government  
The Federal Government shall have the exclusive authority to:  
      a. Make treaties and conduct foreign relations;  
      b. Mint currency and regulate monetary policy;  
      c. Regulate foreign and interstate commerce;  
      d. Tax imports and exports;  
      e. Declare war and maintain the military forces;  
      f. Establish and maintain copyrights and patents.

Section 5 – Concurrent Powers  
Both the Federal Government and the States shall have authority to:  
      a. Collect taxes;  
      b. Make and enforce laws;  
      c. Borrow money;  
      d. Establish and administer the judiciary;  
      e. Adopt constitutions consistent with this Federal Constitution.

Section 6 – Powers Reserved to the States  
States shall retain all powers not expressly delegated to the Federal Government nor prohibited to the States by this Constitution, including but not limited to:  
      a. Establishing local governments;  
      b. Providing public education and healthcare services;  
      c. Conducting elections;  
      d. Ensuring the common good and welfare of their citizens.

Section 7 – Supremacy of Federal Law  
Federal laws, treaties, and regulations shall be the supreme law of the land and shall override any conflicting state laws except where this Constitution explicitly allows otherwise.

## Article III – The Presidency

## 

---

Section 1 – Term and Eligibility

1. The President of Susia shall serve a term of ten (10) years.  
      
    
2. A President may be re-elected one time, serving a maximum of twenty (20) years in office.  
      
    
3. Every literate citizen of Susia between the ages of twenty-five (25) and seventy (70) is eligible to vote for the President.
    

Section 2 – Election

1. The President shall be elected by direct popular vote.  
      
    
2. A candidate must receive a simple majority of votes to be elected. If no candidate receives a majority, the two candidates with the highest number of votes shall proceed to a runoff election.  
      
    
3. The candidate who receives the majority in the runoff shall be declared President.
    

Section 3 – Powers

1. The President is the chief executive of the Techno-Federative Republic of Susia, responsible for enforcing federal law and directing the administration of the government.  
      
    
2. The President may enact martial law in times of national emergency, consistent with the provisions and limitations.  
      
    
3. The President shall appoint, subject to confirmation by Congress or other established procedures, all heads of federal executive departments and key officials, except as otherwise provided by law or this Constitution.
    

Section 4 – Motion of Confidence and Removal

1. Every five (5) years, both chambers of Congress shall hold a motion of confidence in the President.  
      
    
2. Should a majority of members in both chambers vote no confidence, the President shall be removed from office and new presidential elections shall be held within a constitutionally defined period.
    
3. Congress may also call a motion of confidence at any time for any reason, provided a two-thirds (2/3) majority of both chambers votes in favor of such a motion, resulting in the removal of the President.  
      
    

Section 5 – Succession

1. In the event of the President’s death, incapacity, or removal, the Head of the Senate shall serve as Acting President until new presidential elections are held.  
      
    
2. Should the Head of the Senate be unable to serve, the Head of the Chamber of Specialists shall assume the role of Acting President until a new President is elected.

## Article IV – Congress

## 

---

Section 1 – Structure and Composition  
The Congress of Susia shall be bicameral, consisting of two chambers:

1. Senate  
          a. Each state and the Neutral District shall elect five Senators by popular vote;  
          b. Senators shall serve a term of five (5) years and may be re-elected once, for a maximum of two terms;  
          c. Senators must be at least twenty-five (25) years old and no older than seventy (70) at the time of election.  
      
    
2. Chamber of Experts  
          a. Members shall be elected by professional, academic, and technical. institutions through meritocratic examinations or qualifications.  
          b. Members may serve a term of three (3) years. A second non-consecutive term may be allowed after a five-year interval  
          c. Members must be at least thirty (30) years old and no older than seventy-five (75) at the time of election.  
      
    

  

Section 2 – Legislative Powers

1. No law shall be valid unless passed by both chambers.  
      
    
2. The Chamber of Specialists shall have the power to review, amend, and delay legislation, but shall not permanently block any bill.  
      
    
3. The Senate shall initiate all budget and spending bills.  
      
    
4. Both chambers shall share authority over treaties, impeachment proceedings, and governmental oversight.  
      
    
5. Congress shall confirm all high federal appointments except for those of the Secretariat of Efficiency, which remains independent.  
      
    

Section 3 – Legislative Process

1. Bills may be introduced in either chamber, except for budget and taxation bills, which must originate in the Senate.  
      
    
2. Upon approval by both chambers, bills shall be submitted to the President for approval or veto.  
      
    
3. In case of a presidential veto, Congress may override it by a two-thirds (2/3) majority vote in both chambers.  
      
    
4. A quorum for conducting business requires the presence of a majority of members in each chamber.  
      
    
5. Special committees may be established to review complex legislation and policies.  
      
    

Section 4 – Leadership and Sessions

1. Each chamber shall elect a President (Speaker) from among its members to preside over sessions and manage proceedings.  
      
    
2. In the absence of the President, the Deputy or most senior member shall preside.  
      
    
3. Congress shall convene in regular annual sessions and may call special or emergency sessions as necessary.

## Amendment VII – Federal Economic Zones (FEZs)

Section 1. Establishment  
Federal Economic Zones (FEZs) are semi-autonomous economic regions established jointly by the Secretariat of Efficiency and the state government in which the FEZ is to be located. No FEZ may be created, expanded, or dismantled without the consent of both the Secretariat and the state legislature.

Section 2. Eligibility and Assignment  
Only entities granted the official status of Yarnojte recognized corporations in strategic sectors of the national economy may be assigned FEZs.  
The Secretariat of Efficiency shall be solely responsible for assigning FEZs to Yarnojtes and for setting and evaluating growth quotas.

Section 3. Governance  
Each FEZ shall be governed by an appointed Company Administrator selected by the Yarnojte. The Company Administrator may establish local governance structures, draft a local constitution, and issue regulations, provided they do not violate the Constitution of Susia or the penal code of the respective state.

Section 4. Rights and Obligations  
All FEZs shall guarantee and uphold the natural and inalienable rights enumerated in Article I of this Constitution. Each FEZ shall provide, at minimum, the following services to all persons within its jurisdiction:

      a. Law enforcement and public safety;  
      b. Primary and secondary education;  
      c. Healthcare services;  
      d. Sanitation, water, and essential public utilities;  
      e. Housing accommodation.

 Yarnojtes may levy taxes within the FEZ to fund these services.

Section 5. Legal Compliance  
All FEZs are subject to the penal code of their respective state and must comply with the Constitution of Susia.  
While FEZs may adopt their own administrative and commercial regulations, these must not contradict federal or state law.

Section 6. Revocation and Bankruptcy  
If a Yarnojte fails to uphold its obligations, including providing basic services, protecting rights, or meeting economic growth quotas, the Secretariat of Efficiency may revoke its FEZ status.  
In the event of bankruptcy, the assets of the Yarnojte shall be liquidated, and the FEZ reassigned to another eligible Yarnojte.

Section 7. Taxation  
FEZs are subject to a special economic zone tax, levied jointly by the federal and respective state governments, based on profits. This is in addition to any internal taxation established by the FEZ for service provision.

Section 8. Dispute Resolution  
Disputes between FEZs and states, or between Yarnojtes, shall be arbitrated by the Secretariat of Efficiency. All decisions of the Secretariat may be appealed to the Supreme Court of Susia, whose judgment shall be final and binding.

Section 9. Voluntary Participation  
Residency and employment within FEZs shall be entirely voluntary. No person may be compelled to reside in or work for a FEZ. All persons retain the right to exit any FEZ and relocate without restriction or penalty.
