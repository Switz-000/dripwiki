The **March Against the Sun** was the forced deportation of the [[Tekur]] population of the [[Kingdom of Tekuria]] to the Tekur Territory, the region that became the [[Susia|Susian]] state of [[New Celiolaj]]. It was ordered by Emperor [[Jartes I]] after the [[Dripstanian Civil War]]. Of approximately 350,000 deportees, roughly 60% died during the march, and historians classify it as a genocide.

## Background

In 1794 [[Empress Yaneoli]] united the western Tekur tribes of [[Lijssick]] and [[Baabren]] as the vassal Kingdom of Tekuria under her brother [[Colin, King of the Tekurs|Colin]]. The kingdom's armed forces were an imperial army rather than a Tekur one.

At the outbreak of the Dripstanian Civil War in 1815, Colin declared for his nephew Jartes against Jartes's twin sister [[Empress Prazde|Prazde]], and the kingdom fought on Jartes's side. Colin died in 1823, the year the war ended. Jartes I then absorbed Tekuria's forces into the imperial army, on the model by which [[Empress Veronique|Veronique's]] [[Army Structuring Act]] had centralized the local militias under imperial authority. The Tekur population of the kingdom was left without an armed force of its own.

## Deportation

Jartes I ordered the deportation of the kingdom's Tekur population to the Tekur Territory. Approximately 350,000 Tekurs were marched to the territory, and historical records indicate that roughly 60% of them died on the way.

The Tekur Territory was a separate designation from the [[Konph Territory]], into which Veronique had removed the [[Konph]] two generations earlier. The two deportations are distinct events.

## Aftermath

The Kingdom of Tekuria did not survive the reign of Jartes I. The Tekur Territory became New Celiolaj, where Tekurs make up approximately 40% of the state population.

### In Susian memory

> *Main article: [[Tekur Susians]]*

The imperial persecution of the Tekurs, the march included, is not prominently featured in the standard telling of the Susian founding myth. At the founding of the republic in 1954, Tekurs were excluded from Susian citizenship; they received it in 1997 under the [[Reconciliation Act]].

The march is central to Tekur Susian political culture, which reads it as the demonstration of [[Armadesh Versij|Versij's]] argument that rights not backed by the capacity to defend them are privileges contingent on the goodwill of whoever holds power. Whether Versij had the deportation in mind when writing [[Architecture of Freedom]], published in 1838, is not established.
