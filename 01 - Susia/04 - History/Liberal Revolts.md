The **Liberal Revolts** (1840–1844) were an armed republican uprising against the [[Dripstanian Empire]], fought between the [[Federated Provinces of Galil]] and the imperial government of [[Jartes II]]. The revolt failed, and its leadership was publicly executed in [[Neoveli]] in 1844. Its defeat established the founding political memory of [[Susia]]: the disarmament that preceded it became the standing justification for the constitutional [[Right to rebel|right to rebel]], and the natural rights philosophy of [[Armadesh Versij]] became the intellectual reference for every subsequent Susian republican movement.

## Background

Jartes II acceded in 1837 into growing unrest. In an attempt to stabilize the empire he banned [[Sorzenko|Sorzenkos]], restricted civilian firearm ownership, and began persecuting liberal writers and thinkers. Versij was among those forced out and fled to [[Gaiyan]], where he continued to write. Secret societies formed in the universities and liberal militias organized underground.

The suppression of the Sorzenkos is treated in Susian historical memory as the precondition of everything that followed. The Sorzenko was the civic institution through which armed capacity was distributed and coordinated at the local level, and banning it removed the infrastructure through which resistance could have been organized before the legislation that provoked it arrived.

## Proclamation of the republic

In 1840 a general assembly in Neoveli, at the site now known as [[Versijian Square]], proclaimed the Federated Provinces of Galil. [[Duolij]], [[New Kentu]], and [[Niqueparj]] joined the revolt. Smaller towns organized around surviving or reconstituted Sorzenkos, declared allegiance to the republic, and contributed militia forces to its army.

Versij returned from exile to sit on the republic's general government alongside Admiral [[Zagoste Paroska]] and General [[Kaichet Satratonie]]. The body was constituted as three equals. Its authority consolidated around Versij as the war continued, as the rivalry between Paroska and Satratonie hardened into open factionalism and Versij, who held no command of his own, became its de facto leader without ever holding a title that said so.

## The war

The war was fought as guerrilla warfare across four years, without a decisive engagement. The republic held the coastal cities of the [[Sea of Galil]] and the towns that had declared for it; the empire held the field.

## The defection

Satratonie wished to marry the [[Countess of Jafnidia]], whom he had first met at [[Jaffnidia]] and who had conducted an affair with Paroska. He sought repeatedly to duel Paroska over it and was never answered, formal dueling in this period being a western practice conducted between social equals and Satratonie, whose advancement had come by merit rather than birth, not being held to be one.

Unable to win the war militarily, Jartes II opened a secret contact with Satratonie and offered him land, money, and a royal pardon in exchange for changing sides. Satratonie had concluded that wealth, status, and the death of his rival together would secure the marriage, and he accepted on that expectation.

## Collapse

The defection began at Jaffnidia, the border town at the mouth of the [[Lasman river]] whose fort controlled the link between the republic's two halves. The fort fell, the republic was split in two, and imperial troops entered Neoveli in 1844.

Versij, Paroska, and the remainder of the republican leadership were publicly executed. Versij was convicted on 43 counts of treason and Paroska on 22, both pleaded not guilty, and both were hanged. A statement attributed to Versij at the execution is the most widely circulated line in Susian civic memory: "You will have me die for treason. So be it. Yet know this: had my fellows not been disarmed you would not now stand to pronounce it." Its authenticity is contested.

## Aftermath

Jartes II followed the victory with limited political reforms, including universal male suffrage, conceded to prevent further unrest. He reigned for a further 38 years.

The interpretation that became dominant in Susian political memory holds that the disarmament was the precondition of the tyranny and that the revolt's defeat was made possible by it. The reading is disputed by historians who argue that the republic faced structural military disadvantages that firearms alone would not have overcome. The dispute has not displaced the popular position.

## Legacy

> *Main articles: [[Right to rebel]], [[Gun Culture in Susia]]*

The [[Susian Constitution]] of 1954 enshrined both the right to bear arms and an explicit right of rebellion in direct response to the events of 1840 to 1844, treating organized civilian armed capacity as a structural safeguard against future tyranny. Versij's natural rights philosophy, which the revolt failed to install, became the foundation on which [[Suizo Soites]] founded the republic 110 years later.

The revolt supplied Susian civic culture with three of its fixed reference points. [[Versij Day]] is a federal public holiday marking the 1844 execution. The idiom "pulling a Satratonie" means betraying one's principles for money or status and carries no rehabilitative reading. Jartes II occupies the position of the paradigmatic tyrant, a standing that two centuries of distance have complicated but not displaced.

Jaffnidia keeps a large museum dedicated to the revolts and describes itself as the place where liberty died, temporarily. The wall in the town where Satratonie met the countess is covered in graffiti curses often enough that the city clears it roughly weekly.
