The **Secretariat of Efficiency** or SoE is a independent body of the [[Susian Federal Government]]

Structure:
- Independent body (not Cabinet, reports to no one directly)
- Selected through national examination (meritocratic, rotating every 3 years)
- Can serve two terms max (prevents careerism, 6 years maximum)
- Non-partisan by design (technocratic selection)
- 350 members (enough for specialization, small enough to coordinate)
- Conflict of Interest Rules: Cannot have corporate affiliations during service

Functions:

1. [[Free Economic Zone|FEZ]] Oversight (grants/revokes [[Yarnojte]] status)
2. FEZ Dispute Resolution (mediates inter-corporate conflicts)
3. Constitutional Compliance (ensures FEZs meet minimums)
4. Land Allocation (determines where new FEZs can exist)
5. Crisis Intervention (can suspend FEZ autonomy temporarily)
6. Commerce Enforcement (AFCC operates under Secretariat authority)

Why This Exists:

- FEZ system needs oversight BUT not political oversight
- Senators would be captured by corporate interests
- President too busy for day-to-day FEZ management
- Technocratic body can make unpopular but correct decisions

The Selection Process:

National Examination (Every Year):

1. Written exam (economics, law, engineering, public policy)
2. Case study analysis (FEZ scenarios, problem-solving)
3. Interview panel (existing Secretariat members + judges)
4. Background check (rigorous, eliminates corporate ties)
5. Top ~110 scorers selected (replaces 1/3 of Secretariat)

Eligibility Requirements:

- Susian citizenship (10+ years)
- Professional experience (5+ years in relevant field)
- NO corporate affiliations (strict disqualification)
- NO immediate family in Yarnojte leadership (conflict of interest)
- Must resign from any corporate position (before taking exam)
- Clean legal record
- Pass cognitive enhancement disclosure (must declare if enhanced)

The Corporate Influence Problem:

What Corporations Try:

- [[Troli Ustaras|TU]], [[Van Ritter]], [[Nayotai]] all operate elite universities
- Offer free education to talented students
- Condition: "Serve in civil service for 3 years after graduation"
- Hope: Students serve in Secretariat, remain loyal to corporate benefactors

Why It Doesn't Work:

1. Conflict of Interest Disqualification:

- Received [[Yarnojte]] scholarship or studied in a Yarnojte university
- Ban on immediate family in Yarnojte positions
- Spouse must disclose financial statements
- Any financial relationships > D$ 10,000 in past 10 years
- More than 1% stock portfolio in any single Yarnojte
- Must wait 5 years after corporate ties end to apply

2. Short Terms:

- Even if someone gets in, only serves 3-6 years max
- Cannot build long-term relationships with corporations
- Rotated out before corporate influence takes root

1. Examination System:

- Merit-based, not connections-based
- Randomized exam formats that change annually
- Anonymous grading with rotating evaluators
- Former secretariat members prohibited from exam prep counselling for 10 years

1. Cultural Resistance:

- Secretariat members see themselves as "Taming the Behemoths"
- Being perceived as corporate-aligned = social death within Secretariat
- Peer pressure enforces neutrality

1. Legal Consequences:

- Accepting corporate bribes/influence = federal crime
- Immediate expulsion from Secretariat
- Loss of professional credentials
- Possible prison time

The Result:

- Corporations invest millions in "civil service pathway" programs
- Very few graduates actually serve in Secretariat (disqualified)
- Those who do serve aren't loyal to corporate benefactors (culture + rules)
- But the secretariat is still vulnerable to a more informal kind of influence, intellectual capture. Reading [[Troli Ustaras|TU]] funded papers, attending [[Nayotai]] sponsored conferences etc.
- Some members are influenced by corporate/modernist thinking but institutional diversity prevents one ideology from dominating 
- The secretariat is the least captured [[Susian Federal Government|Federal]] institution, but capture attempts never stop. The System is able to expel most, but not all. Institutional mechanisms avoid this problem becoming systematic.
