**Federal Cognitive Enhancement Program** was a Susian federal research program that ran from 2040 to 2048, expediting cognitive enhancement research under streamlined ethics oversight justified by Cold War national-security interests. Its contractors, [[Troli Ustaras]] and the [[Soites Group]], had developed the underlying prototype technology privately from 2034 onward. The program produced nine deaths, twenty-three cases of permanent cognitive damage, and sixty-three moderate injuries before its concealment collapsed in a 2049 public exposure that led to its termination and to the criminal prosecution of Soites Group leadership.

## Background

> _Main article: [[Cognitive enhancement]]_

The technology the program later expedited had been developed privately since 2034, when the Soites Group partnered with Troli Ustaras on cognitive enhancement prototypes. Between 2035 and 2037, early prototypes were tested on paid, consenting participants from the [[Soites Experimenter Program]], producing known side effects including headaches, mood disturbances, and occasional seizures, under Susia's stricter pre-transition regulatory regime. [[The Great Transition]] of 2038 and the establishment of the [[Free Economic Zone|FEZ]] system replaced that regime with one permitting expedited federal research.

## Structure

The federal government launched the program in 2040, contracting Troli Ustaras and the Soites Group to continue and scale the enhancement research the two companies had already begun privately. Ethics oversight remained nominally in place but operated under a streamlined process, expedited relative to the ordinary regulatory standard, under national-security justifications tied to the Cold War.

## The Disaster Years (2040-2048)

The period became known afterward as the Disaster Years. Between 2040 and 2046, procedures under the program caused nine deaths: three from intraoperative complications such as hemorrhage and stroke, four from post-surgical infection or seizure, and two from implant malfunctions causing cascading neural failure. A further twenty-three participants suffered permanent cognitive damage, and sixty-three experienced moderate injuries requiring extended recovery.

The first death occurred in 2041, when a participant died of a fatal seizure during the procedure; the family received 500,000 Drips in compensation and was pressured into signing a non-disclosure agreement. The pattern held for the rest of the program's run: families of the deceased and of the seriously injured received incomplete compensation and were pressured into non-disclosure agreements rather than told the program's actual safety record.

## Public Exposure and Termination (2049-2051)

In 2049, families of three deceased participants broke their non-disclosure agreements simultaneously and coordinated with journalists to expose the program's existence and its safety failures. The leak landed the same year as the unrelated [[Troli Ustaras Corruption Scandal (2047)|Troli Ustaras corruption scandal]], which had already cost TU its [[Yarnojte]] status in 2047 over an attempted bribe of a [[Secretariat of Efficiency]] member. The coincidence of the two stories produced a severe crisis of public trust in both companies.

Congressional hearings, an investigation by the [[Federal Authority of Commerce and Competition]], and a Secretariat of Efficiency review followed. The program was formally terminated, and by 2051 Troli Ustaras had completed its post-scandal restructuring and regained Yarnojte status.

## Prosecutions

> _Main article: [[Trevda Soites]]_

Trevda Soites, chief executive of the Soites Group for the program's entire run (2031-2052), was indicted in 2050 on charges tied to the program's deaths and injuries, including counts of criminally negligent homicide, reckless infliction of serious bodily injury, and conspiracy and obstruction charges tied to the non-disclosure-agreement scheme. He was acquitted on the counts that reached trial in 2052, and had the remaining counts dismissed pretrial. Troli Ustaras's own leadership faced criminal prosecution over the 2047 bribery scandal; whether any TU executive was separately charged over the enhancement deaths themselves is not established.

## Legacy

The program's collapse left cognitive enhancement out of federal hands for a decade: Troli Ustaras ran only privately funded, voluntary clinical trials from 2051 onward, and the eventual [[Cognitive Rights Act of 2061]] built its consent, waiting-period, and disclosure requirements directly against the program's record of concealment.
