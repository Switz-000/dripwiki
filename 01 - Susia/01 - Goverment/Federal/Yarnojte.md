A **Yarnojte** is a corporation granted strategic status by the [[Susian Federal Government]], conferring the authority to govern [[Free Economic Zone|Free Economic Zones]] (FEZs) and participate in federal economic planning. The Yarnojte system was created as part of the [[The Great Transition|Great Transition]] of 2038, establishing the constitutional framework of the [[Susia|Techno-Federative Republic of Susia]]. As of 2080, six corporations hold active Yarnojte status, while three former Yarnojtes have had their status revoked.

## Origins

> *Main article: [[The Great Transition]]*

The Yarnojte system emerged from the constitutional reforms of 2036 to 2038, but its relationship to its supposed intellectual foundation is contested and substantially distorted.

### Kolkov's Philosophy

> *Main article: [[Yário Kolkov]]*

[[Yário Kolkov]]'s political philosophy, developed across works including [[Evolution of Governance]] and [[Democracy as Organism]], was not a blueprint for corporate governance. It was a theory of competitive institutional pluralism: the argument that healthy political systems require multiple competing forms of governance, including but not limited to corporations, traditional democratic states, experimental communities, and other arrangements, all coexisting, competing for residents, and forced to adapt or lose population. Kolkov's central concern was adaptability. He argued that any single governance model, whether democratic, corporate, or otherwise, would calcify without competitive pressure from alternatives.

His work was deliberately non-prescriptive and, by his own admission, at times vague and internally contradictory. He was skeptical of anyone who claimed to have derived a clean ideology from it. The [[Kolkov Interview 2009|2009 interview]] conducted by [[Yavna Raroska]] documents his awareness that [[Troli Ustaras]] was already selectively quoting his work to legitimize corporate autonomy, stripping out the competition, oversight, and exit mechanisms he considered essential.

### The Modernist Alliance's Reading

> *Main article: [[Modernist Alliance]]*

The [[Modernist Alliance]], the political movement that drove the Great Transition, formed around Kolkov's ideas but was never ideologically unified. The movement contained multiple competing factions, each with its own reading of his work: technocratic utopians who emphasized technology and post-scarcity, federalist reformers focused on institutional competition between states, and corporate modernists who interpreted the framework primarily as justification for expanded corporate autonomy. These factions agreed on the need for constitutional reform but disagreed substantially on what the new system should look like.

### Corporate Influence and the Constitutional Compromise

Major corporations, particularly [[Troli Ustaras]] and [[Nayotai]], had actively funded and lobbied the Modernist factions most amenable to their interests during the lead-up to the constitutional convention. By the time the convention opened in 2036, corporate modernists held significant influence over the movement's direction, and TU in particular had invested heavily in shaping how Kolkov's framework was interpreted in policy circles, a process Kolkov himself described as intellectual capture.

The Yarnojte system as ratified in 2038 was the product of negotiation between these competing factions, further compromised by the practical demands of constitutional consensus. The result bore a recognizable family resemblance to Kolkov's ideas: institutional competition, structured autonomy, oversight mechanisms, and easy exit. But the system narrowed his pluralist vision considerably, centering corporate FEZ governance as the primary alternative model rather than one among many. The competition, oversight, and exit provisions Kolkov considered non-negotiable were present in the final text but diluted from what more reform-oriented factions had proposed.

Whether the Yarnojte system represents a reasonable implementation of competitive governance principles or a corporate co-optation of a philosophy that never endorsed it remains a subject of genuine debate in Susian political theory.

## Requirements for Status

Yarnojte status is granted by the [[Secretariat of Efficiency]] (SoE) and requires a corporation to demonstrate:

- National strategic importance in a sector relevant to Susia's economic or security interests
- Demonstrated governance capacity, including the ability to administer housing, healthcare, education, and infrastructure within FEZ territories
- Financial stability sufficient to sustain long-term FEZ operations
- Constitutional compliance, including adherence to the federal minimums that apply within all FEZs
- SoE approval following a review process

Status is not permanent. The Secretariat conducts ongoing performance reviews and retains the authority to revoke status at any time for violations of FEZ constitutional minimums, failure to meet performance targets, or actions that threaten the integrity of the federal system. Attempting to corrupt federal employees or the Secretariat itself carries automatic and immediate revocation with no right of appeal.

## Powers and Constraints

### Powers

Active Yarnojtes may:

- Govern leased FEZ territories with broad internal autonomy, including setting internal regulations, managing housing, healthcare, education, and law enforcement within the zone
- Participate in federal economic planning processes
- Access special government contracts unavailable to non-Yarnojte corporations
- Bid on new FEZ territories as they become available

### Constraints

Yarnojte status does not confer monopoly protection. Yarnojtes must still compete with each other and with non-Yarnojte companies in open markets. Additional constraints include:

- Payment of federal and state taxes at standard rates
- Meeting SoE performance targets on an ongoing basis
- Subject to constitutional rights enforcement within FEZ territories: residents retain federal constitutional protections regardless of FEZ governance structures
- Full compliance with [[Federal Authority of Commerce and Competition|AFCC]] commerce regulations
- Inability to bribe, surveil, infiltrate, or otherwise attempt to corrupt federal employees or Secretariat members: this is the single absolute prohibition, carrying automatic revocation and criminal prosecution

## Status Revocation

When a Yarnojte loses its status, all FEZ leases are immediately canceled. The affected territories may be assigned to another Yarnojte through the standard bidding process, or reincorporated into the jurisdiction of the host state. The federal and state governments are required to coordinate assistance for displaced FEZ residents.

Three revocations have occurred since the system's founding:

- **[[Troli Ustaras]]** (2047): Status revoked after an attempt to bribe a [[Secretariat of Efficiency|Secretariat]] member reviewing a FEZ expansion application. TU was fined D$500 million, its FEZ operations were suspended, and its leadership was criminally prosecuted. Status was regranted in 2051 following a full corporate restructuring under new management. TU had originally been granted status in 2041, making the scandal a decade into its operation.
- **[[Vandernij & Milier]]** (2083): Status revoked due to financial deterioration, resource depletion, and declining strategic relevance. The Nikuria Industrial District FEZ reverted to [[Postia]] state control. V&M continues to operate as a standard corporation.
- **[[POCOIL]]** (2087): Status revoked after the company lost strategic importance in the energy sector, displaced by [[Atompron]]'s dominance in nuclear energy. POCOIL rebranded as a petrochemical company and continues operating without FEZ authority.

The TU case established the clearest precedent in Yarnojte history. No corporation has attempted to bribe a federal official since, and the episode is routinely cited in corporate compliance training across all Yarnojtes as the definitive example of the system's enforcement ceiling.

## Public Perception

Susian attitudes toward Yarnojtes vary substantially by state and political alignment. The [[Modernist Alliance]] treats the system as a foundational achievement, pointing to economic growth, infrastructure development, and technological advancement as evidence of its success. The [[Democratic-Federalist Party]] accepts the system while advocating for stronger worker protections and tighter constitutional minimums within FEZs. The [[Coalition of State's Rights]] tends toward skepticism, viewing Yarnojte authority as a concentration of power that erodes both state sovereignty and individual freedom.

At a cultural level, each Yarnojte carries a distinct public image. [[Troli Ustaras]] is associated with elite technocratic ambition and a cult-like corporate culture. [[Soites Group]] is seen as populist and omnipresent. [[Nayotai]] is respected for reliability and distrusted for its treatment of workers. [[Van Ritter]] commands a mixture of patriotic respect and wariness about its security apparatus. [[Lischev-Verene]] is admired for its medical achievements and regarded with unease for its surveillance model. [[Atompron]] is the most invisible of the Yarnojtes to most Susians, essential to everything and rarely thought about.

States without FEZs, particularly [[Orlítia]] and [[Vitrika]], have developed political cultures explicitly defined by their rejection of the Yarnojte model, viewing FEZ-free status as a point of identity rather than merely an economic choice.
