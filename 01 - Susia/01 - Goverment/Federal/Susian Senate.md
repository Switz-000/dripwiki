The **Susian Senate** is a chamber of the legislature of [[Susia]], established with the republic in 1954. Each of the nine states and the [[Neutral District]] elects five senators by party list, for 50 seats in all. Because representation is equal between states, the Senate gives the smaller states a weight they do not have in the presidency, which is decided by national popular vote.

## History

### Republican era

The first Senate election was held in 1954, when all five seats of every state were filled at once. Two seats in each state drew short two-year terms by lot, which established the two classes of seats elected in alternation from then on.

> *Main article: [[Party Federation]]*

Until the 1970s, matters internal to a state were settled in the Senate rather than by the federal executive. The practice was tested in 1956, when the Senate removed the federal commissioner provision from the [[Dravda Act]]. It eroded as the [[Continental Divide]] escalated, through measures such as the [[Federal Loyalty Act]] of 1961, and ended under [[Čalsor Boňár]] with the [[MAGEN act]] of 1975. As the presidency gained power, the president and the Senate became rivals.

### Techno-Federative era

> *Main article: [[Susia]]*

Since the constitution of 2038 the legislature has been bicameral, composed of the Senate and the [[Chamber of Experts]].

## Structure

Each state holds five seats. Three are elected in the same year as the president, beginning in 1954, and two at the midpoint of the presidential term, beginning in 1956. Senators serve four-year terms.

### Elections

Senate candidates are put forward by state parties, not by federations. A voter votes for an individual candidate; the votes are pooled by party, and seats are allocated to parties by their share, then filled by their most-voted candidates. The ballot shows each candidate's party and, beside it, the [[Party Federation|federation]] the party belongs to. A seat that falls vacant passes to the next most-voted candidate of the same party.

## Powers and Limitations

Spending bills originate in the Senate, as required by the constitution. The Senate confirms nominations to the [[Supreme Court of Susia]] by supermajority. Its present powers are described in [[Susia]].

The president is elected by national popular vote without an intermediate electoral body, and tends to represent the interests of the larger states. The Senate, with equal representation for every state, is the counterweight.
