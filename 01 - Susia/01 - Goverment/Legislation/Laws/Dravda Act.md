The **Dravda Act** is a [[Susia|Susian]] federal law passed in 1956 and named after Senator [[Halios Dravda]] of [[Postia]], who drafted it and was killed by a Nevir before it came to a vote. Written as a bill of federal intervention in Postia after the [[Neoveli Stock Exchange Bombing]], it lost its central provision, a federal commissioner for a state in crisis, in the [[Susian Senate]]. The act as passed founded the federal security agency [[Frumentarze]].

## Background

> *Main article: [[Ant wars]]*

The [[Nevirs]] bombed the [[Neoveli Stock Exchange]] on 30/05/1955, killing 43 people, with a truck that investigators traced to [[Duolij]]. [[Postia]] was then in the second year of the [[Ant wars]], and its governor, [[Filevir Matri]], was conducting the state's response through the [[Postian National Guard]] and his own administration.

[[Halios Dravda]], a senator for Postia from the [[Pačese ro Virspruje]], drafted a bill of federal intervention in the state. On 17/10/1955, while the bill was in committee, he was killed by a Nevir letter bomb opened in his Senate office in [[Soiteslaj]]. The investigation fell to the federal government, which had no body charged with political violence, and produced no suspect.

The killing carried the Nevir campaign into the federal capital. The bill passed without its Postian commissioner and with the federal security agency it created.

## Provisions

As drafted, the bill provided for a federal commissioner who would take command of the internal security of a state in crisis.

The Senate removed the commissioner provision before passage. The opposition came from senators of both federations, who would not set a precedent for federal command over a state's internal security. The act passed in 1956 under Dravda's name and founded the federal security agency [[Frumentarze]].

## Effects

The act left the response to the violence in Postia under the state government. Matri's crackdown continued under his own authority through the end of the Ant Wars.

The defeat of the commissioner provision was an early test of the practice by which a state's internal matters stayed in the Senate rather than passing to the federal executive. The practice held until it eroded during the [[Continental Divide]]. The agency the act created outlasted it.
