A **Sorzenko** is a civic shooting club in [[Susia]], combining firearms training, communal defense, recreational shooting, and social functions under a single institution. Sorzenkos are among the oldest continuous civic institutions in Susian history, predating the [[Dripstanian Empire|Empire]] itself, and remain active throughout the country as of 2080. They are particularly prevalent in rural areas, smaller towns, and the eastern states of [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]]. In Susian civic life, membership in a Sorzenko carries mutual obligations: members are expected to be capable of defending one another, and the bonds formed within them frequently cross class lines in ways that few other institutions produce.

## History

### Settlement Era Origins

Sorzenkos emerged among the earliest [[Armotism|Armotist]] settlers on the Dripstan continent, formed out of practical necessity rather than ideology. Settler communities faced persistent conflict with indigenous populations, and the need for organized, trained civilian defense was immediate. The early Sorzenko was a community shooting club whose primary purpose was to ensure that enough members of a settlement could handle firearms competently and coordinate a defense if attacked. Associated militias formed around most Sorzenkos, providing the organizational structure for communal defense.

The [[Hăjaven]] festival also originated within this early Sorzenko tradition, as a communal hunt organized by the militia that concluded with a shared meal distributed to the wider town.

### Eastward Expansion

During the imperial eastward expansion into the territories now comprising [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]], the [[Cericeiro|Cericeiros]], the skilled frontier horsemen who led and populated the expansion, carried Sorzenko culture with them. The institution spread rapidly across the eastern states, adapting to frontier conditions and becoming embedded in [[Cericeiro]] identity. This eastern diffusion is why Sorzenko culture remains strongest in those states today, where the frontier tradition never fully receded even as the frontier itself closed.

### Imperial Centralization

> *Main article: [[Empress Veronique]]*

[[Empress Veronique]]'s **Army Structuring Act** centralized the Sorzenkos under imperial authority as part of her broader military reforms, creating a permanent professional army from the previously decentralized militia system. The act brought Sorzenkos into a formal relationship with the imperial state for the first time, though they retained their civic character and local organization. The reform also introduced civil examinations for officer promotion, establishing meritocratic principles within what had been an informal institution.

### The Liberal Revolts and Jartes II's Suppression

> *Main article: [[Liberal Revolts]]*

The political significance of Sorzenkos became explicit during the reign of [[Jartes II]], who banned them and restricted civilian gun ownership as part of a broader campaign to stabilize growing unrest. The suppression of Sorzenkos is widely considered in Susian historical memory to have been a prerequisite for Jartes II's tyrannical legislation: disarming the population removed the organized civic infrastructure through which resistance could be coordinated.

When the [[Liberal Revolts]] broke out in 1840, smaller towns organized around surviving or reconstituted Sorzenkos, declared allegiance to the [[Federated Provinces of Galil]], and contributed militia forces to the republican army. The revolts ultimately failed, in part because the suppression had been effective enough to prevent the coordinated armed resistance that Sorzenkos would otherwise have enabled. [[Armadesh Versij|Versij's]] famous statement at his execution, that his fellows would not have been disarmed had he lived, became the defining articulation of this interpretation, though historians continue to debate whether the revolts could have succeeded regardless.

The [[Susian Federal Government|Susian constitution]] enshrined the [[Right to rebel|right to rebel]] partly in direct response to Jartes II's disarmament campaign, treating armed civic capacity as a structural guarantee against future tyranny.

### The Techno-Federative Era

Sorzenkos survived the [[The Great Transition|Great Transition]] of 2038 without significant institutional disruption, continuing to operate under state and local jurisdiction. Their relationship with the [[Free Economic Zone|FEZ]] system is complicated: FEZs may not prohibit firearm ownership entirely, but gun culture is informally discouraged within many of them. [[Troli Ustaras]] and [[Atompron]] in particular have promoted virtual shooting ranges as a sanitized alternative to traditional Sorzenko culture, framing firearms as a relic of a less sophisticated era. Uptake among FEZ residents has been limited, and Sorzenko membership in FEZ-adjacent independent communities has not declined noticeably as a result.

## Structure and Function

Sorzenkos vary considerably in size and formality, from small rural clubs of a few dozen members to larger urban organizations with hundreds. Most share a common set of functions:

**Firearms training** is the foundational activity. Members practice marksmanship, firearm safety, and maintenance. In rural Sorzenkos this often extends to hunting skills and wilderness competence. Gun safety is also taught in all Susian middle and high schools as part of the civics curriculum, and many students encounter Sorzenko culture through this channel before joining as adults.

**Communal defense capacity** remains a formal or informal expectation in most Sorzenkos. Membership creates mutual obligations: members are expected to be capable of defending their shooting partners and, by extension, the community. After mandatory military service, many Susians join the [[National Guard]] of their state and continue their Sorzenko membership in parallel, creating overlap between the two institutions.

**Social functions** are central to what distinguishes a Sorzenko from a simple shooting range. Membership cuts across class lines in a way that is relatively unusual in Susian civic life. A factory worker and a local business owner may shoot alongside each other weekly, share meals at [[Hăjaven]], and carry a mutual obligation of defense that does not exist between them in any other context. This cross-class bonding is recognized in Susian political culture as one of the institution's more valuable social functions.

**[[Hăjaven]]** remains the defining annual event of Sorzenko life, particularly in rural communities, where the communal hunt and shared meal are organized through the local Sorzenko.

## Regional Variation

Sorzenko culture is not uniform across [[Susia]]. In the eastern states, [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]], and [[Troli]], Sorzenkos are deeply embedded in local identity, linked to [[Cericeiro]] heritage and frontier tradition. Open carry is more common in these states generally, and Sorzenko membership rates are higher.

In the western states, particularly [[Dripia]] and [[Postia]], Sorzenkos exist but urban gun culture is more reserved. Gun ownership is normal but treated as a private matter by most city dwellers, who keep weapons at home rather than carrying openly. Urban Sorzenkos tend to be more recreational than civic in character.

[[Tekur|Tekurs]] have notably higher rates of Sorzenko membership, weapon ownership, and military enlistment than the Susian average, a pattern that reflects both the community's strong civic engagement tradition and the particular significance of armed self-defense within a population that experienced state-sanctioned violence within living historical memory.

In [[Nessel]], Sorzenkos exist but operate within the state's distinct [[Doremojism|Doremojist]] religious culture, giving them a somewhat different character from those in the [[Armotism|Armotist]] mainstream.

## Contemporary Status

As of 2080, Sorzenkos remain active civic institutions across [[Susia]], with the strongest presence in rural areas and the eastern states. They are not government bodies and receive no federal funding, operating as private civic associations under state and local law. [[Hăjaven]] is a federal public holiday, giving the institution an indirect form of national recognition without formal incorporation into the state apparatus.

The institution's long-term trajectory within FEZ territories is uncertain. As FEZ populations grow and the balance between FEZ and independent Susia shifts, the cultural norms that sustain Sorzenko membership in those communities face pressure from corporate governance models that are, at best, indifferent to the tradition.
