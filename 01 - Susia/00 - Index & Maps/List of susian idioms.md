| Sinže               | Informal abbreviation of "Sinžecalij" (Syndicalist)    | A [[Syndicalism\|syndicalist]], union advocate, or left-leaning person. Broadly applied to anyone perceived as economically left of the Modernist-Liberal consensus                                 | Pejorative. | Travňeža ro hunochi | “Society of ants”                                      | A metaphor for the mass pendular motion of commuters following a rigid, repetitive daily schedule, moving simultaneously like a colony.                                                             | Ambiguous. Used approvingly by productivity advocates, used with mockery by workers and labor critics. Sometimes associated with [[Ňotrídulij]] culture.<br><br> | Directly tied to Felipe Santiago's campaign for a 12-hour workday, which framed the 8-hour standard as inflexible.                                                                                                                                            |
| Žaveža              | “Sillicon”                                             | Informal umbrella term for technology, consumer electronics, AI, [[Cognitive enhancement\|cognitive enhancment]], digital platforms, and the tech industry broadly.                                 | Neutral to slightly warm. Can carry irony when used by older Susians or rural communities skeptical of [[TU]]'s reach.                                           | First popularized by the susian Startup / FinTech community.<br><br>Reflects technology’s total dominance over Susian daily life.                                                                                                                             |
| Vulre ro žavežas    | “Silicon dreamers”                                     | People who escape from reality through digital means: gaming, social media, neuroexperiences, and simulated interaction, to the point of social withdrawal.                                         | Pitying or dismissive. Not quite an insult, more of a diagnosis.                                                                                                 | Reflects broader anxieties about what happens to citizens who opt out of civic life into [[TU]]-mediated virtual spaces. Estimates place over two million Susians in this category, concentrated in major cities.                                             |
| [[Vuževas]]         | Abbreviation of "Vulre ro žaveža" ("Silicon dreamers") | Colloquial shorthand for both the phenomenon of digital escapism and the individuals who embody it.                                                                                                 | Slightly warmer than the full form, more casual, less clinical. Can be used affectionately among the group itself.                                               | Born in digital spaces, shortened for quick use in the same platforms where Vuževas spend their time.                                                                                                                                                         |
| [[Ňotrídulij]]      | "Servant of capital"                                   | A white-collar salaried worker employed by a Yarnojte or major corporation, characterized by exceptionally long hours, fierce loyalty, and subordination of personal life to professional identity. | Ambiguous and class-loaded. Used mockingly by critics, sometimes worn as a badge of pride by those who identify with the work ethic it implies.                  | Embodies the tension at the heart of Susian corporate culture. the Nayotai efficiency model taken to its human extreme.                                                                                                                                       |
| [[Mejdel]]          | “Flag-eater"                                           | An extreme nationalist who performs patriotism loudly and publicly without substance, someone whose love of country is aesthetic rather than principled.                                            | Derisive. Implies superficiality, tribalism, and bad faith.                                                                                                      | Closely associated with Van Ritter's corporate culture, omnipresent Susian flags create a manufactured nationalism. Mejdel is the civilian equivalent, the person who waves the flag loudest at a rally and disappears when real civic sacrifice is required. |
