**Čalsor Boňár** (1912–1983) was a Susian general and politician who served as the fourth President of [[Susia]] from 1974 to 1983. He led Susia through the [[Continental War]] and the post-war reconstruction, founded the state energy company [[Atompron]], and expanded the powers of the federal government. His presidency realigned the Susian party system and reshaped the [[Susian Democratic Union]] around his political program. He died in office in 1983.

### Early Life

Čalsor Boňár was born in 1912 in [[St. Yepodij]], in the state of [[Troli]], then part of the [[Dripstanian Empire]]. The Boňár family were large landowners in Troli, with holdings dating to the middle period of the Empire.

Boňár served in the [[War of Confian Secession]] (1927–1950), a prolonged guerrilla conflict fought to hold the eastern territories of the Empire against the secessionist [[Free Confian Republic]]. Large numbers of Susian conscripts fought in the war, and its length and conduct were among the factors that turned Susian opinion against the Empire and toward independence. Boňár was decorated for his service.

After the dissolution of the Empire and the founding of [[Susia]] in 1954, the [[Tahuni Accords]] and the subsequent National Agreement brokered by [[Suizo Soites]] granted concessions to a number of established landowning families in exchange for portions of their land and their support for the new republic. The concessions granted to the Boňár family included positions within the military, which advanced Boňár's career in the armed forces of the new republic.

### Military and Political Rise

Following the founding of the republic, Boňár continued his career in the Susian military and rose to the rank of general. During the [[Continental Divide]], the period of confrontation between Susia and the syndicalist [[Confian|Confia]] bloc, he served in several of the proxy conflicts of the era.

In 1969, during the [[Pierej Crisis]], Boňár directed the Susian-backed operation that installed General [[Sarže Saron]] in power. His role in the operation was publicly known, and the change of government was received in Susia as a liberation. He was also involved in a covert operation in [[Chongzha]], the details of which were not publicly acknowledged.

Boňár never held elected or appointed civil office before the presidency. He was nonetheless a recognized political presence throughout the 1960s and early 1970s, known within the [[Susian Democratic Union]] and within the military and intelligence apparatus. He made occasional public statements, consistently warning of the threat posed by Confia and opposing syndicalism, a position he had held since the founding of the republic. He spoke in favor of the [[Federal Loyalty Act]].

The collapse of the détente policy pursued by [[Ergagério Sienes]] and the outcome of the [[Sutsa-Fuhu]] Civil War in 1973 were widely seen as failures of Susian foreign policy. Boňár, whose warnings about Confia were now read as vindicated, became more prominent and more outspoken. He secured the Susian Democratic Union nomination and won the [[1974 Susian presidential election]].

### Presidency

_Main article: [[Continental War]]_

Boňár took office in 1974 and was reelected in 1978 and 1982, serving three terms. His presidency was dominated in its first years by the [[Continental War]] (1975–1977), the open conflict between Susia and the [[Confia|Confian]] bloc. Boňár directed Susian strategy during the war, which ended in Susian victory and the collapse of the hard-line Confian government.

At the war's conclusion Boňár oversaw a lenient peace settlement. The leniency followed from his conception of Susia as the dominant civilization of the continent, a power that would absorb and outlast its defeated rivals rather than punish them. He did not restore relations with Confia, which came later under [[Filevir Matri]].

Boňár retained much of the emergency apparatus established during the Continental Divide and the war. He regarded most of these measures as temporary and others as permanent. The party bans and labor restrictions of the [[MAGEN act]] were treated as temporary and were unwound in stages following the war, a process largely carried out under Matri and [[Filňar Čantij]]. Other expansions, including the enlarged mandate of the [[Frumentarze]], were retained.

In domestic policy Boňár expanded the powers of the federal government, which under the Susian constitution had been deliberately limited. This expansion, the most contested aspect of his domestic record, gave the federal government authority it had not previously held. He pursued a policy of state-directed economic development, of which the founding of the state energy company [[Atompron]] was the principal instance. Atompron was established as a mostly state-owned enterprise during the post-war expansion and the growth of nuclear energy in Susia.

Boňár's presidency realigned the Susian party system. The [[Continental War]] and his response to it reorganized the basis of political division in Susia, and the [[Susian Democratic Union]] was reshaped around his program of centralization, foreign-policy assertiveness, and a stronger federal state.

### Personal Life

Boňár married and had three children.

In his youth Boňár played [[Crolball|crolball]] at a competitive level and was offered a place with the [[Niqueparj Badgers]], which he declined.

Boňár was a heavy drinker and smoker throughout his life. He drank prodigiously and smoked [[Kamino|Kaminoan]] cigarettes in large quantities, habits that became part of his public image. His drinking dated to his service in the [[War of Confian Secession]], where a common belief among soldiers held that adding liquor to unclean water would make it safe to drink.

His relationship with [[Filevir Matri]], his vice president across all three terms, was initially distant but became close over the course of their years in office. The two men were associated in the public mind, and were mocked by the press and by political opponents as "the general and his little sommelier."

### Legacy

Boňár was the longest-serving president in Susian history and the central political figure of the post-war period. The [[Susian Democratic Union]] that followed him was substantially his creation, organized around the program of centralization, federal power, and foreign-policy assertiveness that he had established.

He remains a contested figure. To his critics, particularly within the [[Susian Liberal Party]] and associated with [[Ergagério Sienes]], Boňár represented the blurring of civil and military authority that marked the Continental Divide. A general who had never held civil office, he became president, served longer than any other, expanded the military and the federal state, and retained emergency measures that his critics held had outlasted their justification. The expansion of federal power over a constitutional order designed to limit it was, in this view, a departure from the principles on which the republic was founded.

Boňár's conception of Susia as the dominant civilization of the continent later resurfaced in the idea of Susia integrating its former adversaries into a liberal order, a development that took place after his presidency.

Boňár was an influence on the later [[Modernism|modernist movement]], which drew on aspects of his approach to government, including long-horizon planning, the proposal of ten-year presidential terms, and state-directed economic development.
