**Armadesh Versij** (1801 to 1844) was a [[Dripstanian Empire|Dripstanian]] lawyer and political philosopher whose natural rights doctrine became the philosophical foundation of the [[Susia|Susian]] republic. He sat on the general government of the [[Federated Provinces of Galil]] during the [[Liberal Revolts]] and was publicly executed in [[Neoveli]] in 1844 following the republic's defeat.

## Early Life

Versij was born in 1801 in [[Kruie ro Yietre]], in the imperial territory that later became the state of [[Vitrika]]. He studied law at the [[University of New Kentu]], graduating in 1826.

## Philosophy

### Natural rights

> *Main article: [[Rights]]*

Versij held that rights are discovered through reason rather than granted by government. They are pre-political, inalienable, and universal, and the function of government is to recognize and protect them rather than to create them.

His account of rights is strictly negative. There is no natural right to employment, only the right to offer labor freely, and no natural right to bread, only the right to pursue it without interference. A right that requires violating another's rights is not a right. The position places the tradition that carries his name in standing opposition to positive rights and to welfarism, and makes laissez-faire economics its logical extension.

Rights belong to individuals rather than to groups. Government exercises only delegated powers, and where a delegated power conflicts with a right, the right prevails.

### The self-axiom

> *Main article: [[Versijian Self-Axiom]]*

The foundation of the system is the proposition "I think, therefore I am, therefore I possess myself," known as the Versijian Self-Axiom. Self-ownership follows from consciousness alone and is prior to every other claim.

The axiom was taken up more than a century later by [[Ňotrič Apaj]], who treated it as the only genuinely universal fact about sapient beings and used it as the single foundation of his cosmopolitan ethics. It entered [[Apajianism]] and, through Apaj, the ethical framework of [[Troli Ustaras]].

### Constitutional design

Versij's constitutional theory follows from the priority of rights over majorities. A constitution enumerates rights placed beyond the reach of ordinary legislation, requires supermajorities for rights-affecting laws, and establishes an independent judiciary empowered to strike down laws that violate them. Democracy is a means of protecting rights rather than an end in itself.

The [[Supreme Court of Susia]] was created in 1954 on this reasoning, and was authorized directly by the [[Susian Constitution]] rather than by statute so that its existence and core powers would sit beyond the reach of any single government.

### Civic armament and the right to rebel

> *Main article: [[Right to rebel]]*

In [[Architecture of Freedom]] Versij introduced the concept of the "citizen guards of the republic": rights not backed by the capacity to defend them are privileges contingent on the goodwill of whoever holds power. Ownership of a firearm is therefore not personal protection alone but an expression of civic standing, and competence is owed to family, neighbors, and the republic. The argument is treated at length in [[Gun Culture in Susia]] and in [[Sorzenko]].

The same reasoning produces the right to rebel. Citizens retain the right to overthrow a government that systematically violates natural rights, and the right is credible only where they hold the organized means to exercise it. Both principles were written into the Susian constitution in 1954.

## Liberal Revolts

> *Main article: [[Liberal Revolts]]*

### Exile

Seeking to stabilize growing unrest, [[Jartes II]] banned [[Sorzenko|Sorzenkos]], restricted civilian firearm ownership, and began persecuting liberal writers and thinkers. Versij was among those forced out. He fled to [[Gaiyan]] and continued writing there.

### The Federated Provinces of Galil

In 1840 a general assembly in [[Neoveli]], at the site now known as [[Versijian Square]], proclaimed the [[Federated Provinces of Galil]]. [[Duolij]], [[New Kentu]], and [[Niqueparj]] joined the revolt. Versij returned from exile to sit on the republic's general government alongside Admiral [[Zagoste Paroska]] and General [[Kaichet Satratonie]].

### Trial and execution

The war was fought as guerrilla warfare over four years. Unable to win militarily, Jartes II secretly offered Satratonie land, money, and a royal pardon in exchange for changing sides. Satratonie defected, the fort at [[Jaffnidia]] fell, the republic was split in two, and imperial troops entered Neoveli in 1844. Versij, Paroska, and the rest of the republican leadership were publicly executed. Versij was convicted on 43 counts of treason, having pleaded not guilty, and was hanged.

A statement attributed to him at the execution is the most widely circulated line in Susian civic memory: "You will have me die for treason. So be it. Yet know this: had my fellows not been disarmed you would not now stand to pronounce it." Its authenticity is contested. Its cultural currency is not, and it became the defining articulation of the reading that Jartes II's disarmament campaign was the precondition of the revolt's failure.

## Legacy

### Foundation of the republic

[[Suizo Soites]] founded the [[Susia|Federative Republic of Susia]] on Versij's natural rights philosophy in 1954, and the constitutional protection of natural rights, the right to bear arms, and the right to rebel all descend from it. In 1961 Soites granted Versij the Order of the First Guard of the Republic posthumously, its first award.

Versij's philosophy is one of the three intellectual traditions taught in the national civics curriculum, alongside [[Suizo Soites|Soites']] pragmatic liberalism and [[Yário Kolkov|Kolkov's]] competitive governance theory. Within [[Susian Exceptionalism]] it supplies the secular and universalist strand: the claim that Susian institutions are worth defending not because they are Susian but because they encode discovered truths about freedom. That framing is also the philosophical basis of [[Democratic evangelism]], which reasons that if rights are universal then their denial abroad is a wrong Susia has standing to address.

### Commemoration

[[Versij Day]] is a federal public holiday marking the 1844 execution. [[Versijian Square]] in Neoveli, the site of the 1840 assembly, is now ringed by banks. The idiom "Versij died for this?" is used to register disappointment at mediocrity or institutional failure, the joke resting on the distance between the sacrifice and the occasion.

### Later traditions

> *Main article: [[Versijism]]*

Versijism and [[Neoversijism]] are recognized traditions in Susian political philosophy, and Versijian constitutionalism was the founding ideology of the [[Pačese ro Virspruje]], its paramilitary wing the [[Knights of the Republic]], its newspaper [[The Versijian]], and its youth organization the [[Young Versijians]]. During the [[Ant Wars]], Versij's argument that rights undefended by force are merely privileges supplied ideological cover for armed political organization across the political spectrum, and the [[Supreme Court of Susia|Supreme Court]] ruling that dissolved the [[Eossaj|eossajs]] was contested by the PrV and the Knights as a misreading of his citizen-defender framework.

[[Modernism]] names Versijian liberalism among its intellectual influences. Abroad, [[Kunsek Aljerni]]'s [[Liberty!]] in [[Confia]] is classed as neo-Versijian libertarianism, and the [[Democratizing Multinational Force in Ditania]] adopted Versijism as its official ideology between 1986 and 1994.

## Miscellaneous

- The [[Neoveli]] house where Versij wrote [[Architecture of Freedom]] is now a [[Soitesmart]]. The Versij museum on the upper floor is free, and is reached through the store.
- A second line in wide circulation, "A nation of citizen soldiers is mightier than any standing army," is not his. It is [[Kaichet Satratonie|Satratonie's]], which Susians find either ironic or embarrassing.
