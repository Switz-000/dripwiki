**Saint Emperor Mantichev** (1637–1686) was the King of Forchev and the first emperor of the [[Dripstanian Empire]], reigning from 1674 to 1686. He convened the [[Convention of Forchev]], at which the Armotist coastal kingdoms agreed to unite under a single imperial structure, and carried out a land reform across the new empire before his assassination in 1686.

## Early Life

Mantichev was born in 1637, the elder son of [[Kaichet of Frajes|Baron Kaichet of Frajes]] and [[Damhor of Frajes]]. He became king of [[Forchev]], the first permanent Armotist colony on the continent, established in 1654 by refugees of the [[Armotist Revolution]] who had crossed the [[Sea of Sacoites]] from [[Gaiyan]].

The settlements the refugees founded along the western coast developed into separate kingdoms over the following two decades.

## Convention of Forchev

> *Main article: [[Convention of Forchev]]*

Twenty years after the landing, Mantichev summoned the other Armotist kings to Forchev. The convention negotiated the foundation of the Dripstanian Empire and established the captaincy system, under which the constituent territories were administered by hereditary lords owing military obligation to the crown. Mantichev was made the first Dripstanian emperor. He was crowned in the cathedral at Forchev, which stands as a protected historical site.

## Reign

Mantichev's reign lasted 12 years. Its principal act was a land reform carried out on an imperial scale, which settled competing peasant and aristocratic claims to the territories the kingdoms had brought into the union.

## Death

Mantichev was stabbed to death by a [[Sacoitism|Sacoitist]] dissident in 1686. He left no children, and the succession passed under imperial law to his brother [[Emperor Agamilos]].

## Legacy

Mantichev was canonised in the [[Armotism|Armotist]] tradition. [[Saint Mantichev Day]] is observed as a federal holiday in [[Susia]], and St. Mantichev City, built by [[Jartes II]] for the empire's bicentenary in 1874 and renamed [[Soiteslaj]] under the republic, carried his name for eighty years.
