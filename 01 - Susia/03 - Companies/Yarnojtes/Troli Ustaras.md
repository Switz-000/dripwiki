**Troli Ustaras** (TU) is a Susian technology corporation founded in 1959 in [[Donetruže]], [[Troli]], and the largest company by market capitalization in [[Susia]] as of 2080, with a market cap of D$23 trillion. TU holds [[Yarnojte]] status, granted in 2041 and restored in 2051 following revocation during the [[Troli Ustaras Corruption Scandal (2047)|corruption scandal of 2047]], and governs the [[Susian Innovation Center]] Free Economic Zone in [[Dripia]]. Its primary sectors are artificial intelligence, robotics, automation, computing, and cognitive enhancement hardware. TU employs approximately 800,000 people, including FEZ residents.

## History

### Founding and early development (1959–1977)

Troli Ustaras was founded in 1959 in [[Donetruže]], in the eastern state of [[Troli]], during the early years of the [[Continental Divide]]. The company's initial focus was computing research. Its founding coincided with the rapid industrialization of the post-[[Tahuni Accords|independence]] period and the federal government's growing interest in technology as a strategic asset against [[Confia]]'s syndicalist bloc.

Through the Continental Divide period, TU received federal research contracts oriented toward computing infrastructure and automation systems, positioning it as a strategic supplier to the Susian state. The company grew through the 1960s and early 1970s in parallel with the broader Susian technology sector.

### Post-war expansion (1977–2006)

The end of the [[Continental War]] in 1977 and the subsequent economic boom brought TU into a sustained period of expansion. The company diversified from its original computing and automation focus into consumer electronics, industrial robotics, and early artificial intelligence research. By the  2000s TU had become the dominant Susian technology company and one of the largest employers in [[Dripia]], where it had relocated its primary research and development operations to the area that would eventually become the [[Susian Innovation Center]].

During this period TU began investing in political influence, providing financial support to factions within the emerging [[Modernist Alliance]] that favored expanded corporate autonomy and structured FEZ governance. This lobbying effort was directed toward shaping how [[Yário Kolkov]]'s philosophy of competitive institutional pluralism would be interpreted when the Modernist Alliance came to power. Kolkov, who worked briefly at TU as an engineer from 1987 to 1989, later described the company's engagement with his ideas as intellectual capture, noting that TU selectively quoted his work to legitimize corporate autonomy while stripping out the oversight, competition, and exit provisions he considered essential.

> *Main article: [[Yário Kolkov]], [[Kolkovianism]]*

During the same period TU hired [[Ňotrič Apaj]] as Philosophical Advisor, a position Apaj held until his death. Apaj's 1985 book "[[After Scarcity]]" provided TU with the moral framework its corporate culture required: a historically grounded argument that technological progress was the next stage of human civilization, and that the mission of expanding human knowledge and capability was not merely profitable but historically obligatory. Where Kolkov gave TU institutional language it never consented to provide, Apaj gave TU ethical language he provided deliberately.

> *Main article: [[Ňotrič Apaj]]*

### The Great Transition and Yarnojte status (2006–2041)

> *Main article: [[The Great Transition]]*

TU was among the most active corporate participants in the political process that produced the [[Great Transition]] of 2038. Alongside [[Nayotai]], TU funded and lobbied the Modernist Alliance factions most amenable to corporate FEZ governance. By the time the constitutional convention opened in 2036, TU had invested substantially in shaping how Kolkov's framework would be translated into policy, a process that produced a constitutional text favorable to structured corporate autonomy.

The [[Susian Constitution|constitution of 2038]] established the Yarnojte system and the FEZ framework. TU was granted Yarnojte status in 2041, among the earliest recipients. The grant authorized TU to govern the [[Susian Innovation Center]] FEZ in Dripia, which it had been developing as a campus and residential community for years prior to formal Yarnojte designation.

### Corruption scandal and restructuring (2047–2051)

> *Main article: [[Troli Ustaras Corruption Scandal (2047)]]*

In 2047, TU was found to have attempted to bribe a member of the [[Secretariat of Efficiency]] in connection with a FEZ expansion application. The attempted bribe triggered automatic Yarnojte status revocation under the constitution's absolute prohibition on corrupting federal officials. TU was fined D$500 million, its FEZ operations were suspended, and its leadership faced criminal prosecution.

The scandal coincided with the 2049 public exposure of the [[Federal Cognitive Enhancement Program]], in which TU had been a central participant. The program, which ran from 2040 to 2048, had produced nine deaths, 23 cases of permanent cognitive damage, and 63 moderate injuries. Families of deceased participants had been pressured into signing non-disclosure agreements and received incomplete compensation. The simultaneous exposure of the bribery and the enhancement program deaths produced a severe crisis of public trust.

TU underwent a full corporate restructuring under new management between 2047 and 2051. Yarnojte status was restored in 2051 following review by the [[Secretariat of Efficiency]] and the [[Federal Authority of Commerce and Competition]].

### Post-restructuring and cognitive enhancement era (2051–present)

Following restructuring, TU committed to continuing cognitive enhancement research publicly and with rigorous safety protocols, limiting development to medical applications. The company conducted public clinical trials from 2051 to 2057 with no deaths, and received federal approval in 2057 for enhancement procedures indicated for severe PTSD, expanding to additional psychiatric indications in 2058.

The [[Cognitive Rights Act of 2061]] authorized enhancement procedures for non-medical use by adults over 25, subject to a three-month waiting period, mandatory informed consent, and federal counseling provision. The act also created sanctuary zones protecting unenhanced-majority communities. TU was the primary beneficiary of the act's passage, which opened cognitive enhancement to the general population.

By the 2080s, 98% of TU's workforce had undergone enhancement procedures, the highest rate among all Yarnojtes.

## Operations

### Products and sectors

TU's primary business spans artificial intelligence systems, industrial automation, consumer robotics, quantum computing, neural interfaces, and cognitive enhancement hardware. The company is the dominant domestic producer in each of these sectors and Susia's primary technological competitor against [[Ashgerad]] in the global cold war.

Consumer-facing products include computing hardware and software. TU phones, computers, and AI systems are widely used across Susia and in international markets. The company's industrial automation products are embedded in manufacturing operations throughout [[Dripstan]].

Cognitive enhancement hardware is manufactured exclusively by TU under a mandated cooperation arrangement with [[Lischev-Verene]], which handles the biological pharmaceutical component. Federal regulations require the two companies to maintain cross-licensing agreements for relevant patents.

> *Main article: [[Cognitive enhancement]]*

### Outside the FEZ

TU maintains a substantial presence in independent Susia through offices, research campuses, and manufacturing facilities concentrated in [[Dripia]] and [[Postia]]. Employment outside the FEZ carries high salaries and benefits relative to the broader Susian market, though not at FEZ levels. The company's selectivity is notable: TU hires from the top tier of Susian graduates, and placement within the [[Susian Innovation Center]] is considered the most competitive employment outcome in the Susian technology sector. The company also operates some of the most prestigious universities in Susia such as the [[Kolkov Institute for Innovation]] in [[Neoveli]], the [[Capital Institute of Technology and Policy]] in [[Soiteslaj]], the [[Southern Technical College]] in [[Donetruže]] and the [[Apaj Academy of Sciences and Engineering]] 

## Free Economic Zone

> *Main article: [[Susian Innovation Center]]*

The [[Susian Innovation Center]] FEZ in [[Dripia]] is TU's sole FEZ and its global headquarters. As of 2080, the zone houses approximately 180,000 residents. It integrates research and development facilities, manufacturing, residential infrastructure, schools from primary through university level, healthcare, and recreational amenities under unified TU administration. TU also operates inside the FEZ the

The zone is the most selective residential community in Susia by employment criteria. Residents are predominantly current TU employees and their families. Housing, healthcare, and education are provided by TU as components of the employment relationship. The zone's infrastructure is extensively monitored through smart city systems.

## Corporate culture

TU's corporate culture is organized around what the company describes as the mission: the proposition that technology is the mechanism by which humanity will solve its remaining problems, and that TU's work is therefore of civilizational rather than merely commercial significance. This framework was given its most systematic expression by [[Ňotrič Apaj]], whose [[After Scarcity|post-scarcity cosmopolitan philosophy]] provided the ethical foundation for TU's self-understanding. Institutional language drawn from [[Yário Kolkov]]'s work on adaptive governance is present throughout TU's communications, selectively quoted in ways Kolkov publicly rejected.

The official structure of work within TU includes a four-day, 32-hour work week. The cultural norm, documented by employees and outside observers, diverges from this: employees who work only their contractual hours are understood within TU culture to lack sufficient commitment to the mission, and many work substantially longer hours voluntarily. The company's own characterization of this dynamic is that genuine believers in the mission choose to give more. Critics describe the same dynamic as the structural exploitation of idealism.

Social life within the [[Susian Innovation Center]] FEZ is substantially organized around TU. Because coworkers constitute the default social environment and because housing, schools, and recreational facilities are TU-provided, the boundary between professional and personal life is limited. Children of FEZ residents attend TU schools and are raised within TU's institutional environment; a significant proportion enter TU employment as adults. Employees who leave TU forfeit housing, access to the zone's school system, and the social infrastructure of their daily lives. The practical costs of departure are high regardless of the formal freedom to leave.

[[Yário Kolkov]], who worked at TU from 1987 to 1989, described the culture in "[[Letters to a Dead Republic]]" as one that transforms workers into believers, and characterized the result as more dangerous than straightforward labor exploitation because the believer mistakes the condition for self-actualization. 

## Notable controversies

### Federal Cognitive Enhancement Program deaths (2040–2049)

> *Main article: [[Federal Cognitive Enhancement Program]]*

Between 2040 and 2046, TU's participation in the federal cognitive enhancement program resulted in nine participant deaths and substantial additional injuries. Families of those who died were pressured into signing non-disclosure agreements and received incomplete compensation. The program's existence and safety failures were not publicly disclosed until 2049, when three families simultaneously broke their NDAs and coordinated with journalists. The exposure triggered congressional hearings, an AFCC investigation, and a Secretariat review, and contributed directly to the conditions that produced the 2047 corruption scandal.

### Corruption scandal (2047)

> *Main article: [[Troli Ustaras Corruption Scandal (2047)]]*

TU's attempted bribery of a Secretariat of Efficiency member in 2047 resulted in automatic Yarnojte status revocation, a D$500 million fine, FEZ suspension, and criminal prosecution of company leadership. The revocation is the most significant regulatory action taken against any Yarnojte in the system's history and remains the primary reference point in Susian public and legal discourse on corporate accountability within the FEZ framework.

## In popular culture

TU is the subject of sustained public ambivalence in Susian culture. The company's products are used by nearly all Susians. Its FEZ is simultaneously aspirational, regarded as the most intellectually serious workplace in the country, and unsettling, regarded as the clearest example of what total corporate integration of daily life produces.

TU sponsors the [[TU Innovators]] [[Crolball|crolball]] team in the [[Susian National Crolball League]]. The team occupies the ambivalent position common to corporate-sponsored clubs: it draws a consistent audience from the TU employee community but lacks the civic roots of the league's most popular teams.

The [[Kolkov Learning Center]] at TU's [[Donetruže]] headquarters opened in 2009 and features seventeen quotations from [[Yário Kolkov]] displayed in large typography throughout the main hall. None of the displayed quotations include their surrounding context. Several are drawn from passages that explicitly criticize corporate culture.
