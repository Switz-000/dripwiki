**Nayotai Corporation** is a [[Susia|Susian]] multinational corporation and one of the nation's six active [[Yarnojte|Yarnojtes]]. Founded in 1909 in [[Duolij]] through the merger of three engineering firms, Nayotai is Susia's primary manufacturer of transportation vehicles and infrastructure systems and one of the largest infrastructure contractors on the [[Dripstan]] continent. The company is headquartered at the [[Postia Manufacturing Center]] in [[Postia]] and employs approximately 700,000 people worldwide. As of 2080, its market capitalization is D$2.2 trillion. Its motto is "On Time, All the Time."

Nayotai holds [[Yarnojte]] status granted in 2050 and operates two [[Free Economic Zone|Free Economic Zones]]: the [[Postia Manufacturing Center]] in [[Postia]] and the [[Sužielaj Industrial Park]] in [[Sužielaj]]. The company is publicly respected for the reliability of its engineering work and publicly criticized for the conditions under which that work is produced.

## History

### Founding and imperial era (1909–1954)

Nayotai was established in 1909 in [[Duolij]] through the consolidation of three engineering firms. During the imperial period, the company operated primarily as a contractor for the [[Dripstanian Empire]], producing military vehicles and heavy transport equipment. This relationship with state military procurement persisted through the final decades of imperial rule and established the company's orientation toward large-scale, deadline-driven production.

### Republican era (1954–2038)

Following the dissolution of the empire and the founding of [[Susia]] in 1954, Nayotai transitioned toward civilian and [[Susian Federal Government|federal government]] contracting while retaining its military vehicle manufacturing capacity. During the [[Continental Divide]] (1957–1977), the company continued supplying military transport under federal contract through the proxy conflict period and the [[Continental War]] (1975–1977).

The post-war economic boom brought sustained federal infrastructure investment, and Nayotai expanded to become the country's primary infrastructure contractor. Among its major projects from this period is the [[Arkaime Bridge]] (1993), a 47-kilometer span across the [[Sea of Galil]] connecting [[Arkaime]] to [[Duolij]], the longest bridge in the world at the time of its completion.

In the years preceding the [[The Great Transition|Great Transition]], Nayotai was among the corporations that financially supported and politically advocated for the [[Modernist Alliance]], alongside [[Troli Ustaras]]. The company sought structured corporate autonomy within the reformed constitutional framework and was granted [[Yarnojte]] status in 2050 following the transition's implementation.

### Yarnojte era (2050–present)

> *Main article: [[Yarnojte]]*

Nayotai received Yarnojte status in 2050, concurrent with [[Soites Group]]. In the same year, responding to the precedent set by the [[Troli Ustaras Corruption Scandal (2047)|TU corruption scandal of 2047]] and the subsequent empowerment of the [[Federal Authority of Commerce and Competition]], the company implemented a radical internal transparency initiative. The initiative and its consequences are described in the [[#The transparency crisis (2050–2057)|section below]].

Following the resolution of that crisis, Nayotai stabilized under new leadership and resumed growth. The primary contractor role on the [[Kolkov Line]] high-speed rail network, connecting [[Postia]] and [[Dripia]]'s major cities, was among the most significant federal contracts of the post-transition period.

## Operations

### Susia

#### Free Economic Zones

> *Main article: [[Postia Manufacturing Center]]*
> *Main article: [[Sužielaj Industrial Park]]*

Nayotai's domestic manufacturing base is concentrated in its two Free Economic Zones. The [[Postia Manufacturing Center]], located two hours east of [[New Duloc]], is the company's global headquarters and primary production facility, housing approximately 82,000 residents. The [[Sužielaj Industrial Park]], located two hours north of [[Sužielaj City]], houses approximately 33,000 residents and specializes in rail systems and precision manufacturing. Both zones integrate residential, transit, and production infrastructure under unified management.

#### Independent Susia

Outside its FEZs, Nayotai's domestic operations are concentrated in [[Postia]] and [[Sužielaj]], with a lighter project-based presence across the remaining states. The company holds recurring federal contracts for road, bridge, port, and rail construction and operates as the primary contractor on several of Susia's most significant infrastructure projects. These include the [[Arkaime Bridge]], the [[Kolkov Line]] high-speed rail network, and numerous urban transit systems across the country's major cities.

### Confia

Nayotai expanded its operations in [[Confia]] following the [[Lasman Economic Initiative]] of 1995. The company established a local subsidiary with assembly operations and has grown into one of the largest automotive manufacturers in the Confian market. Nayotai's Confian presence benefits from Confia's status as a lower-cost manufacturing environment and from the trade facilitation structures of the Lasman framework.

### Other Dripstan nations

Nayotai holds infrastructure and vehicle supply contracts across several [[ONDD]] member states, including [[Pierej]], [[Kamino]], and [[Kapiogg]]. Operations in these markets are primarily export-oriented, supplying vehicles and infrastructure materials rather than maintaining fixed local facilities.

### Incria

Nayotai maintains vehicle export and infrastructure contracting operations in several nations on the [[Incria]] continent.

## Corporate culture

### Working conditions

Nayotai's official working week is 40 hours. In practice, salaried employees routinely work between 50 and 65 hours per week, with unpaid overtime treated as an implicit condition of employment. Project deadlines are absolute; delays attributed to individual employees carry career consequences. Every minute of working time is tracked through badge systems. Lunch breaks are enforced at 30 minutes. Meetings are held standing. Email responses are expected within two hours.

Errors are logged, visible to the employee's team, and retained in performance records. An error caught in review and corrected before causing any impact is nonetheless documented, discussed in team meetings, and referenced in subsequent performance evaluations. This practice generates significant anxiety among engineering staff, for whom the fear of recorded mistakes can outweigh the practical consequence of the mistakes themselves.

The FEZs provide comprehensive employee benefits: housing assigned by role and seniority, subsidized meals, free internal transit, healthcare, and gym access. Housing is adequate and standardized. Its location is optimized for production logistics rather than resident preference. Because coworkers constitute the default social environment in both FEZs, the separation between professional and personal life is structurally limited.

Therapy utilization among Nayotai employees is approximately 62% of the workforce, the highest rate among all active Yarnojtes. Divorce rates among FEZ residents run at approximately 35%. Stress-related illness, including chronic insomnia, anxiety disorders, and gastrointestinal conditions, is common. Nayotai provides mental health services as a standard employee benefit; the services exist to sustain the workforce under conditions the company's own structure produces.

Engineer salaries range from approximately D$125,000 to D$230,000 annually. Advancement is merit-based and the promotional path is clearly defined, which contributes to retention despite the working conditions. The company's reputation for reliability is itself a professional credential: employment at Nayotai carries weight in the Susian engineering labor market.

### Cognitive enhancement

Cognitive enhancement adoption among Nayotai employees reached approximately 85% by the 2070s, the second-highest rate among the Yarnojtes after [[Troli Ustaras]]. Enhancement is not formally required as a condition of employment under the [[Cognitive Rights Act of 2061]], but the performance advantages of enhanced workers in deadline-intensive environments create sustained economic pressure toward adoption.

## The transparency crisis (2050–2057)

In 2050, concurrent with receiving Yarnojte status, Nayotai implemented what it described as a total organizational transparency initiative. The initiative was a direct response to the [[Troli Ustaras Corruption Scandal (2047)|TU corruption scandal of 2047]], which had resulted in TU's temporary loss of Yarnojte status and the significant expansion of [[Federal Authority of Commerce and Competition|AFCC]] investigative authority. Nayotai's leadership concluded that pre-emptive, comprehensive transparency would eliminate any basis for AFCC investigation.

The systems implemented included universal employee access to all company data, including salaries, performance reviews, strategic plans, project budgets, and meeting records; a Decision Traceability System requiring full documentation of every decision, including alternatives considered and outcomes tracked against predictions; and a real-time metrics dashboard ranking individual and team performance across all measurable outputs, updated continuously and visible to all employees.

The intended effect was accountability. The actual effect was organizational paralysis. With every decision subject to company-wide review, feedback, and permanent record, decision-making velocity collapsed. The time required to complete a bridge design phase increased by a factor of more than three between 2049 and 2053. On-time delivery rates fell from 94% to 34%. Revenue declined by 15% in 2053. Annual employee turnover reached 35%. Senior managers resigned rather than operate under conditions where every personnel decision was subject to public comment and indefinite documentation.

In 2056, following the resignation of the CEO, incoming leadership assessed the situation and concluded that the transparency architecture had to be substantially dismantled. A reform implemented in 2057 replaced company-wide data access with team-level transparency, limiting salary visibility, performance data, and decision records to project teams of eight to fifteen people. AI-generated recommendations were reclassified as advisory rather than authoritative. Individual performance dashboards were removed from public view.

The reform restored functional operations. On-time delivery recovered to 84% by the early 2060s. Revenue grew at approximately 8% annually from 2057 to 2065. The cultural residue of the crisis period persisted: documentation practices remain heavier than industry norms, performance anxiety within teams remains high, and the company's tolerance for operational risk remains low relative to its pre-2050 profile.

## Regulatory history

### 2075 unpaid overtime class action

In 2075, a whistleblower complaint triggered an [[Federal Authority of Commerce and Competition|AFCC]] investigation into Nayotai's overtime practices. The investigation found that salaried employees across the company's Susian operations had systematically worked between 600 and 700 hours of unpaid overtime per year. Nayotai's position was that salaried employees working on project-based schedules were not entitled to overtime compensation. The AFCC and subsequently the courts ruled that the practice constituted systematic labor exploitation. Nayotai paid a fine of D$780 million and back pay to approximately 12,000 affected employees.

The ruling did alter working conditions within the FEZs. Unpaid overtime remains less of a structural feature of Nayotai employment.

## In popular culture

The Susian saying "Built like Nayotai" is used to describe something reliable, durable, and precisely constructed. It is among the more common corporate references in everyday Susian speech and reflects the company's association with infrastructure that functions without visible effort.

[[Yário Kolkov]], who worked at Nayotai from 1982 to 1987 before moving to [[Troli Ustaras]], wrote about the experience in his 1990 work [[Letters to a Dead Republic]]. His characterization of the company's labor model as "efficiency is tyranny with a stopwatch" became one of the more cited passages in Susian political philosophy, though Kolkov himself drew a distinction between Nayotai's explicit instrumentalization of workers and what he considered the more insidious dynamic at TU.

Nayotai sponsors the [[Nayotai Motors (Crolball team)|Nayotai Motors]] [[Crolball|crolball]] team, one of few corporate-sponsored teams in the [[Susian National Crolball League | Susian National League]].
