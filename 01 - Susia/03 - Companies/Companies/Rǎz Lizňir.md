**Rǎz Lizňir Corporation** is a [[Susia|Susian]] Public Benefit Corporation headquartered in [[Velúria]], [[Orlítia]], founded in 2073. The company operates in the post-mortem organ procurement market, purchasing the rights to a living client's organs in exchange for an upfront cash payment, then harvesting and auctioning those organs upon the client's natural death. Rǎz Lizňir was founded one year after the [[Bodily Autonomy Act of 2072]], which legalized the regulated for-profit organ market in [[Susia]], and is widely credited with resolving the organ supply crisis that preceded the act. With a market capitalization of D$73.2 billion as of 2080, it is the dominant player in the sector.

## Business Model

### The Contract

Rǎz Lizňir's core product is a pre-purchase organ contract. A living client signs over the rights to their organs in exchange for an immediate lump-sum payment. The amount offered is calculated actuarially based on the client's age, health, and projected organ viability at time of death:

- Healthy individual aged 25: FD$180,000 (full package)
- Healthy individual aged 45: FD$120,000
- Individual aged 65: FD$60,000
- High-risk or unhealthy individuals: FD$20,000 to FD$40,000

Individual organ sales are also available on an à la carte basis for clients who wish to sell rights to specific organs rather than the full package.

In exchange, the client agrees to annual health check-ups, maintenance of a baseline health standard, and the implantation of a tracking chip to allow the company to locate the body promptly upon death. Contracts must be co-signed by a guarantor, preferably a family member. If the client dies under circumstances that constitute a breach of contract, outstanding debts pass to the guarantor.

### The Supply Chain

Rǎz Lizňir does not hold contracts on its own balance sheet beyond the short term. Upon signing, the company sells the contract to third parties, primarily investment banks such as [[Red Iron]] and specialist debt collection firms. These intermediaries bundle contracts into **Organ-Based Securities (OBS)**, which are then sold to pension funds and insurance companies. The securities have attracted significant institutional interest, with [[Red Iron]] traders noting annual returns of 15 to 20 percent and low default rates given that the underlying asset, the organ itself, is biologically guaranteed to materialize eventually.

Upon a client's death, organs are harvested and sold at auction to the highest bidder, with proceeds distributed according to the contract terms and the security structure.

## Clients

Rǎz Lizňir's client base is concentrated among populations facing acute financial pressure or seeking large upfront cash settlements. Primary client groups include:

- Indebted gamblers
- Former [[Soites Experimenter Program|Soites Experimenters]] in financial decline
- Retired [[Poceiros|poceiros]] managing long-term debt or healthcare costs
- Debt refugees sheltering in [[Velúria]]
- Individuals in acute moral or financial distress seeking a definitive resolution

The company's [[Velúria]] headquarters is not coincidental. The city's concentration of people seeking fresh starts, combined with its legal environment and the anonymity afforded by the [[Velúrian identity|Velúria ID system]], makes it the primary recruitment ground for new contracts.

## Relationship with Lischev-Verene

Rǎz Lizňir has a publicly adversarial but privately cooperative relationship with [[Lischev-Verene]]. [[Lischev-Verene|LV]] has formally denounced Rǎz Lizňir's activities as incompatible with its corporate philosophy of biological preservation and human dignity, and the two companies compete philosophically on the question of what the body is owed. LV's tissue regeneration services have also suffered reduced demand as Rǎz Lizňir's organ supply has expanded and driven down market prices.

Despite this, the two companies cooperate occasionally on research, particularly in areas of organ viability, preservation techniques, and post-mortem biological assessment. The commercial incentive to improve organ quality aligns their technical interests even where their ethics diverge.

## Public Perception

Rǎz Lizňir occupies a uniquely uncomfortable position in Susian public life. The company is legal, regulated, and by most measures fulfills a genuine social function: the [[Bodily Autonomy Act of 2072]] created a market, and Rǎz Lizňir organized it. The organ crisis that preceded the act was real, and the company's supply chain has materially reduced transplant waiting times across [[Susia]].

At the same time, the company's client profile, primarily the desperate and the indebted, and its marketing tone have generated persistent criticism. Advocacy groups argue that the company systematically targets populations with diminished capacity for free choice, effectively monetizing desperation. The guarantor clause, which transfers debt liability to family members upon a breach of contract, has attracted particular scrutiny.

The company's PBC status is frequently cited in its own defense: unlike a standard corporation, Rǎz Lizňir is legally obligated to weigh social benefit alongside profit. Critics argue the designation functions more as reputational cover than meaningful constraint.
