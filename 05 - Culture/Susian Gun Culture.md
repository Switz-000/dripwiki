History:

- [[Susia|Susian]] attitudes on gun ownership date back to the early settlers, and also arise from traditions of hunting, militias, and frontier living.
    
- Guns became a symbol of independence, self-preservation and property. Susian settlers created an early form of shooting clubs called “[[Sorzenkos]]” whose purpose was to provide firearms training to the community.
    
- Many Sorzenkos also had militias associated with it, primarily for the defense of the community against native attacks, but also for recreational shooting and “[[Hăjaven]]”, a festival where militias do a communal hunt and share the game with the townsfolk.
    
- During the [[eastwards expansion]] into the now states of [[Misocévia]], [[Sužielaj]] and [[Troli]], settling and exploring was done by frontiersmen called [[Cericeiro|Cericeiros]], skilled horsemen known for being brave and unruly. These expanded the practice of Sorzenkos to the east and popularized gun culture, being constantly referenced in susian literature.
    
- [[Jartes II]] disarmed the people before enacting tyrannical legislation, susians organized militias and resisted tyrannical rule. The susian constitution now guarantees the [[Right to rebel|right to rebel]], the citizens may take up arms and overthrow the government in case if the natural rights explicitly recognized by the constitution (freedom of speech, right to self-defense and bear arms, right to own and use property, right to life, etc.) are systematically infringed upon.
    
- It is commonly believed by [[Susia|susians]] that the liberal revolts failed due to Jartes II’s disarmament campaigns and crackedowns of Sorzenkos, much of this is attributed to one of [[Armadesh Versij|Versij's]] quotes: 
	- “You will have me die for treason. So be it. Yet know this: had my fellows not been disarmed you would not now stand to pronounce it” though it is disputed by historians if the revolution would be able to succeed even without the disarmament."

Philosophical Roots:

- To susians, owning a firearm is a civic duty, this concept was first presented in Versij's architecture of freedom as the "citizen guards of the republic”
    
- Owning a firearm does not only defend you, but your family, property, community, rights and country.
    
- Rights that you're not able to defend are just privileges waiting to be revoked.
    
- You owe a competence to your family, neighbors and to your country. That's why it's taught in civics class. “Here's why you have a duty to be capable of violence on behalf of the republic”
    
- "A nation of citizen soldiers is mightier than any standing army” funnily enough, this famous quote is actually from Satratonie but often attributed to [[Armadesh Versij|Versij]]
    

  

Day to Day Impact:

- Within civics class, gun safety is a required part of the curriculum in all middle and high schools. Susians in civic class learn about how the susian system works and why, they read and learn about Versij and [[Yário Kolkov|Kolkov]] and learn to debate. They also do projects like the state annual fair, where students research about other states and have to show off their culture, food etc.
    
- Open carry is more common in the eastern states, while western states prefer concealed carry, both are constitutionally federally protected
    
- Owning a gun is considered attractive in many parts of [[Susia]] and going shooting is a surprisingly common date idea
    
- Though owning guns or not is completely normal in urban areas, urbanites tend to be less open about them, since they consider it a more personal/political topic, most gun owners in urban Susia keep their weapons at home.
    
- The ownership of guns is more frowned upon in [[Free Economic Zone|FEZs]] compared to independent Susia, it can be seen as “odd” or even “primitive”. [[Troli Ustaras|TU]] and [[Atompron]] encourage instead a more "sanitized" version of the tradition, offering virtual shooting as a safer alternative.
    
- FEZs and states may create “gun free zones” in specific buildings like schools or government buildings but they cannot prohibit it entirely.
    
- Susia has had mandatory military service since the late imperial times, and was reinstituted during the start of the [[Continental Divide]]. Both sexes are required to serve for one year in combat roles or may choose to serve two in non combatant roles. Susians from all different backgrounds and locations are forced to eat, sleep and depend on each other, learning skills like firearm competence, discipline, organization, physical fitness and disaster response. This makes the right to rebel credible, since organized militia capacity does exist. After service, susians also have an option to join the [[National Guard]] of their state, where they act as reserves who can be called by the state (in case of natural disasters, or public disorder) or by the federal government in foreign missions.
    
- [[Sorzenkos]] still exist and are popular to this day, especially in smaller towns and the countryside and in the eastern states. [[Hăjaven]] is celebrated nationally and is a federal public holiday. Families normally make a special dinner and eat with their neighbors or family. In rural areas it is more traditional, with a communal hunting taking place and a communal dinner in the town.
    
- In Susia, Sorzenkos are civic institutions that create cross class relationships and mutual obligations (you're expected to defend your shooting partner).
    
- [[Tekur|Tekurs]] have higher weapon ownership, higher enlistment rates and Sorzenko affiliations in [[Susia]]
