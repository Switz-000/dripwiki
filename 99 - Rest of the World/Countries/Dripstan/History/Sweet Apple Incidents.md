The **Sweet Apple Incidents** were a sustained chemical warfare campaign conducted by [[Kapiogg]] against [[Confia|Confian]] forces on the northern front of the [[Continental War]] between 1976 and 1977. Kapioggi artillery and aircraft delivered persistent vesicant agent against Confian positions and against settlements in the contested provinces of [[Oesburg]] and [[Jaeregg]] across two winters. A joint commission established in 1978 recorded 9,200 deaths.

## Background

Kapiogg entered the war in 1975 as the northern member of the [[ODDN]] alliance, holding a static front against the Confian army while [[Susia]] conducted mobile operations in the south. The northern front did not move. Kapioggi forces had been supplied and modernized under Susian programmes during the [[Continental Divide]] period and were sustained in the field primarily by Susian air support, but they possessed neither the mechanized strength to break the Confian line nor the depth to absorb a Confian breakthrough. The provinces behind the front had been partitioned from [[Drippalia]] following the [[Second War of the Forest]] and held mixed [[Tekur]] populations on both sides of the line.

Susian modernization aid to Kapiogg included agricultural programmes. Among these was the construction of chemical works in the western provinces to supply orchard pesticide to the region's fruit growers, one of which produced a product line designated Eplevakt.

Kapiogg's decision to weaponize the facility is not documented. No authorizing order has been recovered, no Kapioggi officer was named in the 1978 proceedings, and the point at which the plant's output changed is known only from its own production records. Weaponized fill left the works from some point in 1976.

## The agent

> *Main article: [[Eplevakt]]*

The fill was a sulfur mustard agent dissolved in chlorobenzene, a vesicant that injures skin, eyes and airways after a latency of roughly one to two days. It was issued in two seasonal formulations under the plant's existing product numbering. The winter fill was solvent-dominant, which kept it liquid in cold weather and caused it to volatilize readily; the summer fill was agent-dominant and persisted in soil and on material for weeks. The plant's records show the ratio drifting within each designation according to feedstock supply. No specification was ever issued.

The chlorobenzene component carries a sweet, aromatic odour. Confian and Drippalian troops named the agent for that smell, and the name entered general use in both armies during the winter of 1976.

## Employment

Delivery was by artillery and by aircraft across a front sector rather than against discrete targets. Because the solvent-cut fill was less persistent per round than comparable agents, effective ground denial required sustained expenditure, and the campaign took the form of repeated saturation of the same sectors over two years rather than a single concentrated attack.

The two fills produced different casualty patterns. Winter deliveries volatilized quickly and pooled as vapour in trenches, dugouts and cellars, producing mass respiratory and ocular casualties in enclosed positions. Summer deliveries contaminated ground, equipment and buildings, producing skin injuries over a longer period and among people who had not been present during any attack. Medical personnel treating casualties and civilians handling contaminated clothing and stores were injured in both patterns. Agent persisting in cold soil returned to vapour with rising spring temperatures.

Civilians in Oesburg and Jaeregg were not targeted separately from military positions. They were present in and around the sectors under fire throughout.

## Contamination of Kapioggi forces

Kapioggi infantry sustained casualties on ground contaminated by their own artillery. The solvent evaporated within hours while the agent remained in soil and structures for weeks, so a position ceased to smell of anything long before it ceased to be dangerous. Troops on both sides drew the same conclusion from this, that the sweet odour indicated the presence of gas and its absence indicated safety, and advanced across quiet ground accordingly. Kapioggi units were not issued guidance correcting the inference.

## Susian response

President [[Čalsor Boňár]] issued a formal condemnation of the use of chemical weapons by an ODDN member in 1976. Susia took no further measure. Susian air support to the northern front continued without interruption for the remainder of the war, and Susian aircrew operating over contaminated sectors were among those injured. The northern front was the condition on which the southern campaign depended, and its collapse would have ended the war on Confian terms.

Susian intelligence did not identify the programme while it was running. Collection on the facility existed but sat in economic and trade files, since the plant reported its output under an agricultural product designation that was never amended. The material was correctly filed and was not read against the war.

## Casualties

The commission recorded 9,200 deaths: approximately 5,800 Confian soldiers and allied personnel, 2,100 Tekur civilians, and 1,300 Kapioggi soldiers.

The figure is a reconstruction from ammunition expenditure records, delivery grid references and unit strength returns, cross-checked between Kapioggi and Confian archives. It is not a count of bodies and does not rest on medical records, because the variable fill made exposure impossible to model. It excludes deaths from delayed effects, and it excludes civilian populations for which no unit returns existed. Both governments accepted it, and it is the figure used in the subsequent convention, in sentencing, and in the historiography, on the grounds that it is the only figure both archives can produce.

## Aftermath

The joint commission of 1978 established the production history from the plant's own ledgers. In the course of that work it recorded that Eplevakt had been an orchard pesticide line before it was a weapons fill, and that the name under which Confian and Drippalian troops had known the agent, taken from its smell, matched the product it had been manufactured as. Neither name referred to the compound responsible for the injuries.

Susia negotiated a continental convention prohibiting the production, stockpiling and use of chemical weapons in the post-war period. Susian internal documents from the period address proliferation within the ODDN bloc rather than the conduct of the campaign. Kapioggi disarmament was concluded in the same negotiation as the constitutional settlement that produced the parliamentary monarchy of 1980.
