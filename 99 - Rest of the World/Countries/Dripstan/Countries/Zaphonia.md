### Brief History

Zaphonia Before Dripstan:

- The zaphonians are native to the continent and have their origins traced back to a tribe who inhabited the plains of modern [[Troli]], who, because of a drought, immigrated southeast and crossed the desert to settle near the [[Khetre river|Khetre]], creating [[Tahuni]]; they also found six other tribes.
    
- [[King Mawesi]] the great, unified both sides of the Khetre, crowning himself emperor of Zaphonia
    
- [[Zaphonian-Bakaruian wars|Zaphonians and the Bakurians fought each other many times]], especially for the region of [[Huttivia]], where the mouth of delta of the Khetre river is.
    
- After the arrival of the [[Armotism|Armotist]] and settlement eastwards, [[Cericeiros]] eventually found [[Tahuni]], describing it as the most advanced native civilization they had found.
    
- The Emperor of Zaphonia agreed to become a vassal of the Dripstanian Empire, marrying princess [[Empress Prazde|Prazde]]. After his death, Prazde became queen of Zaphonia.
    

Zaphonia During the Dripstanian Empire:

- Zaphonia adopted armotism, dripstanian institutions and customs, successfully integrating itself in the empire. Zaphonians intermixed with the Dripstanians and as a result, look similar, with the only notable differences being their more tanned skin and distinct facial features.
    
- Shortly after the Tahuni Accords, Zaphonia declared its independence from Confia creating the First Zaphonian Republic. 
    

First Zaphonian Republic and Union of Zaphonian Syndicates:

- The First Zaphonian Republic was very unstable, being it a non elected provisional government, being divided by two rival factions:
    

- [[Sons of Libert (Zaphonia)|YP]] (Sons of liberty) - Pro [[Susia]] liberals, defending opening economic and diplomatic ties with susian companies. They secured trade agreements with Susia, including a joint refinery project with the Susian giant [[POCOIL]].
    
- [[Sindicates of the Oil Workers (Zaphonia)|SKT]] (Sindicates of the Oil Workers) - sindicalists who wanted the nationalization of the oil industry, a more centralized economy and land reform. They brought in major infrastructure investments and subsidized loans from Confia’s syndicalist government, along with expanded labor rights and business regulations.
    

- President [[Mikheli Kinpaum]] spent the entire period trying to balance these competing demands, resulting in constant cabinet reshuffles and ongoing factional conflict. The era was also marked by frequent political assassinations.
    
- The Union of Zaphonian Syndicates was the pro-Confia syndicalist government that ruled the country from the Oil Workers’ Petition from 1960, when the SKT took power, until the Susian invasion during the [[Continental War]], which ended the regime and replaced it with the Democratic Republic of Zaphonia in year 1977 after the [[Treaty of Karlotopol’]].
    

Second Zaphonian Republic:

- After the syndicalist regime fell, a referendum decided on the new republican model. A parliamentary system modeled on [[Pajanpar]]’s Rose Republic was chosen
    
- [[Pheroto Kuthelo]], a former colonel, won the first election. His program mixed civic nationalism and economic nationalism:
    

- Re-nationalized all recently privatized oil platforms without compensation, creating the state owned Zaphonian Oil Company. Confian investors retaliated by pressuring their government to cut ties with Zaphonia.
    
- Revived territorial claims over [[Huttivia]], threatening to block the river that powers the [[Jertele-Glann Dam]] (75% of local energy). [[Kamino]] expelled Zaphonian diplomats and imposed a petroleum embargo.
    
- After being criticized by Susia’s president [[Filňar Čantij]], Kuthelo escalated and ordered the kidnapping of Susian diplomats inside their own embassy, demanding an apology.
    
- The parliament immediately passed a vote of no confidence, removing him from office before the crisis spiraled further. New elections were called.
    

- After the election, leader [[Mawesi Photeko]], an economist influenced by the [[Tahuni School of Economics|Tahuni School]], became prime minister. He planned to Modernize and industrialize Zaphonia, launch import-substitution programs and restore friendly relations with Confia, which Tahuni economists saw as Zaphonia’s natural commercial partner.
    
- Photeko signed the [[New Oil Agreement]] with Confia to repair relations after Kuthelo’s seizures of Confian-owned platforms. Under the deal:
    

- Zaphonian crude would be refined by [[Końteraf]], Confia’s state oil company, ensuring domestic gasoline supply and supporting Zaphonia’s emerging automotive industry.
    
- The agreement, however, was heavily one-sided in Confia’s favor and led to an economic crisis in Zaphonia.
    

### Other facts

- 90% of the Zaphonian territory is deemed uninhabitable, as it is a scorching desert. Only a narrow strip of fertile land alongside the Khetre river sustains the population and gives life to the region. The source of the Khetre is in the mountain chain in the south. This mountain chain also determines the border with the replidian countries.
    
- In 1984, during the [[Boris Serec|Serec]] government in Confia and the third year of Mawesi Photeko’s administration in Zaphonia, both countries met to resolve the issue of Confian-owned oil platforms that had been nationalized by Kuthelo. Desperate to restore relations at any cost, while keeping his developmentalist agenda alive, Photeko signed a highly unfavorable agreement. Its main clauses:
- 43 out of 45 platforms were handed over to the Confian state company. The [[Zaphonian Oil Company]] collapsed and went bankrupt the same year. Exports collapsed (oil was 76% of all Zaphonian exports).
- Mandatory annual purchase of 100 million barrels of gasoline, Zaphonia had to buy the fuel at least 20% above the global price. Presented in state propaganda as a victory that would “save” the automotive industry. In reality, the trade balance plummeted from +1.4% of GDP (1982) to –25.3% (1985), a massive snowballing debt to Confia was created.
- Confia must reinvest 10% of Końteraf profits from Zaphonian oil in projects in Zaphonia. These projects were riddled with corruption, stalled works, and overpricing. In 1989, during a default, Zaphonia halted its own infrastructure spending and requested Confia to maintain its through this clause, leading to a nationwide decay of the road system.
    

- Końteraf becomes a protected “national champion” inside Zaphonia.
    

- Full tax exemption; any deficit or debt covered by the Zaphonian treasury, D$ 100 million subsidy per platform, and a 75% tax on competing suppliers made Końteraf a total monopoly. Fuel prices in Zaphonia surged.
    

- The treaty lasts 20 years.
    
- Outcomes for Zaphonia:
    

- Debt reached 336 billion drips within eight years, 87.3% debt/GDP ratio.
    
- Oil rights were lost; the main export sector vanished.
    
- Confian skilled workers replaced locals on the platforms.
    
- 1.13 million Zaphonian were thrown into unemployment.
    
- Fuel in Zaphonia cost 25 to 50% more than the same gasoline sold domestically in Confia.
    

- Outcomes for Confia:
    

- Końteraf’s profits Surged
    
- Expansion of Confian social and economic programs
    
- Deficit reversed
    
- Confia strengthened its position against Susia and POCOIL in global oil markets.
    
- Secured new export contracts with countries like [[Pierej]] and [[Pajanpar]].
