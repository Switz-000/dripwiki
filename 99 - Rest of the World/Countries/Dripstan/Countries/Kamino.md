### Brief History

Kamino before Dripstan:

- The main economic and social institution in Kaminoan society was the glysan, small, traditional, decentralized family-based subsistence units. Each [[Glysan]] was led by a hereditary patriarch responsible for preserving the family’s faith. Their structure varied, but typically consisted of several households of nuclear families.
    
- [[The Bakurian Campaign]] was one of the four major conquests of [[Jartes I]], starting in 1826. The [[Kingdom of Bakuro]], ruled by [[King Rejnai I]] of the young [[Carryn dynasty]], was attacked without warning in a brutal invasion full of plunder, massacres, and village burnings. Jartes I remains widely hated in Kamino. Rejnai was forced to surrender to stop the destruction terrorizing his population.

During the [[Confian Secession War|Secession War]]:

- After the Capture of the Fort in Imgospalje in 1936, the Dripstanian [[Emperor Soliman]] agreed to grant independence to the Kamino garrison on the condition that they fight the Confianist rebels. Even so, the combined armies of [[Confia]] and [[Zaphonia]] launched a northern campaign through [[Cadack]] and forced Kamino’s capitulation before [[Hutivia]] fell. From 1944 to 1950, Kamino briefly existed as the [[Free Bakurian Republic]], joining the [[Free Confian Republic]] under the [[Treaty of Hutivia]]. During this period, 50,000 Bakuro soldiers were sent to fight on the Susian fronts in support of the rebels.
    

Algyn’s Kamino:

- When Marshal [[Perrilon Algyn]] declared independence in 1953, he confronted the country’s structural weaknesses. Kamino was overwhelmingly agrarian, with 83.7% of its workforce in the primary sector. The economy was stagnant after the collapse of colonial institutions and aristocratic hierarchies triggered by the Confian Secession War. Traditional glysans began re-forming and dominating society again. Although Algyn was formally head of state, real power lay with clan leaders, limiting the government’s ability to enforce laws or import machinery for agricultural modernization.
    
- Days after independence, Algyn launched the [[March to Cadack]], a political-military campaign to centralize administrative power in his government. This campaign marked the start of his dictatorial phase. The Kaminoan army, accompanied by the newly created [[Civil Police (Kamino)|Civil Police]], swept the countryside, taking control of villages from clan leaders and issuing land titles to family patriarchs within the glysans, establishing private property for the first time. The March effectively abolished the glysans.
    
- The campaign became notorious for harsh and often violent methods. Soldiers were accused of looting, assaults, and rape. When elderly patriarchs refused to surrender their land, soldiers frequently killed them, claimed the land titles, and expelled or hired the resident families. The press demanded an audit of the “de-glysanization,” but the government censored all coverage.
    
- During the March, more than 300,000 electric poles were installed and 1,570 police stations established across Kamino. The campaign produced a new agrarian elite, low-ranking military officers who seized glysan lands and used them to develop agricultural technologies, integrate Kamino into global value chains, and build an export-oriented agro-economy.
